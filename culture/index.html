The Culture Hub

Everything in one place

Cultural institutions sit on tax lots. They have zoning, an owner of record, a land use code, and a community district, same as every other property in the city. Reading them that way says something the listings never do.

This is the start of it. There is more coming. Let me know what is missing. —Mike Racioppo

The longer versionRead ▼

456 places across all five boroughs: 208 library branches across the three public systems, 131 museums, 62 movie theaters, 41 Broadway theatres, and the performing arts venues and parks. 143 of them carry their full lot record, pulled by BBL from MapPLUTO after the address was resolved through the city's Geoclient.

That record is where it gets interesting. BAM sits on a city owned lot whose owner of record is the Parks Department, with BAM Rose Cinemas on the same lot. Brooklyn Bridge Park is zoned M2-1. The Met sits on Central Park's tax lot. Zoning and current use disagree constantly, and that is normal rather than an error.

CB6 has eight: the Old Stone House, the Waterfront Museum on the barge at Conover Street, Nitehawk on Bartel-Pritchard Square, Cobble Hill Cinemas on Court Street, the Park Slope and Pacific branches, and the pop-up libraries in Carroll Gardens and Red Hook.
