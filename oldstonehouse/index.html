In brief

The Old Stone House and Washington Park does the kind of work a neighborhood only notices when it stops. It preserves and teaches local and national history where that history actually happened, and it is the conservancy for JJ Byrne Playground and Washington Park, which means it cares for the park, the playground and the gardens that thousands of people use without ever thinking about who keeps them working. It runs family programs, arts and cultural events, and shows up on neighborhood issues. A historic house that could have settled for being a museum instead became one of the more useful civic institutions in this district.

Much of that is down to Executive Director Kim Maier, who has built it into a place that serves the present as seriously as it interprets the past.

The building is a reconstruction of the 1699 Vechte-Cortelyou House, on land taken from the Lenape as early as 1639. It stands in Washington Park on the border of Park Slope and Gowanus, marking where the original Dutch farmstead stood and where the culminating engagement of the 1776 Battle of Brooklyn took place. It is also the birthplace of the Brooklyn Dodgers. It is a Historic House Trust of New York City site and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

What it runs

An interactive exhibit on the largest battle of the Revolutionary War and what living in Brooklyn was like from 1776 to 1783. Contemporary shows by emerging artists. Gardens of native and useful plants. School visits and field trips. Concerts, readings, lectures and theater, including Piper Theatre. A summer camp. And the playground, courts, turf field and dog run that make up JJ Byrne.
