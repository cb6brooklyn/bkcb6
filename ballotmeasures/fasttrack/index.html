Affordable Housing Fast Track

2025 Question 2, taking effect January 1, 2027, in the 12 community districts that built the least affordable housing.

In November 2025, voters approved Question 2, . One of the things it created is the Affordable Housing Fast Track. Starting January 1, 2027, affordable housing projects in the 12 community districts that have built the least affordable housing for their size get a shorter public review.

Community boards keep the same 60-day review they have under ULURP. Tap any district for its numbers and its target in the Fair Housing Growth Strategy.

How the Fast Track works

A project qualifies when its main purpose is more housing, including affordable housing, and it has Mandatory Inclusionary Housing levels of affordability or higher. That generally means 20 to 30% of the residential floor area is permanently affordable.

Review takes 90 days. The community board has 60 days, at the same time as the Borough President. Then the City Planning Commission reviews the project and takes the final vote, in 30 days, or 45 for a project that needs more environmental review. After that, a project follows the existing process for affordable housing in an MIH area.

The Fast Track does not change environmental review or landmark protections.

How the 12 districts were picked

The City Planning Commission set the method by rule in April 2026. It does not rank districts by how many affordable homes they built. It divides the affordable homes built in each district by the homes already there, so large and small districts can be compared.

This first cycle counts affordable housing from July 1, 2021 to June 30, 2026. The City published the 12 on October 1, 2026, and a new list comes every five years.

The other new paths from the 2025 Charter changes

HPD lists four faster public reviews for affordable housing that voters adopted in November 2025:

  • Affordable Housing Fast Track (Charter §197-f): this page.
  • BSA fast track (§666-a): HPD-supported affordable housing on land already zoned for housing can ask the Board of Standards and Appeals to waive bulk, use or parking rules. 60 days of community board and Borough President review, then a BSA hearing and decision.
  • Public land (§197-e(k)): buying, leasing or selling public land for affordable housing. 60 days of community board and Borough President review, then 30 days at the City Council, which decides.
  • Modest zoning changes (§197-e(b)(2)): for example, up to 30% more floor area in a medium-density district. 60 days of community board and Borough President review, then 30 days at the City Planning Commission.

HPD says these replace a process that otherwise takes about seven months.

Timeline
  • November 4, 2025: voters approve the Charter amendments.
  • April 2026: the City Planning Commission adopts the rule for picking the 12 districts.
  • October 1, 2026: the City publishes the 12 Fast Track districts.
  • December 3, 2026: DCP holds a virtual information session (RSVP).
  • January 1, 2027: the Fast Track takes effect.
