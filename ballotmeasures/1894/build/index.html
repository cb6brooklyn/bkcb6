Build your own New York City

In 1894 voters in New York, Kings, Queens and Richmond counties and four Westchester places decided whether to join one big city. Mount Vernon said no, half of Queens County stayed out and became Nassau County, and parts of East Chester and Pelham stayed in Westchester. Here you draw the line yourself with the places that were on that ballot: put any piece in or take any piece out, and see how many people your city would have had at every census from 1900 to 2020, and every year since through the latest estimate, 2025.

What these numbers are

Population is the U.S. Census count taken every ten years, from 1900 to 2020, for each place as it is drawn today. 2021 to 2025 are the Census Bureau's yearly population estimates for July 1 of each year (the latest set, released in 2026); they are estimates built from births, deaths and moves since the 2020 count, not a count. Land area leaves out water. People per square mile is population divided by land area. Homes is the count of housing units in the 2020 census. Growth compares the 2025 estimate with 1900.

Why it starts in 1900. 1900 is the first census after Greater New York took effect on January 1, 1898, and after Nassau County was created out of Queens County in 1899. Before that the lines were different: the Bronx was split between New York County and Westchester County, and Nassau was part of Queens. Starting in 1900 keeps every piece the same place in every year.

What is not here. The Census Bureau's tables of age, race, income and the like require a key to download and are not broken out the same way back to 1900, so this page uses the counts that exist for every piece in every census: people, and in 2020, homes.

How the 1894 places line up with the pieces here. New York County in 1894 was Manhattan and the West Bronx; the Town of Westchester became the East Bronx in 1895, along with parts of East Chester and Pelham. Census counts for those Bronx parts do not exist separately back to 1900, so the Bronx is one piece. Queens County in 1894 is two pieces: the western towns, now the Borough of Queens, and the eastern towns, now Nassau County. Eastchester and Pelham here are the parts of those towns that stayed in Westchester, villages included. Mount Vernon is the city as it is today. Yonkers, New Rochelle and the rest of Westchester, and Suffolk County, were not on the 1894 ballot and are not here.
