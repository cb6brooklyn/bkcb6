Nothing here is calculated. Every tile opens the citywide address search, which reads zoning and land use from PLUTO and districts from Geoclient. Each address was run through that search one at a time and the card’s own answer recorded.
Museums are not zoned as museums. There is no cultural zoning district. The Guggenheim, the Frick and the Cooper Hewitt all sit in R10, the same residential district as the apartment buildings around them. The Museum at FIT is C6-2, ordinary Midtown commercial.
Six sit on joint interest land. The Metropolitan Museum is in Central Park, the Brooklyn Museum and Lefferts Historic House in Prospect Park, the Queens Museum in Flushing Meadows, Bartow-Pell in Pelham Bay. Those are areas no single community board covers, so the card names the joint interest area instead of a board.
Six addresses were corrected. The culture file lists some museums by intersection or place name, which a geocoder cannot resolve. Where a street address was found, it was accepted only if it geocoded within 250 metres of the museum’s known location. That check matters: a plausible-looking address for one museum resolved two kilometres from the building and was rejected.
