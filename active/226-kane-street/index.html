What's here now
Kane Street Synagogue (Congregation Baith Israel Anshei Emes), a Conservative congregation founded in 1856 and known as the Mother Synagogue of Brooklyn, the oldest Jewish congregation still serving the neighborhood where it began. The sanctuary and meeting building went up in 1855 as a Dutch Reformed church; the congregation moved here in 1905, when the block was still Harrison Street. Aaron Copland had his bar mitzvah here in 1913 under Rabbi Israel Goldfarb, who led the congregation for sixty years and composed the Shalom Aleichem melody sung in most Ashkenazi synagogues. The Neo-Gothic sanctuary was restored in the 1970s, and the Sol and Lillian Goldman Educational Center (the school building this application concerns) followed a renewal campaign in the early 2000s. Today the site houses weekly services, the Kane Street Kids preschool, a Hebrew school, and community programs, and the congregation is midway through the Mikdash capital campaign to renew the sanctuary and shared spaces.
