Bins and setout: your questions, DSNY's answers

31 questions, each answered in DSNY's words with a link to the page it comes from

Every answer below follows what the Department of Sanitation says on its own pages, and links to the page it comes from.

Which bin does my building need?

What kind of trash bin does my building have to use?

It depends on how many homes are in the building:

  • 1 to 9 homes: official NYC Bins for trash.
  • 10 to 30 homes: Empire Bins or official NYC Bins, where Empire Bins are offered.
  • 31 or more homes: Empire Bins, once they come to the district.

City agencies, nonprofits, houses of worship and professional offices in residential buildings that get DSNY pickup also have to use NYC Bins.

Source: DSNY, Empire Bins FAQ · DSNY, NYC Bin FAQ

When did this start?

Bins with secure lids, 55 gallons or less, have been required for trash at buildings with 1 to 9 homes since November 12, 2024. Since June 1, 2026, those bins have to be official NYC Bins. A warning period ran through September 7, and full enforcement began Tuesday, September 8, 2026.

Source: DSNY, Collection Laws for Residents · DSNY, Residential Waste Containerization

Can I keep using my own trash cans?

Not for trash, if your building has 1 to 9 homes. Only official NYC Bins count for trash now. You can still use your own bins for recycling and compost.

Source: DSNY, Empire Bins FAQ · DSNY, NYC Bin FAQ

Does my recycling or compost need an NYC Bin?

No. NYC Bins are sold for trash, recycling and compost, but they are only required for trash. Recycling can go in a labeled bin or clear plastic bags. Compost has to go in a labeled bin, 55 gallons or less, with a secure lid.

Source: DSNY, Empire Bins FAQ · DSNY, NYC Bin FAQ

My building has 10 or more homes. Do we need bins?

Not yet. Buildings with 10 or more homes can still set trash out in a bin of 55 gallons or less with a secure lid after 6 PM, or in bags on the curb after 8 PM. Empire Bins are in Manhattan Community District 9, start in Brooklyn Community District 2 this fall, and are planned citywide by 2032. DSNY lists the next districts as Bronx 2 and 5, Queens 2, Manhattan 2, Brooklyn 8 and Staten Island 1. CB6 is not on that list yet.

Source: DSNY, Trash · DSNY, Empire Bins FAQ

NYC Bins

Where do I buy one?

At NYC Home Depot stores, for in-store pickup at homedepot.com/NYCBins, or for delivery through DoorDash, Instacart or Uber Eats.

Source: DSNY, NYC Bin FAQ

What sizes are there?
  • 45 gallons: for buildings with several homes, holds up to 4 kitchen trash bags.
  • 35 gallons: for small single-family homes, up to 3 kitchen bags.
  • 25 gallons: for tight spaces or little trash, up to 2 kitchen bags.
  • 21-gallon compost bin: up to 7 three-gallon compost bags.
  • 13-gallon compost bin: up to 4 three-gallon compost bags.

Source: DSNY, NYC Bin FAQ

How many do I need, and who buys them?

The property owner or building manager buys them, and has to have enough bins to hold all the building's trash with the lids closed. DSNY gives the same answer for one-time extra trash, like a party or a cleanout: have enough bins.

Source: DSNY, NYC Bin FAQ · DSNY, Collection Laws for Residents

Do the bins need a bag inside?

No, but a liner keeps the bin clean. For compost, line it only with clear plastic, paper or compostable bags.

Source: DSNY, NYC Bin FAQ

What is the fine?

$50 for the first offense, $100 for the second and $200 for the third and after, for not using a bin of 55 gallons or less with a secure lid. Fines go up with each violation in a 12-month period.

Source: DSNY, NYC Bin FAQ · DSNY, Collection Laws for Residents

Where do I keep my bins?

Inside, or in an alley, courtyard or other spot off the public sidewalk. If there is no room, within three feet of your building, kept neat so people can walk by. Sidewalk enclosures fall under Department of Transportation rules.

Source: DSNY, Trash

How do I get rid of an old bin?

Bins that are mostly plastic or metal are recyclable. Put them out upside down on recycling day with a note saying they go with recycling. Other bins go out with trash.

Source: DSNY, Recycling for Residents

Setting out trash, recycling and compost

What time can I put things out?
  • Bins with secure lids (trash, recycling or compost): after 6 PM.
  • Bags directly on the curb (buildings with 10 or more homes, bagged recycling, extra yard waste): after 8 PM.
  • Everything should be out by midnight.

Setting out at the wrong time or on the wrong day can mean a fine: $50, then $100, then $200.

Source: DSNY, Collection Laws for Residents · DSNY, Trash · DSNY, Empire Bins FAQ

What goes in recycling?

Two groups, each in a labeled bin with a secure lid or a clear plastic bag: metal, glass, plastic and cartons together; and mixed paper and cardboard together. Flattened cardboard can be tied with twine and set next to the bins. Don't use cardboard boxes as bins.

Source: DSNY, Recycling for Residents · DSNY, Collection Laws for Residents

What goes in trash, not recycling?

DSNY's list includes plastic bags, bubble wrap and snack wrappers, foam, diapers and wipes, napkins and paper towels, dishes and mirrors, hardcover books, cables, cords and string lights, and wooden furniture.

Source: DSNY, Trash

My trash wasn't picked up. What do I do?

Report it through 311 or online starting at 8 AM the day after your collection day.

Source: DSNY, Trash

How do I get rid of furniture or a mattress?

Pickup is free. Put up to 6 large items at the curb per collection day; appointments are no longer taken. Metal and hard plastic items go out with recycling, and everything else on your trash-only day. Mattresses and box springs must be fully sealed in a clear plastic bag, or you can be fined $50, then $100, then $200.

Source: DSNY, Large Items · DSNY, Collection Laws for Residents

Composting

Is composting required?

Yes. Since April 1, 2025, every New York City resident has to keep food scraps, food-soiled paper and yard waste out of the trash, and property owners can be fined if they don't.

Source: DSNY, Curbside Composting · DSNY, Collection Laws for Residents

What is the fine?
  • Buildings with 1 to 8 homes: $25, then $50, then $100.
  • Buildings with 9 or more homes: $100, then $200, then $300.

Source: DSNY, Collection Laws for Residents

What goes in the compost?

All food scraps, including meat, bones, shells and dairy; cooked food; greasy uncoated paper plates and pizza boxes; anything labeled compostable; and all leaves and yard waste, including flowers and Christmas trees. Not wrappers, pet waste, diapers, foam, metal, glass, plastic, cartons, clean paper or cardboard.

Source: DSNY, Curbside Composting

When is it picked up, and in what?

Every week on your recycling day. Use a labeled bin of 55 gallons or less with a secure lid, or your DSNY brown bin. Extra yard waste can go in paper lawn bags or clear plastic bags, and branches can be tied with twine.

Source: DSNY, Curbside Composting

Will it smell or attract rats?

DSNY says a bin with a secure lid and a clear plastic liner smells no worse than the trash. Its tips: keep the lid shut, drain liquids, put paper or baking soda in the bottom, tie the bag before it goes to the curb, and keep scraps in the freezer until then.

Source: DSNY, Curbside Composting FAQ

My building doesn't compost. What can I do?

Buildings with 4 or more homes have to give residents a labeled compost storage area. You can request a DSNY training or site visit, point building staff to DSNY's webinar, volunteer to manage the bin, report the building, or use a food scrap drop-off. CB6 has six; they're on the map.

Source: DSNY, Curbside Composting FAQ · DSNY, Curbside Composting

Sidewalks, curbs and litter baskets

Who has to clean the sidewalk?

The property owner, along the building's edge, including tree pits and the 18 inches of street next to the curb. Sweepings go in a lidded bin, not a public litter basket. Residential properties are checked 8 to 9 AM and 6 to 7 PM, seven days a week. Fines run up to $100.

Source: DSNY, Sidewalks and Gutters

What about businesses?

Businesses have to keep their sidewalks, tree pits and the 18 inches of gutter clean. Since December 19, 2024, repeat fines in a 12-month period are $50, then $300, then $500. Businesses must put out trash and food waste in lidded bins one hour before closing or after 8 PM.

Source: DSNY, Cleaning Rules for Businesses · DSNY, Setout Rules for Businesses

Can I put household trash in a corner litter basket?

No. Public litter baskets are for people walking by. Household or business trash in a basket can mean a fine of up to $350. Dog waste is allowed if it's in a tied bag.

Source: DSNY, Litter & Litter Baskets

How do I get a litter basket on my corner?

Request one through DSNY. Baskets go on commercial or mixed-use streets or near transit. DSNY doesn't accept requests for mostly residential or industrial streets or for the middle of a block.

Source: DSNY, Litter & Litter Baskets

What is the fine for littering?

Up to $400, for littering or for an owner who doesn't keep the public areas by their property clean.

Source: DSNY, Litter & Litter Baskets

Businesses and Commercial Waste Zones

What are Commercial Waste Zones?

Local Law 199 of 2019 splits the city into 20 zones, each with three carters the City has authorized to serve businesses there. When a zone rolls out, every business with a private carter has to sign a new agreement with one of that zone's carters.

Source: DSNY, Commercial Waste Zones

What does a business in CB6 need to do, and when?

CB6 is in the Brooklyn West zone, which has no rollout date yet. When it rolls out, businesses compare the zone's carters, check DSNY's maximum price calculator, negotiate below those prices and sign. A business that hasn't signed by the end of the rollout gets a carter assigned by DSNY, and posts the green DSNY decal its carter gives it.

Source: DSNY, CWZ What You Need to Do · DSNY, Commercial Waste Zones

Does this apply to every business?

No. Nonprofits, government agencies and professional offices in residential buildings that get DSNY pickup are not covered.

Source: DSNY, CWZ What You Need to Do
