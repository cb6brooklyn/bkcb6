Why Citi Field has no community board

Joint interest areas explained

A joint interest area is city land that no single community board covers.

New York is divided into 59 community districts, each with its own board. But large parks and airports are not carved up among them. Instead the City designates them joint interest areas, numbered in the 60s through the 90s, and the neighbouring boards share an interest in what happens there rather than one board owning it outright.

Citi Field is the clearest example. The ballpark, along with the whole USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center next door, sits inside Queens Joint Interest Area 81, Flushing Meadows Corona Park. It is not in Queens Community Board 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 or 9 — it is bordered by all six of them.

Why this matters beyond trivia. When Mets ownership proposed the Metropolitan Park casino and entertainment complex on the Citi Field parking lot, the land was legally mapped as parkland even though it is asphalt. That is why the project needed zoning text and city map amendments rather than an ordinary rezoning, and why several adjacent Queens boards each took up the proposal instead of one board deciding for the site.
