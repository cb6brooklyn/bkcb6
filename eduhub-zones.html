Address lookupWhat am I zoned for?

Street address plus borough works best, for example 250 Baltic Street Brooklyn.

A zone is the school your address has a right to apply to first. It is not a guarantee of a seat, and it is not the only school you can apply to. Elementary zones cover most of the city. Middle school varies: some districts zone you to one school, others are choice districts where families rank schools instead. High school is mostly citywide choice, so most addresses return no zoned high school at all. Where that is the case, the result says so and gives the enrollment phone number published with the zone.

SourceWhat these zones are

Boundaries are the NYC Department of Education 2024-2025 school zones: 770 elementary, 279 middle and 80 high school zones. School names, addresses, grade spans and phone numbers are joined by DBN to the DOE school location directory published on NYC Open Data. 799 of 812 elementary, 275 of 279 middle and 32 of 32 high school code references matched a directory record; the remainder are shown by their DBN code with a note, because those schools opened after the directory snapshot. 30 elementary, 36 middle and 48 high school areas carry no zoned school at all, mostly parks, cemeteries, airports, and choice districts where families apply rather than being zoned.

Being zoned for a school is not the same as being placed in it. Most high schools admit citywide rather than by zone, which is why 48 of the 80 high school areas are marked as citywide choice. Always confirm with the school or your enrollment office.

ProgramsWhat sits behind these categories

Every site on this page belongs to one of these city programs. The logos are the agencies' own.

Public and charter schoolsRun or authorized by the Department of Education, which also draws the school zones and the 32 community school districts. Private and parochial schools are counted here too but are not DOE schools.
Day careLicensed child care programs, including school-based preschool child care.
Pre-K and 3-KThe DOE early childhood programs. The city's facilities data files both under one early education code, so this hub counts them together rather than guessing a split.

Data: NYC Department of City Planning Facilities Database (FacDB), dataset ji82-xba5, published on NYC Open Data. Sites are placed in community districts using the district code published with each record; records missing that code are placed by point-in-polygon against DCP community district boundaries, or by geocoding the published address through NYC Planning GeoSearch. Every source record is accounted for on the hub page.

School counts include public, charter, private and parochial, and special education sites. Grade level comes from the facility type published with each record; 331 school records carry no grade level and are shown as Level not published. Childcare counts include day care, DOE pre-K and 3-K, and preschools for students with disabilities. Public libraries are not on this hub. Basemap © OpenStreetMap contributors, © CARTO.
