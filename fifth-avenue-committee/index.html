Fifth Avenue Committee

Community based organisation · Park Slope and Gowanus, Brooklyn

The Fifth Avenue Committee has been at this since 1978, and it is one of the few organisations in the district that does development, organizing and services at the same time rather than picking one. It reaches more than 6,500 low and moderate income New Yorkers a year.

Its stated aim is economic, social and racial justice, pursued through community-centred affordable housing, grassroots organizing, policy advocacy, and education and training. That combination is the point. An organisation that only builds housing does not have tenants organized; one that only organizes does not own anything.

The lot at 621 DeGraw is owned by FAC Center Local Development Corporation, which tells you something. They own the building they organize from.

—Mike Racioppo

What it does

On housing it builds and manages affordable and supportive housing, with a pipeline running to as many as 1,900 units, and it has done the unglamorous work too: gut renovations of small rental buildings, LEED Gold mixed-income developments, and a public library, a public park and five Pre-K classrooms folded into its projects.

On the ground it runs tenant counseling and organizing, foreclosure prevention and homebuyer counseling, and adult education including GED prep, English classes and digital literacy for over a thousand adults a year.

Workforce development runs through its affiliate Brooklyn Workforce Innovations. South Brooklyn Against Displacement is its organizing arm.
