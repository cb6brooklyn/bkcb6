The Old Stone House

Historic house and Washington Park · Park Slope and Gowanus, Brooklyn

What it is

The Old Stone House sits where history actually happened, in the middle of Washington Park, between 3rd and 4th Streets and between 4th and 5th Avenues. It runs family programs, arts and cultural events, addresses neighborhood issues, and manages the gardens; aka it’s a great institution.

A house that has grown by leaps and bounds in the decades since I was in middle school in the late 1990s. Much of that is down to Executive Director Kim Maier, who has built it into a place that honors the past, lives in the present, and improves the future.

The building, the actual house, is a reconstruction of the 1699 Vechte-Cortelyou House, on land taken from the Lenape as early as 1639. It stands in Washington Park on the border of Park Slope and Gowanus, marking the site of the original Dutch farmstead and the culminating engagement of the Battle of Brooklyn in 1776. It is also the birthplace of the Brooklyn Dodgers back when there were trolley’s to dodge. It is a Historic House Trust of New York City site and rightly resides on the National Register of Historic Places.

An interactive exhibit on the largest battle of the Revolutionary War and what living in Brooklyn was like from 1776 to 1783. Contemporary shows by emerging artists. Gardens of native and useful plants. School visits and field trips. Concerts, readings, lectures and theater, including Piper Theatre. A summer camp. And the playground, courts, turf field and dog run that make up JJ Byrne.
