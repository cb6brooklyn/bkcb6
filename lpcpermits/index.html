Landmarks Permits Building by Building

Every application filed with the Landmarks Preservation Commission, mapped and searchable
Nothing changes on a landmarked building without this. Replace a window, repoint a facade, rebuild an areaway, add rooftop mechanicals, put up a storefront sign: in a historic district or on an individual landmark, it goes through the Landmarks Preservation Commission first. Most of it is decided by staff and never reaches a public hearing. The record of what was asked for, what was approved, and who asked is public, and this is that record.
Filings run from the mid 1980s to the present. Certificates of Appropriateness, the ones the Commission votes on in public, are a small share of the total. Everything else moves at staff level.

What the permit types mean.
Certificate of Appropriateness
The Commission itself votes, in public, after a hearing. Reserved for work that changes a building's significant features: additions, new construction, major facade alterations. This is where a community board weighs in.
Certificate of No Effect
Staff finds the work does not affect any significant protected feature. Usually interior work or work requiring a Department of Buildings permit that leaves the exterior alone.
Permit for Minor Work
Staff approves work that does not require a Buildings permit: window replacement in kind, repointing, painting, small repairs.
Notice of Compliance
Sign off confirming work was completed as permitted.
Miscellaneous, amendment, status update
Changes to a permit already issued, or administrative correspondence on an open application.
