History

Dutch farmers settled the area in the mid-1600s, when it was called Punkiesberg. During the Battle of Long Island in 1776 the Continental Army called it Cobble Hill, after a hill it had fortified during the Siege of Boston. The fort here, Smith's Barbette, was part of a defensive line from Gowanus Bay to Wallabout Bay.

The block between Verandah Place, Clinton Street and Henry Street once held the Weber and Whitten mansions and an empty Unitarian church. The Bohack Corporation bought the land in the late 1940s and tore down the mansions; in the early 1960s it proposed a supermarket there, and the neighborhood opposed it.

In 1962 builders bought the site to put up a six-story, 128-family apartment building. From April to July 1962 the Cobble Hill Association, the West Brooklyn Independent Democrats and neighbors gathered 1,200 signatures and petitioned the City Planning Commission, the Budget Bureau, Parks and the Mayor's Office to make it a park.

The land became City property on March 7, 1963, and Cobble Hill Park was dedicated on July 14, 1965, with trees, shrubs, game tables and a sand pit.

Parks rebuilt it in 1989 in materials that match the neighborhood's 19th-century houses: bluestone, cast iron, granite entrance columns and herringbone brick paths. The design won the Art Commission's 1988 Award for Excellence in Design and the Parks Council's first Philip Winslow Award in 1990.
