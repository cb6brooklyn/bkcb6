History

Named for Thomas Greene (1911-1988), born in Savannah, Georgia, who came to New York in 1932 and lived in the Gowanus Houses. After retiring from Cornell University's New York City offices in 1976 he led the Gowanus Tenant Association, served on Community Board 6 and the boards of local development groups, and ran the Gowanus Senior Citizen Center.

Parks took charge of the site in 1935, when it opened as the playground locals called Double D, for its spot between DeGraw and Douglass Streets. The City acquired the property in 1938.

A 1988 local law renamed it for Thomas Greene. A $213,659 renovation funded by Borough President Howard Golden was completed ten years later, with new play equipment, safety surfacing and handball courts.

It has camel animal art, benches, play equipment, a drinking fountain, picnic tables and basketball and handball courts.
