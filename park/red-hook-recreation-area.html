Red Hook Recreation Area

Community park · Red Hook · 58.50 acres

What it is

Handball, basketball and soccer, a busy playground, an Olympic-size outdoor pool in summer, a running track and a recreation center with cardio equipment, weights and exercise classes.

History

The City acquired the first piece of this land on October 10, 1913, for freight rail terminals. It was assigned to Parks on June 27, 1934, and the other parcels came to Parks between 1935 and 1947.

Landscape architect Gilmore D. Clarke laid out the original plan under Parks Commissioner Robert Moses. Then called the Red Hook Recreation Center, it opened on August 12, 1936; the New York Times reported more than 40,000 people, many of them children, cheering Mayor Fiorello La Guardia at the opening.

Parks' history of the site ties it to Red Hook's story: the docks of the Atlantic and Erie Basins, the Red Hook Houses (opened in 1938 for dockworkers' families), the 1946 Gowanus Expressway that cut Red Hook off from the rest of Brooklyn, and a population that fell from 18,575 in 1960 to 10,485 in 1980.

Today it holds paths, benches, handball courts, baseball fields, a soccer and football field, picnic tables and the Sol Goldman Pool next door.
