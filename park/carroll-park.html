CARROLL PARK

Carroll Park

Neighborhood park · Carroll Gardens · 1.87 acres

What it is

One of the oldest parks in Brooklyn, in three parts: an asphalt yard with basketball hoops and a baseball and kickball diamond for older kids, a play area for older and younger kids with a compass-shaped spray shower, and a shady section with seating and public bathrooms.

History

Brooklyn's third-oldest park. It is named for Charles Carroll (1737-1832), a signer of the Declaration of Independence who served in the Continental Congress and as a U.S. Senator from Maryland. Carroll Street carries his name too.

It began in the late 1840s as a private garden, soon after surveyor Richard Butt laid out much of what is now the Carroll Gardens Historic District. The City of Brooklyn bought the land for a public park in 1853.

Around 1870 it got drainage, new walks and a children's playground. Later rebuilds in the 1890s, 1930s and 1960s added playgrounds and more room for active play.

In 1994 a $1.3 million reconstruction funded by Borough President Howard Golden replanted the park, reorganized the play areas and put back cast-iron gates and fencing that echo the neighborhood's brownstones. The bronze and granite World War I Soldiers and Sailors Monument (1920) by sculptor Eugene H. Morahan was conserved in the same project.

The Committee to Improve Carroll Park, formed in 1975, works with NYC Parks on programs, upkeep and care of the park.
