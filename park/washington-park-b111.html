Washington Park

Neighborhood park · Park Slope · 3.03 acres

What it is

Once part of the ground fought over in the Battle of Brooklyn, Washington Park has a skatepark, basketball courts, a dog run, a synthetic turf field, J.J. Byrne Playground for toddlers and older kids, and the Old Stone House, now a center for Brooklyn history and the Revolution.

History

Parks acquired the land in 1926, and the Board of Aldermen named it J.J. Byrne Park in 1933 for James J. Byrne (1863-1930), who grew up near the Navy Yard, rose from Building Department clerk to Brooklyn Commissioner of Public Works, and was Brooklyn Borough President from 1926 until he died in office in 1930.

The Old Stone House at the back of the park reconstructs the Vechte-Cortelyou house, built by Claes Arents Vechte in 1699 and torn down around 1897. Its foundations were found in 1930, Byrne ordered it rebuilt from the original material, and it was finished in 1934 as a park office and restroom.

On August 27, 1776, in the Battle of Long Island, the Maryland 400 under Lord Stirling attacked British troops holding the house so George Washington's army could escape across Gowanus Creek. About 250 of them died.

The house later belonged to the Cortelyou family (from 1790) and Edwin C. Litchfield (from 1850), and served as the clubhouse of the Bridegrooms, the baseball team that came before the Dodgers.

In 1951 the park was joined with the new William Alexander Junior High School 51 and its playground. A full reconstruction of the Old Stone House was finished in 1996, and Parks' page reports a $10.9 million allocation to make it more accessible and upgrade it.
