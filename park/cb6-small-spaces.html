What these are

The Parks Department counts every piece of land it holds as a park, including slivers left over where streets meet or highways were built. These four are in CB6. Each is a few thousand square feet or less of planting, paving or empty lot, with no playground, courts or fields: not what most people picture when they hear “park.” They still count toward the district's park acreage.

For scale: an acre is 43,560 square feet. The smallest here, 0.03 acres, is about 1,300 square feet.

A Greenstreets planting bounded by Todd, Columbia and Halleck Streets, with London plane trees and a flagpole.

Named for William H. Todd (1857-1932), who started as a rivet boy, became foreman of the Robins Dry Dock in Red Hook in 1896 and built Todd Shipyards Corporation, with seven shipyards the largest company of its kind in America. The land came with the larger Red Hook Park acquisition in 1934.

Greenstreets, a Parks and Department of Transportation program begun in 1986 and revived in 1994, turns paved leftover street spaces like this one into planted green areas.

New Yorkers for Parks: listed as “Todd Triangle” among the district's parks in its 2021 Open Space Profile.
