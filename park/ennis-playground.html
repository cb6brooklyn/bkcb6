History

The City bought the land from John Bean in 1856. It was a storage yard; with almost no open space in Gowanus, it became a playground in the 1920s, assigned to Parks in two parts, in 1924 and 1929.

It is believed to be named for Thomas Ennis (1835-1900), an Irish immigrant who served three terms as a Brooklyn alderman, was city assessor for 25 years, and in 1890 founded a coal company on the Gowanus Canal. It was named for him in 1929.

The Gowanus Alliance and the Friends of Ennis Playground have organized cleanups, public art and family events. The playground reopened after a $2.9 million renovation in 2019.
