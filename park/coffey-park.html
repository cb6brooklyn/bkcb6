History

Named for Michael J. Coffey (1839-1907), Red Hook's longtime Democratic district leader, who also served as an alderman, Assemblyman and State Senator. Born in County Cork, Ireland, he grew up in Red Hook, worked at the Brooklyn Navy Yard and served in the Navy in the Civil War. By the end of his 39 years as district leader, Red Hook was known locally as "Coffeyville." Partition Street was renamed Coffey Street in 1891.

The land came in three pieces: 5.284 acres bought by the Brooklyn Parks Department in 1892 (the park opened in 1901), 2.298 acres bought in 1907, and a 0.689-acre parcel on Pioneer Street between Richards and Dwight Streets added in 1943.

A renovation finished on July 7, 1999, paid for with $840,000 from Council Member Angel Rodriguez, $1,055,000 from Borough President Howard Golden and $24,000 from Mayor Giuliani.

The park has a spray shower, play equipment, swings, game and picnic tables, basketball and handball courts, an asphalt baseball diamond, hippopotamus animal art and crocodile-shaped benches.
