History

Named for Tracy S. Voorhees (1890-1974), a lawyer, president of Long Island College Hospital from 1936 to 1944, Assistant Secretary of the Army under President Truman, and President Eisenhower's representative for resettling 32,000 Hungarian refugees in 1956 and 1957. His family traces back to Steven Coerten Van Voorhees, who settled in Flatlands in the mid-1600s.

It began as the Jeannie Scott Dike Playground, a block north between Pacific, Congress, Columbia and Hicks Streets. The City acquired the land in four parcels, in 1864, 1941, 1942 and 1947; the 1941 parcel was a gift from Long Island College Hospital while Voorhees was its president, and the park took his name.

Building the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway enlarged the park to 5.25 acres by 1956.

Council Member Angel Rodriguez funded a $714,942 rebuild of the playground in 1998, and Mayor Giuliani a $155,425 renovation of the handball courts and paving in 2000. Parks' page also reports a new synthetic turf ballfield.

Parks counts 32 trees here that are at least 50 years old.
