New Yorkers for Parks

Parks advocacy organization · citywide, based in Lower Manhattan

New Yorkers for Parks is the city’s parks advocacy organization. It works with local communities, public officials and advocates on the creation, protection and care of New York City’s parks and open spaces.

It began in 1908, when the Brooklyn Society for Parks and Playgrounds merged with two other groups, and took the name New Yorkers for Parks in 2002.

What New Yorkers for Parks does

It produces research on the city’s open space: Open Space Profiles for every community board, the Open Space Index, City Council district profiles and the Report Card on Parks series. Its 2021 profile of Brooklyn Community Board 6 covers Park Slope, Carroll Gardens, Gowanus and Red Hook.

It co-founded the Play Fair for Parks coalition, more than 400 members, with the New York League of Conservation Voters and District Council 37, calling for greater city investment in the parks system, and runs campaigns such as Fix the Basics: Fund Parks Now, NYC Needs Car-Free Parks, Fix the Bathrooms and Drinking Fountains, and Let Red Hook Swim, to reopen Red Hook Pool and invest in public pools citywide.

Each fall it runs the Daffodil Project, distributing free daffodil bulbs and native seeds to volunteers in every borough as a living memorial to the victims of September 11. The project began in 2001.
