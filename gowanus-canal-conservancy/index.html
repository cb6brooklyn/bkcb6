Gowanus Canal Conservancy

Community based organisation · Gowanus, Brooklyn

The Gowanus Canal Conservancy is the environmental steward of the canal and of the watershed that drains into it. In a combined sewer city those are the same thing, and if that sentence means nothing to you, spend ten minutes with them and it will. Their mission is ecologically sustainable parks and public spaces in the Gowanus lowlands, and a community of stewards to look after them. Easy to write down. Harder to pull off on a Superfund site.

Consider where they work. A federal cleanup, a rezoning and a combined sewer system all land on the same few blocks, and the Conservancy is the group holding the environmental thread through all of it. They work with the EPA, DEP, Riverkeeper and more universities than I can keep straight on water quality, and they run the green infrastructure and street tree work that determines whether the rain that falls on Gowanus ends up in the canal or in the ground.

During the rezoning they did something I wish happened more often. They showed up with a plan, not a position. Their Gowanus Lowlands master plan was out years before the vote and it said, in pictures, what a public waterfront on the canal could actually be: parks and esplanades that connect, stormwater handled where it falls, the canal as the middle of the neighborhood instead of the back of it. When the City’s plan came, they supported it and worked to get the Lowlands thinking into it, the waterfront access, the open space commitments, the stormwater standards new buildings have to meet. A good deal of what will make the rezoning livable rather than just bigger goes back to that, and they are still at the table on the Oversight Task Force making sure it gets built.

Then there is the week to week, which is what I’d point to first. The Lowlands Nursery at the Salt Lot grows the native plants that end up in bioswales and rain gardens around the district. Gowanus Blue Schools puts local kids in the watershed, literally, learning how the sewer works by testing the water. Volunteer days keep the street trees, tree pits and bioswales alive, and if you have ever wondered why one bioswale works and another is a planter full of trash, that’s the answer. And the water quality monitoring gives the rest of us numbers instead of guesses, which I appreciate more than most.

A lot of that is Andrea Parker. She has run the Conservancy for over a decade. She is a landscape architect by training and it shows: systems, not slogans. Every meeting I’ve been in with her, whether on the rezoning, the CSO tanks or one tree pit on Third Avenue, she comes prepared, says what she thinks, and knows the difference between what’s possible and what’s easy. She took a group that started with volunteers and rakes and made it the one agencies call before they finalize a plan instead of after.

The team around her is why the nursery is stocked, the volunteer days are full and the students show up: educators, horticulturists, organizers, people who can explain a combined sewer to a fourth grader and a commissioner in the same afternoon. One of them, Aurelia Casey, is also a member of Community Board 6, so the Conservancy’s work and the board’s work meet in the same person more than once a month. Someone who knows both the watershed and the ULURP calendar is exactly what this district needs, and we’re lucky to have her.

Environmental stewardship is easy to say and hard to keep doing. They keep doing it.

—Mike Racioppo

What it does

It runs the Lowlands Nursery, growing native urban-adapted plants and selling them to residents, institutions and landscape designers, with proceeds going back into education and stewardship.

It runs the Gowanus Green Team, paid high school apprentices doing conservation work, and the Gowanus Tree Network, which trains neighbours as Citizen Pruners to look after the street trees on their own blocks.

It works out of the Old American Can Factory, a three storey 1890 complex at Third Street and Third Avenue that now houses artists, makers and non-profits. The lot is zoned M1-4/R7X inside the Gowanus special district, the mixed use zoning the 2021 rezoning brought in.
