Trees in CB6

Every street tree in Brooklyn Community Board 6, the tree canopy over every block, and when each street was last pruned, on one map. Pick what to see with the buttons above the map; each one says in a line what its colors mean.

How the street trees are doing

The Parks Department's latest rating of every street tree in CB6.

How big they are

Trunk width measured 4.5 feet off the ground. Bigger trunks mean bigger, older trees and more shade.

The most common street trees

The 15 kinds planted most along CB6's streets. Tap a bar to see only that kind on the map.

Blocks with the least shade

The share of each block, with its half of the surrounding streets, covered by tree canopy in 2017, lowest first. Blocks that are mostly park or waterfront are included as they are. Tap a block to find it on the map.

What these numbers are

Tree canopy is the ground covered by the leaves and branches of trees seen from above, from the City's 2017 laser survey of the whole city (LiDAR), compared with 2010. It counts every tree, on streets, in parks and in backyards. It is the same source New Yorkers for Parks uses; their 2021 profile puts CB6 at 17%, and this map comes to . Canopy has not been surveyed citywide since 2017.

Street trees are the trees the Parks Department cares for along sidewalks, from its current tree inventory, with each tree's condition, kind and trunk size. Trees inside parks and on private property are not in it.

Pruning is the last year the Parks Department pruned every tree on a street segment (block pruning), from its work orders back to 1995. The full history, tree by tree, is on the Tree Pruning Map.

Sources: NYC Parks Forestry Tree Points (NYC Open Data hn5i-inap), retrieved ; NYC Tree Canopy Change 2010 to 2017 (by9k-vhck); NYC Parks Forestry Work Orders (bdjm-n7q4), through . Blocks are tax blocks; each block's canopy share includes the ground between it and the next block, split down the middle.
