Block by Block, Read Against the Record

The city's housing plan, set beside what the data shows
In May 2026 the Mayor and the Deputy Mayor for Housing and Planning published Block by Block. It makes a set of factual claims about why housing costs what it does in New York, and commits the city to a scale of building it has not attempted in decades. This page takes the claims that can be checked and checks them against the public record, district by district.

What the city says caused this

The plan is unusually direct about the role of land use rules.
From the plan
The comprehensive 1961 rezoning reduced citywide housing capacity by 80 percent, and the regulatory tools that followed have "too frequently been used for exclusionary ends, especially in the city's wealthiest, resource-rich neighborhoods."
Block by Block, chapter 4.2

That is a claim about who decides what gets built and where. It is checkable, because every one of those decisions leaves a record.

Does new housing raise rents nearby?

The most common objection at a land use hearing. Every community district, homes completed 2015 to 2024, against how much median asking rent moved over the same ten years.

There is no relationship. Rents rose almost everywhere over this period, at nearly the same rate in districts that built at scale and districts that built next to nothing. How much a district built explains none of how much its rent moved.

This does not show that building lowers rents. It shows that the claim new development drives up rents in the surrounding district has nothing in ten years of citywide data supporting it.

What the review process has actually produced

Every rezoning on record since 1976, read from the applications themselves.

The plan describes ULURP and environmental review as tools that made housing harder to build. The record shows what they have been used for: the single most common change is manufacturing land converted to residential, and the second most common is a manufacturing district changed to another manufacturing district.
