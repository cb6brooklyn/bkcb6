What this page counts

Every number on this page links to the public data or document it comes from. Nothing here is a projection of the final design, which the city has not released.

  • The corridor. The city announced bike boulevards on "the Bergen and Dean Street corridors from Court Street to East New York Avenue in Brooklyn," spanning 10 miles (NYC Mayor's Office, May 2026). This page maps Bergen Street (4.67 miles) and Dean Street (4.44 miles) as drawn in the NYC street centerline file (lengths calculated).
  • The block. Bergen Street between 4th Avenue and 5th Avenue, about 784 feet long (calculated from the centerline).
  • "Within 100 feet." Crashes, 311 requests and police complaints are counted if their mapped point is within 100 feet of the street's centerline, which takes in the intersections at each end. Each count links to the NYC Open Data query that returns it, drawn with that same 100-foot outline.
  • Dates. From January 1, 2020 through the latest record: crashes through June 11, 2026, 311 through September 25, 2026.

The plan

In May 2026 the Mayor's Office announced bike boulevards on "the Bergen and Dean Street corridors from Court Street to East New York Avenue in Brooklyn" (NYC Mayor's Office).

  • What a bike boulevard may include: "protected bike lanes, sidewalk extensions, medians, traffic-calming measures and pedestrian improvements" (NYC Mayor's Office).
  • Timeline: the city "plans to release a design proposal later this year, with the first phase targeted for installation in 2027" (NYC Mayor's Office).
  • What is not decided: Streetsblog reported "It is even unclear which stretches of the roadways will get the treatment," and "Details of the design have not been finalized" (Streetsblog, May 6, 2026).
  • The Bergen Bike Bus: the Mayor's Office called it "the city's most popular 'bike bus,' a weekly caravan of parents and students traveling to school together" (NYC Mayor's Office).
  • Reactions on the street: News 12 quoted a cyclist who said "these streets in particular leading into Downtown Brooklyn are absolutely a mess," and a store owner who said "The delivery truck can't park next to the curb because the bike lane is next to the curb, so that could be problematic" (News 12 Brooklyn, May 8, 2026).

Buses: the B65 today and in the draft plan

Today the B65 "runs between Downtown Brooklyn and Crown Heights" over 4.4 miles; the draft plan shortens it to 4.1 miles and would reroute it "to travel on Atlantic Av in both directions between Downtown Brooklyn and Washington Av," discontinuing service on "Dean St and Bergen St west of Washington Av" (MTA, B65 Local draft plan).

  • On the block today. The MTA's bus speed data lists a B65 timepoint at "BERGEN ST/5 AV," at the block's east end, and one at "DEAN ST/5 AV" (MTA segment speed data).
  • Status. The MTA page presents the change as a draft plan and gives no effective date (MTA).
  • Why it matters for the boulevard. Streetsblog reported the reroute frees "one mile on Dean Street and 1.5 miles on Bergen Street for a bike boulevard design that won't put cyclists in conflict with buses" (Streetsblog, May 11, 2026).

Truck routes

Bergen Street between 4th and 5th Avenues is a designated local truck route, and so are 3 more Bergen Street segments in BKCB2 (calculated from NYC DOT truck route data, split by district on the bkcb6.app truck routes map). Dean Street has no truck route segments.

  • What a local route is. "The Local Truck Route Network is designated for trucks with an origin and destination within a borough." A truck may use a street that is not a truck route only "for the purpose of arriving at his or her destination" (NYC DOT, Truck Routing).
  • October 4, 2026. None of the 103 truck route changes on DOT's list is on Bergen or Dean (calculated from NYC DOT, map and list of changes).
  • What is not known. The city has not said whether the boulevard design would change Bergen Street's truck route status (none of the sources above addresses it).

Open questions

Questions the public record does not yet answer, with where each comes from.

  • Which stretches, and what design? "It is even unclear which stretches of the roadways will get the treatment" (Streetsblog); the city says a design proposal is coming "later this year" (Mayor's Office).
  • Deliveries and curb access. A store owner raised whether delivery trucks could reach the curb if a bike lane runs next to it (News 12). The block is a local truck route (see above).
  • Parking. None of the city documents above says how much curb parking a design would keep or remove.
  • When the B65 moves. The MTA's B65 page is a draft plan with no effective date (MTA).
  • The truck route. Whether Bergen Street stays a local truck route under a boulevard design is not addressed in the announcement (Mayor's Office).
