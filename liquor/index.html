Liquor Licenses District by District

Every active and pending on-premises licence in the five boroughs
Community boards get a say on these. Before the State Liquor Authority acts on a new or renewed on-premises licence, the applicant is expected to notify the community board, and the board can hold a hearing and send the SLA a recommendation. The SLA is not bound by it, but a board on record against an application matters. This is every licence the state currently lists, so you can see what is already on a block before the next application turns up. CB6’s own detailed map is at the CB6 liquor map.

What the classes mean, and what this list is not

The three groupings

  • Full Liquor: restaurants, bars, clubs, hotels and caterers licensed to serve spirits as well as wine and beer.
  • Wine & Beer: the same kinds of premises limited to wine and beer, with no spirits.
  • Brewery / Special: manufacturers with a licence to serve on site, so brewers, cideries, wineries and distilleries.

What this list is not

These are on-premises licences only, places where you drink where you buy. Liquor stores, grocery beer licences and other off-premises classes are excluded, which is why the number here is a fraction of every SLA record in the city.

A licence is not a business. One address can hold several, an additional bar licence sits alongside the main one, and a licence can outlast the business that took it out. Pending means the application is filed, not granted.

The address is the premises as filed with the state, and a few hundred records statewide carry no usable coordinates and are therefore not shown.
