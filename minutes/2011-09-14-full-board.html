CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……..………………………...……..………….....…...DANIEL M. KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel M. Kummer began his report by re-introducing our newest members: Paige Bellenbaum (Levin, Carroll Gardens), Neena Dutta (BP, Carroll Gardens), John Heyer (BP, Columbia Waterfront) our former liaison from the Borough President’s Office, Hildegaard Link (BP, Park Slope) a longtime member of our Transportation Committee, Charles Pigott (BP, Park Slope), Benjamin Solotaire (Lander, Park Slope) member of our Youth/Human Services/Education Committee, and Dolly Williams (Levin, Park Slope) former member of CB6 but had to resign when she was appointed a City Planning Commissioner.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that everyone should have received their official committee appointment letters with committee assignments for the year. He announced that he has made a couple of small changes to the Executive Committee. He appointed Benjamin Solotaire to Chair our Human Services Committee, as a new addition to our Executive Committee. In addition, Mark Kolman, who had been chairing Human Services, will now be Co-Chairing our Education Committee with Suzanne Turet. He welcomed Benjamin to the Executive Committee and thanked all of our current committee chairs for their continuing commitment to the board.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that over the course of the summer the Executive Committee did have to meet once to vote on some issues that had come out of various committees. A full list of these votes is in the July Executive Committee minutes, which are included in tonight’s Board Reports. They largely consisted of ratifying recommendations by our Permits & Licenses Committee, which continues to work tirelessly on behalf of our board to balance our desire to maintain a thriving local business climate that is respectful of our community’s quality of life. We did also ratify our Land Use Committee’s recommendation to conditionally approve the Department of City Planning’s Special Fourth Avenue Enhanced Commercial District rezoning which sought to improve upon the past rezoning by requiring uses that are more active and pedestrian- friendly designs of the ground level spaces for new buildings along 4th Avenue. He added that the Borough President has also held his hearing and offered his support for the recommendation, and the application will now be before the City Planning Commission who will be conducting a public hearing next Wednesday, September 21st at 10:00am at the Manhattan Office at 22 Reade Street if anyone is interested in attending. We will have a representative there to present testimony on our behalf.

Chairperson Kummer advised the Board that September is also the time of year when the Chairperson appoints an Elections Committee should any of our officer positions be contested which would result in the need to hold an election at our regular meeting in December. This year he asked last year’s Chair, Board Member Peter Fleming, to continue in that position. A letter from Elections Chair Fleming will be going out within the next few days outlining and explaining the process more fully.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that October is the month that we must review, consider and adopt Capital and Expense budget requests which are submitted to the Mayor’s Office and City Council for funding consideration for the fiscal year 2013 City budget. District Manager Craig Hammerman, Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch, Budget Chair Rick Luftglass and I will be attending a budget consultation with many of our City agencies next week where we will have a chance to talk to them, albeit briefly, about some of our pending budget requests. If Board members are interested in seeing the requests that we submitted last year, along with the City agencies’ responses to them, they are available on the Community Board’s website on the Budget Committee’s homepage. District Manager Hammerman will also send out the draft budget requests for FY2013 after the Executive Committee adopts them next month but before the general meeting so you will have an opportunity to look them over beforehand.

District Manager Craig Hammerman reported to the Board that for this academic year the district office would have a new planning intern, Melissa Umburger from Pratt. Melissa and I have been working on a draft RFP that we would like to send out by the end of October so that we can bring our consultants on board and move forward with the project. Luke DePalma, representing Borough President Marty Markowitz invited everyone to the Sixth Annual Brooklyn Book Festival starting on September 18th at Brooklyn Borough Hall. The Festival is the largest free literary event in New York City presenting an array of literary stars and emerging authors who represent the exciting world of literature today.

Chairperson Kummer recognized Council Member Brad Lander whom began by informing the Board on two events he will be hosting.

1. Participatory Budgeting, which is grassroots democracy at its best. Residents in our district will come together to decide what to do with $1 million in capital funding, the part of the budget that is used to repair streets, improve parks, buy school technology, or build bike lanes. Community members will exchange and debate ideas, work together to turn ideas into project proposals, and then decide what ideas are funded at the ballot box. The process will make budgeting more transparent and accessible. It will open up participation to people who have never been involved before and make budgeting more effective. There will be a public meeting on October 5th at the Old First Reformed Church where the Council Member will present information on the budget funds, and residents will brainstorm project ideas and select budget delegates. For more information, please visit http://bradlander.com/PB.

2. Bike Share Program, which will provide secure and convenient bicycles on a 24-hour basis, and at publicly accessible places. The City has selected a vendor and will be seeking the Community Boards input on where the stations should be located. The vendor will bear all the cost and responsibilities for the system during an initial five-year period while sharing revenues with the city, and with no taxpayer funds being used for the system’s implementation, upkeep or maintenance. A system could be started by spring 2012.

TRANSPORTATION COMMITTEE………..……..………………….THOMAS L. MISKEL, CO-CHAIR

…………………………………….STEPHEN BURZIO, CO-CHAIR

Transportation Co-Chairperson Stephen Burzio reported to the Board that the committee reviewed and discussed a presentation with representatives for the Department of Transportation on paired pedestrian ramp

9

installations as necessary on the eastside sidewalk of Prospect Park West as part of the construction of the raised median islands.

Motion: by Board Member Richard Bashner, seconded by Board Member Glenn Kelly; Recommendation that the Department of Transportation include paired pedestrian ramp installations as necessary on all sidewalks of Prospect Park West as part of the construction of the raised median islands as well as the inclusion of granite curbs.

Board Member asked what the rule was in regards to the placement of pedestrian ramps when there is no crosswalk. District Manager Hammerman explained that pedestrian ramps are required to be installed where there are traffic control devices. Crosswalks are not traffic control devices. A traffic control device would be a traffic signal, Stop sign or some other element that controls traffic.

Board Member Atkins made a friendly amendment not to move pedestrian cross signals. Board Member Richard Bashner did not except the friendly amendment.

Amendment: by Board Member James Bernard, seconded by Board Member Benjamin Atkins; To stipulate that the Department of Transportation not move the pedestrian cross signals.

Board Member Mark Shames spoke in favor of the motion stating that you are not going to be able to change the behavior of people by move the signals, so why do it. Board Member Levine spoke in favor of the motion.

Board Member Bashner stated that we are mixing issues a little bit. He supported adding signal for bicyclist but DOT rejected it. When you see a green light from the island on the park side you think is okay to walk and you think that the bike will stop because you have a green light and this creates confusion.

Board Member Martha Kenton spoke out against the motion stating that the issue may be one of enforcement and would be interested in seeing something in regards to the issue.

Board Member Norman Cox spoke out against the motion.

Board Member Sones spoke out against the motion stating that moving the signals makes it safer for everyone.

Board Member Turet stated pedestrians, cars and bikes should all have to follow traffic laws, just letting the bikes have the right of way is wrong. When did they become a special category?

Board Member Lalli spoke out against the motion stating that the signals should be moved because it would make it easier to get across the street.

Board Member Glenn Kelly spoke out against the motion stating that moving the lights will increase pedestrian safety.

Board Member Thompson stated that moving the signals is a waste of taxpayer’s money.

Board Member Bernard stated that there is no reason to move the signals. When bikers see the signals, they perceive that pedestrians will be there which makes them more cautious and in effect makes it safer for pedestrians.

Board Member Reilly spoke out against the motion.

VOTE: 19 YEAS, 12 NAYS, 1 ABSTENTION
