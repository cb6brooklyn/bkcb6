CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……..………………………...……..………….....…...DANIEL M. KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel M. Kummer began his report by stating that while we had listed as our first order of business tonight the 2011 Youth Acknowledgement Awards, this year we did not receive any nominations for awards. He therefore asked that our Youth Service Committee Chairs meet with our District Manager – perhaps over the summer – to review our procedures to see how we could make sure that we are reaching the right people so that we can continue to provide an annual opportunity to shine a bright light on the positive contributions our young people are making in our district.

Chairperson Kummer asked for a moment of silence for Hope Reichbach, aide to Council Member Steve Levin who died since our last board meeting.

Chairperson Kummer thanked Parks/Recreation/Cultural Affairs Committee Co-Chair Glenn Kelly for representing us at the official groundbreaking ceremony held at the J.J. Byrne Playground at Washington Park yesterday. Construction at the playground started a few weeks ago and is expected to take one-year to complete. To partially offset the temporary loss of this important facility, the Old Stone House has been working with parents in the area to create a robust schedule of programs and activities that will fully utilize the 4 th Street cul- de-sac during daytime hours. They are extending their Play Street application status beyond the school year to include the summertime, and are continuing to work with the local community and merchants on developing weekend events as well. Parking for the community will still be available on the street during overnight hours.

Chairperson Kummer reported that yesterday District Manager Hammerman and I attended a meeting organized and hosted by Council Member Brad Lander with DOT and a representative of Prospect Park to continue our dialogue on the Prospect Park West bike lane plans. The department is exploring options for the raised pedestrian islands and they would like to come out to present their design to us within the next month, possibly at our June Transportation Committee meeting. Last month you may recall that we had been talking to them about opportunities to restore parking in the vicinity, and the resolution from our April meeting had a long list of other items that we had asked them to consider as well. The department appears to be taking our suggestions very seriously. We are expecting to have another conversation with them before our June meeting, and will be pursuing updates on all of the items from our list. Naturally, when the department is ready to come back to our committee we will alert the community.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by advising the Board that before our Executive Committee meeting last night we also received a briefing from representatives for the Whole Foods Market project at 3rd Avenue and 3rd Street. Their representatives advised us that they were expecting to file their application with the Board of Standards and Appeals imminently, which means that we will be hearing the application within the next month. I would like to plan our review so that the full Community Board will get to vote on this at our June general meeting rather than afterward and only have the Executive Committee ratify the Landmarks/Land Use Committee’s recommendation; therefore, I will probably be asking our Landmarks/Land Use Committee to shift their regularly scheduled May meeting to accommodate this schedule.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT……………..................................................…CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that the Department of Transportation (DOT) issued a Request for Proposals (RFP) for private companies to provide a bike share program in New York City. The RFP calls for a private company to bear all the cost and responsibilities for the

2

program during an initial five-year period while sharing revenues with the city, and with no taxpayer funds used for the program’s implementation, upkeep or maintenance. A program could be in place by spring 2012, using the latest in bike share technology to provide secure and convenient bicycles on a 24-hour basis, and at publicly accessible prices. Once the RFP is been awarded, DOT will be engaging the community to solicit more input and feedback.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the Board that the district office would be hosting an informational meeting on May 17th in an effort to better understand the rules and regulations governing restaurants and bars in our district. Representatives from the State Liquor Authority, City agencies and local precincts will be on hand to answer any questions in respect to noise, smoking, backyard usage, garbage and other related issues.

District Manager Hammerman informed the Board that the Carroll Gardens Association is offering Section 8 housing in the Red Hook section of our district. For more information, please contact Lisa at (718) 243-2233.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by reporting that the School Construction Authority will be continuing its restoration to the exterior of Brooklyn Secondary School for Collaborative Studies/PS 146 on Henry Street. The Contractor is planning for an aggressive schedule this summer. Starting May 23rd to June 30th there will be a crane on Henry and Rapelye Streets. The details are as follows:  No street closure  No parking permitted for approximately 200 ft sections on either side of Henry Street from 3pm-11pm (M-F) These 200 ft sections will move each day as they move down Henry Street from Rapelye Street.  Rapelye Street will have no parking on either side of the street in front of the school for two weeks (M- F) 3pm-11pm.

Ann Schaetzel, representing State Assembly Member James Brennan, reminded everyone to attend the May 19th housing forum to discuss issues effecting homeowners, cooperators, renters, and homebuyers from 6pm-8:30pm at Ditmas Junior High School located at 700 Cortelyou Road in Brooklyn.

Tamar Smith, representing Assembly Member Joan Millman, started by introducing the Assemblywoman’s new Chief-of-Staff Anne Stahl. The Assemblywoman is partnering with State Senator Daniel Squadron to present her Annual Senior Resource Fair at St. Francis College, 180 Remsen Street, Brooklyn Heights (Between Court and Clinton Streets), on Friday, June 24th from 11AM-2PM. This free fair will offer seniors the chance to find information and assistance from a host of organizations, which offer programs and services for seniors. These will include the New York City Department for the Aging, Access-A-Ride, Social Security, Reduced Fare Metrocards, the Center for Medicaid and Medicare Services, free blood pressure, hearing and vision tests. She advised the Board that on May 14th the Friends of the Carroll Gardens Library will be sponsoring “Love Your Library Day” book sale to benefit the Carroll Gardens Branch. There will be thousands of books at bargain prices, baked treats for sale and free performances. The Assemblywoman will again be hosting free bone density scanning, courtesy of Long Island College Hospital. The scans can help determine whether you are at risk of osteoporosis.

Oscar Jonas, representing State Senator Velmanette Montgomery, spoke about two programs, which will give youth the opportunity to work this summer. First, the Summer Youth Employment Program (SYEP) which provides youth between the ages of 14 and 24 with summer employment and educational opportunities. For more information, please call 311 or (800) 246-4646. Second, the Summer Young Adult Skills Introduction Program at the Brooklyn Navy Yard. The goal of the program is to place young people directly in private sector work environments and give them the opportunity to learn about the work-place and specific skills needed for certain industries. Students must be at least 18 years old. For more information, please call Annamarie Vilardi at (718) 907-5924. He also stated that the Senator was working on legislation to reform rent regulations. He ended by informing the board that the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance urged taxpayers to take advantage of FreeFile - a program providing free state and federal tax preparation and e- filing services to qualified New York State taxpayers.

Chairperson Kummer recognized State Senator Daniel Squadron who began by stating that he is still petitioning his fellow senators to allow a Senate Democratic ethics reform package to the floor for a vote. Democrats are advocating changes in five major areas: an independent commission to police ethics with neither the Governor nor anyone else able to dominate appointments. He is also calling for a complete ban of tourist helicopters that are a real nuisance to people who live along and enjoy the South Brooklyn waterfront. He invited everyone to a rally on May 15th at Pier 6 in Brooklyn. He thanked the Board for their resolution to the alternative housing committee for the Brooklyn Bridge Park. He ended by stating that something needs to happen before the rent regulation laws expire. He urged everyone to contact their elected officials and ask them to vote extend and/or expand the laws.

Veronica Aveis, representing Congress Member Yvette Clarke announced that all high school students in the 11th Congressional District are eligible to compete in the Congressional District High School Arts Contest. The winner of the contest will have his or her artwork displayed in the U.S. Capitol Building for one year. For more information, please call (718) 287-1142. She also stated that the Congresswoman is working on a Health Fair with the residents of Wyckoff Gardens Houses for sometime in the summer.
