CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……………………………............................…...RICHARD S. BASHNER

Chairperson Richard S. Bashner began his report by reporting to the Board that since our last general meeting in January we’ve had a couple of Board Members resign from the Board, both citing personal reasons – Deborah Buscarello of Carroll Gardens and Nicholas Heyward of Gowanus. I’d like to thank them for their selfless service to our communities and wish them all the best.

Chairperson Bashner announced to the Board that Board Member Joanne Foulke, has decided not to put in for reappointment so tonight may be her last general meeting as a member. Joanne, I wish you well in the new job you recently started and understand how much of an adjustment that is. I’m glad you’re here tonight so that I can thank you, on behalf of myself and the Board, for all the years of service you gave to this Board – as the Co-Chair of the Transportation committee, as a member of our Executive Committee, and as our Elections Committee Chairperson – your place on the Board will not be easily filled.

Chairperson Bashner congratulated District Manager Craig Hammerman who is going to be this year’s recipient of the Mary Ellen Critchlow Award for Service to seniors by Heights and Hills at their annual spring event on May 13th.

Chairperson Bashner gave special thanks to the members of our Landmarks/Land Use Committee who came out on one of the worst weather nights we’ve seen in years, February 25th, the night the major blizzard started. Not only did all 3 of our applicants show up for the meeting, but the committee achieved a quorum and was able to conduct business because these 11 stalwart members took their job seriously.

Chairperson Bashner informed the Board that on March 2nd the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency announced that they were listing the Gowanus Canal on their National Priorities List as a Superfund site. While we don’t know exactly how this will affect all of the other agencies doing work at the Gowanus Canal, including some of the City’s own agencies, the Department of Environmental Protection’s Gowanus Canal Facilities Upgrade Project is proceeding, as is the remediation of the Whole Foods site on 3rd Avenue and 3rd Street. When we learn more about the status of other pending Gowanus Canal items, like the Department of City Planning’s proposed rezoning, and HPD’s Gowanus Green project, we will continue to share news as soon as it becomes available.

Chairperson Bashner introduced Jennifer Posner of the Brooklyn Office of City Planning to make a brief announcement about the updating of its Comprehensive Waterfront Plan which was last done in 1992. Ms. Posner stated that the Department of City Planning is currently preparing a Comprehensive Plan for the over 500 miles of New York City’s waterfront, defined as New York Harbor and its tributaries, creeks and bays. 2

Vision 2020 will build on the original Comprehensive Waterfront Plan, published in 1992, and the city’s experience over the past 18 years in order to set forth a new long range vision for a 21st Century NYC waterfront. Specifically, Vision 2020 will identify key opportunities for improving our waterfront and outline strategies to realize this new vision. Public input will be critical throughout the year-long visioning process. A kick-off citywide public meeting to discuss the process and solicit feedback will be held on: April 8th from 6pm – 8:30pm at Murry Bergtraum High School located at 411 Pearl Street in Manhattan. For more information on how to get involved please visit their website at www.nyc.gov/waterfront.

Chairperson Bashner introduced John Griffin of Census 2010 to make a presentation about the Census. Mr. Griffin stated that the U.S. Census counts every resident in the United States, and is required by the Constitution to take place every 10 years. The 2010 Census will help communities receive more than $400 billion in federal funds each year for things like Hospitals, Job training centers, Schools, Senior centers, Bridges, tunnels and other-public works projects, and Emergency services. The data collected by the census also help determine the number of seats your state has in the U.S. House of Representatives. Starting this month, census forms will be delivered to every residence in the United States and Puerto Rico. When you receive yours, just answer the 10 short questions and then mail the form back in the postage-paid envelope provided. If you don't mail the form back, you may receive a visit from a census taker, who will ask you the questions from the form. The majority of the country will receive English–only materials. Households in areas with high concentrations of Spanish-speaking residents may receive a bilingual (English/Spanish) form. Any personal data you provide is protected under federal law. For more information please visit their website at www.2010census.gov.

Chairperson Bashner encouraged all Board Members to consider attending the next meeting of our Economic/Waterfront/Community Development and Housing Committee at 6:30pm on Monday, March 15th at the 78th Precinct, 65 6th Avenue (between Dean/Bergen Streets) because we are having a special presentation from Council Member Brad Lander on a new online tool his office created to monitor and track stalled development projects in his district, which is located at www.stalleddevelopment.com. We’re hopeful that we can use this opportunity to look at specific sites in our district and follow-up with various housing-related agencies to see how best we can help restart or repurpose of some of the stalled development sites in our district and get them into a more finished and usable state. Stalled development affects all of us, so I would encourage all of you to consider attending and contributing to this important discussion.

Chairperson Bashner ended his report by advising the Board that Board Member Anthony Pugliese mother, Teresa, passed away on February 9, 2010. He extended condolences on behalf of the Board to the Pugliese Family.

severely beaten by five men shouting anti-gay slurs, near Luquer Street and Hamilton Avenue. City Council Speaker Christine C. Quinn and other elected officials as a sign of solidarity and outrage of the attack will be hosting a candle light vigil on March 15th to show support for a 22-year old victim and the 76th Police Precinct.

District Manager Hammerman ended his reported by informing the Board that the Department of Transportation has responded to the district office in regards to their investigations for the installation of various speed humps throughout our district. They are also in the process of investigating Butler Street between Court and Smith Streets and 8th Street between 7th and 8th Avenues for speed humps. Their investigations will take about 90 days to complete.

LANDMARKS/LAND USE COMMITTEE.......................ROBERT LEVINE, LANDMARKS CHAIR

…..…………........PETER FLEMING, LAND USE CHAIR

Landmarks Committee Chairperson Robert Levine reported to the Board that the committee reviewed an application to request a special permit under Section 73-19 of the Zoning Resolution to allow a Use Group 3 charter school with first floor retail in an existing Use Group 16 warehouse in an M1 district at 255 Butler Street.

Motion: by Committee;

Recommendation to disapprove an application submitted to the Board of Standards and Appeals (BSA Cal. No. 327-09-BZ) to request a special permit under Section 73-19 of the Zoning Resolution to allow a Use Group 3 charter school (non-profit school without residential accommodations) with first floor retail in an existing Use Group 16 warehouse in an M1 district at 255 Butler Street (northwest corner Butler/Nevins Streets).

Board Member Judith Thompson asked to what extent is the Board’s approval relevant. Landmarks Committee Chairperson Levine stated that since the Board is advisory, our recommendation will carry the same weight as any other item the Board votes on.

Board Member Mark Kolman stated that this building being located in a heavy traffic area should not be a factor in deciding whether or not we approve this motion. Students all over the City especially along 4 th Avenue have to deal with heavy traffic each and everyday, it is a part of living in a major city. Landmarks Committee Chairperson Levine stated that this is a M1 zone with narrow streets and it seems that they would have a difficult time parking the buses and dropping off or picking up.

Board Member Daniel Kummer stated that it is very difficult to find suitable locations to house schools and if DOT says it’s a safe environment traffic-wise, he does not think we have the expertise to second guess them.

Board Member David Reiss spoke out against the motion stating that he supports putting a charter school in this building.

Board Member Susanne Turet asked what kind of retail will be on the first floor. Landmarks Committee Chairperson Levine stated that we did not know.

Board Member James Bernard spoke out against the motion stating that it is very hard to find a site suitable for a charter school.

Board Member Debra Scotto spoke in favor of the motion stating that this is a horrible location for a school since it is in the middle of an active manufacturing district which will be dangerous to children.

Board Member Betty Lester spoke out against the motion stating that the building has not been used for years and we need sites for future schools.

5

Board Member Jordan Dressler asked if the environmental impacts on the children as they relate to being in a manufacturing zone (i.e., exhaust from other facilities in the area) were discussed. Landmarks Committee Chairperson Levine stated yes.

Board Member Steven Burzio spoke out against the motion stating that all traffic issues should be mitigated before we vote.

Board Member Mark Shames spoke out against the motion.

Board Member Nica Lalli stated that we need to take a look at the numbers of seats in our district because she heard that there are a lot of seats available. She has some concerns with charter schools in general and when they share sites with other DOE schools it does not work, such as the case at P.S.15.

Board Member Glenn Kelly spoke in favor of the motion stating that this area was set aside for manufacturing and is too dangerous because of the commercial business there to place a school there.

VOTE: 20 YEAS, 12 NAY, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED

TIME: 7:55 P.M.

PUBLIC SAFETY/ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION/PERMITS & LICENSES COMMITTEE

………..…………RAYMOND LOHIER, PUBLIC SAFETY CHAIR

…………..…...DANIEL KUMMER, PERMITS/LICENSES CHAIR

...BOB ZUCKERMAN, ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION CHAIR

Environmental Protection Chairperson Bob Zuckerman reported to the Board that the committee reviewed a presentation for an application for a Gowanus Watershed Initiative grant that proposes various Combined Sewer Overflow reduction measures for the Gowanus Canal.

7

Motion: by Committee;

Recommendation to provide a non-exclusive letter of support for an application for a Gowanus Watershed Initiative grant that proposes various Combined Sewer Overflow reduction measures for the Gowanus Canal.

Board Member David Reiss asked if this would be a non-exclusive letter of support. Chairperson Bashner stated yes.

Board Member Mark Shames spoke against the motion stating that this letter of support is inappropriate for the Board to do. We should not be supporting one applicant’s proposal over another.

Board Member Judith Thompson asked how much money is involved exactly. Environmental Protection Chairperson Zuckerman stated that there is $2.9 million to be given out between the Gowanus Canal and the Flushing Bay.

Board Member Elly Spicer spoke out against the motion stating that the Board should not support any specific group. We don’t need to go on record to support anything until we get more specific information.

Amendment: by Board Member Ellie Spicer and seconded by Board Member Mark Shames; Recommendation to only support the concept of given a grant to look at the kinds of issues that may be included in that particular proposal without endorsing a specific proposal over others.

Board Member Lou Sones stated that the letter should be broader and he agrees that we should support the concept of giving grants but not a particular proposal.

Board Member Susanne Turet spoke out against the motion stating that we need more information.

Motion: by Board Member David Reiss, seconded by Board Member Debra Scotto; Recommendation to Recommit.

VOTE: 32 YEAS, 1 NAY, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED
