CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……..………………………...……..………….....…...DANIEL M. KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel M. Kummer began his report by stating that the Borough President has sent out his official letters of appointment to Community Board members. The Chairman welcomed the new additions to the board. Paige Bellenbaum (Councilman Levin’s appointee, Carroll Gardens) needed to be excused from tonight’s meeting, Neena Dutta (Borough President Markowitz appointee, Carroll Gardens), John Heyer (Borough President Markowitz appointee, Columbia Waterfront) our former liaison from the Borough President’s Office, Hildegaard Link (Borough President Markowitz appointee, Park Slope) a longtime member of our Transportation Committee, Charles Pigott (Borough President Markowitz appointee, Park Slope), Benjamin Solotaire (Councilman Lander’s appointee, Park Slope) member of our Youth/Human Services/Education Committee, and Dolly Williams (Councilman Levin’s appointee, Park Slope) former board member but had to resign when she became a City Planning Commissioner. He reminded all Board Members to sign in whenever they attend a meeting, and that they can make an appointment to meet with the District Manager to help them get up-to-speed on their responsibilities and issues.

Chairperson Kummer reported that last month Board Member Barbara Ginsberg chose not to reapply for membership; however, she will remain on our Parks/Recreation/Cultural Affairs Committee. We have also since learned that Lisa Cirando, Anthony Pugliese, and Maria Reca-Heyer have chosen not to reapply for membership. The Chairman took the opportunity to thank them all for their service to the board and our communities.

Chairperson Kummer congratulated our Treasurer Gary Reilly and his wife Mia who celebrated the birth of their first child, Vaughan Marie Reilly early this morning. According to Gary, “Mom and baby are excellent.” Vaughan came in at 7 lbs, 11.6 oz. and 21 inches long. If anyone would like to send a card or note to the Reilly family, District Manager Craig Hammerman has Gary’s address and will make it available on request.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that at a formal merging ceremony last week our Continuum Health Partners Long Island College Hospital formally became part of the SUNY Downstate network. According to all of the new signage immediately posted to signify the change, the hospital is now officially the SUNY Downstate Medical Center’s University Hospital of Brooklyn at Long Island College Hospital. He thanked District Manager Hammerman for attending the ceremony on our behalf and would like to extend a welcome to Dr. John LaRosa, President of SUNY Downstate, and express to him our thanks that our hospital is open for business and continues to serve our community’s medical and health care needs. I’d also like to thank our elected officials – Borough President Markowitz, Council Members Levin and Lander, Assemblywoman Millman, Senator Squadron and Congresswoman Velázquez – whose intervention and leadership were critical and made it possible for us to celebrate this moment.

2

Chairperson Kummer advised the Board that we received a response from Con Edison on the issue related to employee parking at their Third Avenue Yard. In their letter, they reiterated that their primary goal is to no longer provide free parking for employees to help save money for their customers. They are actively promoting the possibility of carpooling, the use of pre-tax transportation spending accounts, and other messages to encourage mass transit options, but they will not commit to continuing to provide parking for their employees, regardless of who pays for it, at this time.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that on June 14th Empire State Development Corporation and Brooklyn Community Boards 2, 6 & 8 will be co-sponsoring a Community Forum on the latest traffic changes that will be taking place near the Atlantic Yards project. These changes were originally due to begin on or around July 15th, but the start date has since been pushed back to on or around July 24th. Sam Schwartz, their traffic consultant, will be on hand to make the presentation and there will be an opportunity to ask questions and learn more about the plan.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that District Hammerman and I recently met with Senator Eric Adams to talk about overlapping issues of interest that affect our board and the Senator’s district. The Senator was extremely clear that he is strongly in favor of seeing marriage equity legislation in New York State and that he is doing as much as he can on the issue. We have invited him to visit our Public Safety Committee to talk about some of the public safety initiatives he has been working on.

Chairperson Kummer also reported to the Board that they recently met with Emily Lloyd, Tupper Thomas' replacement as the new Administrator for Prospect Park, to discuss our interests in Prospect Park, which is not technically in our district, but is certainly an important asset and one which affects daily life in the district. We talked about a wide-range of issues – everything from parking around the park to the impact of barbequing and large-scale events on our district. With her varied background in government, having been in charge of transportation, sanitation and environmental agencies, we believe she has the potential to make some great contributions that will enhance our quality of life, firm up Prospect Park as the major regional park attraction that it is, and build upon Tupper’s wonderful legacy.

Chairperson Kummer further reported to the Board that they also met with Michael Cairl, the President of the Park Slope Civic Council to get a better understanding of some of the local issues that the council is dealing with and see how we can best support each other’s goals. The Civic Council has really embraced a proactive livable streets agenda, is continuing to push for a further expansion of the Park Slope Historic District, and has really forged some strong ties with the local merchant community and continues to promote support for local businesses.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by reminding the Board Members that the board will not meet in July or August but will resume its meetings in September. If there is an issue of a time-sensitive nature that arises over the summer then the Executive Committee is empowered to act on behalf of the board according to our by-laws. Should the Executive Committee need to meet, we will make sure that all Board Members are aware of the meeting so they have the option of attending.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT……………..................................................…CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that the Department of Transportation (DOT) has begun to implement the improvements in and around Grand Army Plaza. They will be improving conditions for pedestrians, motorists and cyclists by:  Shortening and improving pedestrian connections, improving traffic movement, and expanding four landscaped pedestrian islands and sidewalks at Grand Army Plaza North. 3

 Adding a new signal and traffic calming markings to improve safety in the inner ring of Grand Army Plaza.  Adding five new crosswalks and expanding landscaped pedestrian islands, improving public space, and filling in a gap in the bicycle network in Grand Army Plaza South.

The implementation Schedule is as follows: Pedestrian safety islands: June-July 2011; Road repaving: August 2011; and Plaza spaces: August 2011. For more information, please visit their website at www.nyc.gov/dot.

District Manager Hammerman advised the Board that DOT would make a presentation at our June 16th Transportation Committee on the preliminary design investigation for the Prospect Park West bicycle lane raised medians. All interested parties should attend.

District Manager Hammerman extended an invitation on behalf of the Fire Department to all Board Members to attend the street co-naming ceremony of Richards/Seabring Streets to “Seven in Heaven Way” on June 11th.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by informing that Board that the Committee Membership Request Form is now available and should be returned ASAP. All appointments are for one-year and will go into effect September 1, 2011.

Alex Delisi, representing State Senator Daniel Squadron stated that on May 15th, along with other local elected officials, the Senator hosted a rally calling on New York City Economic Development Corporation (EDC) to ban tourist helicopter traffic at the Downtown Manhattan Heliport. As a formal follow-up to the rally, he co- sponsored a letter to the President of EDC reiterating the need to end tourist helicopter traffic. He also wrote a letter to the mayor about his proposed closures of three fire companies in his district. He explained in the letter that closing these fire companies is unacceptable, but also expressed his fear that his proposal has more to do with budget negations with the City Council than with questions of public policy. On May 26, he gathered with local elected officials and community members to rally to save Engine Company 205 in Brooklyn Heights. We sent a strong message that we can safely expect the neighborhood's growth to continue, necessitating Engine 205's presence in the community. He is still working diligently to pass the following pieces of legislation: an extension and expansion of rent regulation, marriage equality, and a comprehensive ethics package.

LANDMARKS/LAND USE COMMITTEE.............................ROBERT LEVINE, LANDMARKS CHAIR

…..……..………........PETER FLEMING, LAND USE CHAIR

Land Use Committee Chairperson Peter Fleming reported to the Board that the committee reviewed an application for a variance for the Whole Food Market store at 172-220 3rd Street aka 214 3rd Street.

Motion: by Committee;

Recommendation to approve an application submitted to the Board of Standards and Appeals (BSA Calendar No. 66-11-BZ) for a variance of Section 42-00 of the Zoning Resolution that

5

would permit a Food Store (UG 6) in excess of 10,000 square feet in an M2-1 zoning district at 172-220 3rd Street aka 214 3rd Street (Block 978, Lots 1, 7, 16, 19, 23, 30, 32), block bounded by 3rd Street, 3rd Avenue, 4th Street Basin and Gowanus Canal with the following conditions:  After one year, Whole Foods will do another traffic study with a ¾-mile radius and Whole Foods agree to the expense of any mitigation.  A closing hour of 10pm.  Make the 3rd Street and 3rd Avenue walls include windows and be treated as storefronts as shown in the blueprints.

Board Member Ben Atkins asked why they were given a year for a traffic study. Land Use Committee Chairperson Fleming stated that the traffic study that they did, showed minimal impact and the committee felt it necessary to do another after one year as we had done with IKEA.

Board Member Judith Thompson stated that she had a concern about the heat conditions in/on the parking lot.

Board Member Hildegaard Link stated that we should not support their traffic study since the area they study was so small. The study area should expand to include the surrounding communities. In addition, we should ask Whole Foods to sell more than 10% of products produced from local resources. Land Use Committee Chairperson Fleming stated that Whole Foods was not able to speak to that issue.

Board Member Glenn Kelly spoke out against the motion stating that there was plenty of discussion on the building design and that Whole Foods created their own hardship.

Board Member Elizabeth Shipley stated that Whole Foods could get more than 10% of the produce from local sources but would not hold it against them.

Board Member Robert Levine stated that Whole Foods did nothing improper, even though they brought the property knowing it was zoned as Manufacturing. Therefore, the hardship on them was not self-imposed.

Board Member Steven Burzio asked if they were still trying to get LEED Green Building Certification. Land Use Committee Chairperson Fleming stated yes.

Board Member Dolly Williams asked if they were committed to hiring and doing business locally during the construction stage. In addition, was this property in the IBZ zone? Land Use Committee Chairperson Fleming stated yes.

Board Member Lou Sones has two problems with this project, they did an inadequate traffic study and the inconsistency in standards of properties in the IBZ zones based on what neighborhoods you’re in. In Red Hook, you can’t build but here you can.

Board Member John Heyer asked if there would be public access to the canal. Land Use Committee Chairperson Fleming stated that access would be limited to the entrance of the store.

Board Member Rick Luftglass spoke in favor of the motion stating that we should focus on the jobs this development will create and make sure they are quality jobs with good benefits.

Board Member Hildegaard Link stated that we should ask Whole Foods to do some food processing on site.

VOTE: 31 YEAS, 3 NAYS, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED
