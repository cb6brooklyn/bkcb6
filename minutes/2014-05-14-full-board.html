CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by informing the Board that he has received a resignation from Board Member Elizabeth Shipley, who recently moved outside of our district. Citing escalating work demands coupled with this change of residence, she felt that the best option for her was to resign at this time. With Elizabeth’s resignation we currently have 43 Board Members. While membership terms technically expire on March 31st for those of us who are up for reappointment, anyone who is currently serving as a board member automatically continues to serve until new appointments are made which could happen at any time.

Chairperson Kummer reminded the Board that each year in June we begin the process of applying for membership to our standing committees for the yearlong term that begins on September 1st. At our Executive Committee meeting on Monday night it was the consensus of the committee that our existing committee structure is working well so it will remain in place for the 2014-2015 session. The district office will send out new Committee Membership Request forms next month, which I urge you all to complete and return to them as quickly as possible. Even if you think we have your resume on file, please provide us with your most up-to- date version, and especially let us know if any of your personal contact information like phone numbers or email addresses has changed. Please keep in mind that I do make every effort to give board members their top choices when possible.

Chairperson Kummer also reported to the Board that the Executive Committee reviewed and discussed changes which we believe will strengthen our public notification and outreach standards and rules to ensure that the public has convenient and ample access to information about actions coming before us which may impact the community. Some changes to our requirements for applicants will be incorporated into our existing rules over the summer, which will be in place in time for our new committee session in September. One special new policy was also discussed and adopted which I will share with you now. As you all are very well aware, our Permits & Licenses Committee often has a significant caseload dealing with all of the new applicants coming to us for liquor licenses and sidewalk café permits. While we have not routinely reviewed liquor license renewals, we have been automatically reviewing sidewalk café permit renewals which, by and large, have not surfaced any problems with these operations or produced any further changes to any conditions we may have imposed when we first reviewed them as new applications. Therefore, we will now apply the same rule for sidewalk café permit renewals that we use for liquor license renewals – unless the district office has a reason to put a

2

sidewalk café permit renewal before the committee, which would typically be because of a persistent, chronic and founded complaint history, or if the community petitions us to hear it, we will no longer be automatically hearing sidewalk café permit renewal applications before our Permits & Licenses Committee. I believe this will allow us to continue to intervene in situations where reviews are warranted, and not bog down our committee and board with mindless automatic reviews that have not added value to the City’s overall permitting process.

Chairperson Kummer ended by invited all interested parties to the Transportation Committee meeting on May 15th, where they will be discussing with our elected officials an act to amend the vehicle and traffic law and administrative code of the City of New York, in relation to reducing the speed limit to 20 miles per hour.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that he was invited to be a presenter at a Brownfield conference that was given by New Partners for Community Revitalization (NPCR). In the discussion he proposed an idea that the City as well as the State could create a dedicated earmark of funding stream for environmental remediation programs from environmental violation penalties that they collect, especially since the theme of the conference was how the future of brownfield funding is uncertain in New York State. NPCR stated that they would include the concept in their final report to Albany.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that we have submitted a draft of the Gowanus Brownfields Opportunity Area (BOA) report to the State which is currently under review. As soon as they sign off on it, we will share the final report more broadly. One of the recommendations of the report is to study the feasibility of creating an Industrial Business Improvement District (iBID) somewhere in the Gowanus. We have applied for discretionary funds from our local city council members so we can bring together the area’s indigenous manufacturers, artist, designers and business advocates to see whether an iBID is something that would suit their needs.

District Manager Hammerman informed the board that DOT will be resurfacing the segments of Bond Street that needs it before they install the new bike lanes and markings. This is in response to what the board had recommended in its conditional approval of the bike lanes. The other section of Bond Street that does not need resurfacing, the bike lanes will be installed imminently. DOT has agreed to come to our June Transportation Committee meeting to make a presentation and discuss how roadways get resurfaced. They are also in the process of installing pervious pavers at the 4th Street cul-de-sac, per the plan they presented to Transportation Committee last year.

District Manager Hammerman reported that the Department of Sanitation is in the process of distributing the new brown bins for the organic waste recycling collection that will begin next month in portions of our district. They will begin with the east/west streets from Union to 15th Streets and move from the canal to Prospect Park west to the east, before doing the north/south Avenues. We will be including reminders the Gowanus and Park Slope communities.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that the Department of Design and Construction will be finishing their work on Henry Street in Cobble Hill and will restore the trench by the end of the week. The sidewalks are being done by National Grid, who is still working on Warren Street, and will be completed by the end of June. Any bluestone or tinted concrete that was disturbed will be restored or replaced in-kind.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by announcing that the Reconstruction of Columbia Street is nearly completed. The contractor at this point is working on the various odds-and-ends or “punch list” items. If anyone sees anything that they believe should have been done, let the district office know as soon as possible so we can see if it can be added to the list before the City signs-off and releases the final payment. As a byproduct of their completion the community and city agencies have already begun the construction of new, interim open

Chairperson Kummer recognized Council Member Brad Lander

Councilman Lander stated that today the City Council passed a rules reform bill that would make the council more transparent in its work and responsive to New Yorkers. The reforms will be:  Take the politics out of Member Items by allocating discretionary funding for Council Districts on a fair and objective basis.  Ensure fair consideration of legislation.  Further empower chairs in the running of their committees, giving them greater control over committee hearings and staffing.  Dedicate a greater share of Council resources to enabling Members to provide effective services to the public.  Establish a formal procedure by which Members can file a grievance with the Rules Committee if they believe the City Council rules are not being followed.

The Councilman ended by praising the Legal Aid Society for its law suit on behalf of the Prospect Park Residence against Haysha Deitsch and the New York State Department of Health. He also invited everyone to the next Bridging Gowanus meeting on June 12th.
