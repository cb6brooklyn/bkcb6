CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by welcoming our newest Community Board members who are joining us tonight for the first time. He also thanked Nica Lalli, Monique Verret and Neena Dutta for their service as they have all opted not to reapply for membership and are therefore no longer serving as board members. Tonight our ranks swell back up to 50 members, we are at capacity and we have 7 new members. Our new members are: Christina Calabrese (BP) of Cobble Hill; Vilma Heramia (39), Executive Director of the Carroll Gardens Association, Inc. and member of our Economic/Waterfront/Community Development & Housing committee; Ariel Krasnow (BP) of Carroll Gardens who works at the Old American Can Factory in Gowanus and serves on our Economic/Waterfront/Community Development & Housing committee; David Mazzuca (BP) of Cobble Hill; Philip Mindlin (33) of Carroll Gardens who also serves on our Economic/Waterfront/Community Development & Housing committee; Mike Racioppo (BP) of Carroll Gardens; and, returning as a Board Member after a hiatus of several years, Jeff Strabone (39) of Cobble Hill. If you are new to the board, I’d suggest that you may want to consider scheduling an appointment with District Manager Craig Hammerman who could answer any questions you might have and quickly get you acclimated.

Chairperson Kummer reminded the Board that application forms for Committee Membership have been sent out and are due next week. It is generally my preference to give our members their top choices so please complete and return them to the district office as quickly as possible. I will rely on the information you provide in your application to make appointments to our committees. Appointments are for a one-year term effective September 1st. We would like to get appointment letters out in early August.

Chairperson Kummer highlighted a couple of important items that will be discussed at our next Transportation/Public Safety committee meeting. At that meeting we will have a brief presentation from representatives for Forest City Ratner Companies on the effectiveness of their Traffic Demand Management initiatives. If you would like to learn more about the actual traffic impacts associated with the opening of the Barclays Center, see the data about how people are arriving and leaving, and have any ideas to offer on how things can be further improved, this would be a good meeting to attend. Also on the agenda that evening, we will have a representative from the Office of Emergency Management who will be briefing us on disaster preparation. They will be unveiling their newest hurricane evacuation plans which are especially relevant to our district since we regularly experience flooding in our Gowanus and Red Hook waterfront communities. The committee meeting will take place at 6:30pm on Thursday, June 20th at the 78th Police Precinct, 65 Sixth Avenue (between Bergen/Dean Streets).

Chairperson Kummer congratulated Board Member Yvonne Girela who was married on May 22nd. On behalf of the board he wished her many years of good health and happiness.

Chairperson Kummer thanked Board Members Gary Reilly, Mark Shames, Glenn Kelly, Lou Sones and Joseph Porcelli who recently met in a working session with myself, District Manager Craig Hammerman and Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch, to review our existing Liquor License Application Questionnaire. We got some really good, thoughtful feedback and our district office is in the process of revising our forms. The information we collect from applicants, and the format we receive the information in, should help our committee in their review of applications in the future which, ultimately, means a more efficient and effective process.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by reminding the board that in the event that we must act on matters of a time-sensitive nature, our Executive Committee is empowered to meet and act on behalf of the full board to ratify any recommendations from our committees. We do this so that the board can take July and August off as

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that the Department of Transportation has advised us that they plan to move ahead with the Mill Street extension across Hamilton Avenue, using DOT in-house resources in late summer/early fall of this year. This project was presented to the Community Board 6 Transportation Committee and was unanimously supported by the full Board at our December 14, 2011 meeting. The project will create much needed connectivity between Red Hook and Carroll Gardens and improve access between the Red Hook Houses and the closest subway station at Smith and 9th Streets. It will enable MTA-NYCT to streamline its B61 and B57 bus routes between Red Hook and the Smith/9th Station. They will prepare a one-page project flyer for distribution shortly before implementation which will be widely shared once received.

District Manager Hammerman informed the board of a new service offered by the United States Postal Service at the Cadman Plaza Station, it’s called “gopost” - a new, convenient way to receive and ship packages. GoPost units are automated, secured, self-service parcel lockers placed in convenient locations where you can pick up or ship packages 24/7 at no additional cost to the consumer. For more information go to https://tools.usps.com/go/EPLAction!input.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the Board that the New York City Department of Health and Mental Hygiene and the CUNY School of Public Health will randomly select 3,000 New Yorkers in all five boroughs to participate in the New York City Health and Nutrition Examination Survey or NYC HANES. We will ask them to answer questions about their health and to take a brief physical exam that includes free lab tests. Each individual will receive a stipend of $100.00. When NYC HANES was first conducted in 2004, they found out how many New Yorkers were at risk for heart disease, how many had diabetes, and how many of those with diabetes were being effectively treated. They also learned that New Yorkers were breathing in more second- hand smoke than other people around the country, and that some groups had higher levels of mercury in their blood. For more information got to http://www.nyc.gov/html/doh/html/data/nyc-hanes.shtml.

District Manager Hammerman advised the Board that Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch and I met with Kevin Jeffrey, Brooklyn Borough Commissioner of the Parks Department, Captain Jeffrey Schiff, Commanding Officer of the 76th Precinct, and Captain Carlos Valdez, Commanding Officer of PSA1 to review and discuss last year’s problems with closing down some special events in our local parks. We reviewed the protocols and discussed strategies to prevent recurrences of last year’s difficulties. They will be meeting with some of the organizers of large events to help them understand that we would like to see well-run events that build and unite the community rather than poorly run events that can divide them.

District Manager Hammerman also reported that he and other District Managers met with new Brooklyn Borough Assistant Chief Owen J. Monaghan to discuss the importance of the NYPD use of technology/social 3

media tools to stay linked with the community. He also had the opportunity to share with the group the innovative program started by the commander of the 76th Precinct, “Spot It to Secure it” in which a team of four police officers will patrol a selected area of the 76th Precinct and look for parked cars where iPhones, iPads, laptops, or wrapped packages are left on the seats. The officers will snap a picture and mail it to the car's owner with a form letter. They will also be checking apartment building doors to see if they are locked. If one is, the landlord will be getting a letter from the NYPD encouraging him to make sure the door is locked at all time.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by informing the Board that Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch and I met with Council Member Brad Lander, representative from Assembly Member Joan Millman’s office, Parks Department, DDC and DOT to discuss the transition of the land that is currently being used as a staging area on west side of Columbia Street between Kane and Degraw Streets by the Gowanus Facilities Upgrade project and the Reconstruction of Columbia Street project. Since both of those projects are coming to a close we would like to reintroduce a conversation that began several years ago to transition the property into a public park with open space where the community could enjoy.

Jim Vogel, representing State Senator Velmanette Montgomery stated that the senator’s office continues to work on New York State Bill S.1379/A.2305 which would stop police and prosecutors from using possession of condoms as evidence of prostitution. Currently, police and courts can use the fact that a person has or is carrying condoms to prove that they are engaging in criminal activity. Sex workers report that they are more likely to be arrested if they carry condoms. Police officers regularly confiscate condoms from people they allege are engaged in prostitution to use as evidence against them at trial. As a result people are hesitant to carry condoms to protect themselves and others; for fear that it will lead to arrest or be held against them in court. Sound public health policy would encourage condom use by eliminating the fear that carrying a condom will be used against you by police or in a court of law. On June 9th the State Senator join Assemblyman David I. Weprin for a Press Conference on the steps of New York City Hall to support the passing of Senate Bill S.2490 /Assembly Bill A.909, which would allow adult adoptees to request from the Department of Health a noncertified copy of an original birth certificate and a medical history form, if available. Under current NYS law, adoptees are unable to view their birth certificates and are thus unable to trace any family medical conditions or other background information that could greatly affect them and their children. He ended by stating that last year the Senator voiced her strong opposition to the granting of an unlimited liquor license for a proposed club called Kemistry Lounge. This application has been revived and she recently sent a letter to the State Liquor Authority (along with Assemblywoman Joan Millman and Councilman Stephen Levin) reiterating there opposition. She will be supporting her constituents concerns and will be speaking against this application at the upcoming SLA hearing.

Zeeshan Ott, representing State Senator Daniel Squadron, stated that the Senator is continuing to push for the passage of GENDA (S.195/A.4226) legislation which would prohibit discrimination against transgender New Yorkers when it comes to housing, employment, credit, and public accommodations, while expanding New York's hate crimes law to include crimes against transgender individuals.

She then stated that the development is totally incongruous with the existing R6B zoning. Furthermore, she pointed out that the developer insistence that the Rock and Roll Playhouse will be a theatre around children's programing is inconsistent with what he told her group. She further stated that in a meeting in early May the developer spoke of the evening programing in terms of a late night music venue featuring jazz and possibly other music seven days a week for those 21 and older. It would also feature a full bar with a last call around 1:30 AM, 2 AM on week-ends. She ended by asking the board to please keep in mind all the facts, to examine this closely, and to consider what she has told them when he comes before the board.
