1. DOT: Public E-Bike Charging (PEC) overview

Presenter: Derek Walker, Brooklyn Borough Commissioner's Office Deck: PEC Overview 2026 09 17 BK CB 6.pdf, sent 9/17

Since 2022, lithium-ion batteries in NYC have resulted in more than:

- 900 fires

- 400 injuries

- 30 deaths

DOT pilot tested battery-swapping cabinets in 2024 with delivery workers who rely on e-bikes (100+ delivery workers)

- Popular, improved fire safety, reduced at-home charging

- Willingness to pay for service

Solution: $3 million in state grant funding to put 25 battery charging cabinets

Bike swapping cabinets are a safer alternative to charge e-bike batteries

- Certified to UL Standards, weather- and theft-proof with fire safety features

- Vendor will charge fee and be responsible for site maintenance and cleanliness

Sites were identified where the most deliveries are taking place and took into account congregation, clear path, fire safety, and appearance issues

CB6 locations:

- Dean St and 5th Ave, NW Corner

- Court St and 1st Pl, NE Corner

- Replace curbside charger given Court Street redesign no longer makes sense (with bike lane)

Board questions/comments:

1. Are we racing against private sector with this (e.g., do private charging docks already exist)?

1. Answer: No public charging stations, there is one at city hall that was done by the parks department. Private companies do this already and we want to expand their network, not compete with them, but increase how many we can get on the ground.

2. Excited about the Court Street location because the current charging station is useless.

3. Will there be a DOT representative to remove an old battery and if it's replaced is it given to that particular delivery person for nothing?

1. Answer: You do not have to be a delivery person to be a part of this program. If there's a bad battery, the company will take the battery out and replace it. No one owns anything, but it's a rental service.

4. Were delivery drivers consulted about type of battery and issue of spare batteries?

1. Answer: We see some people already using these vendors and they do have two batteries. We want to continue to expand the network so they don't need to rely on the extra spare at home. Eventually, there will be so many they won't have to charge a spare at home.

5. Can we add more benches and shelters near the charging areas?

1. Answer: Will take this into consideration.

6. Can business owners opt in for battery stations out front of their business?

1. Answer: We do have places where business owners can opt in, will send follow-up information on this.

7. Are all batteries the same for e-bikes?

1. Answer: This program, they will be universal batteries in the sense that all batteries for this program will be the same. But not everyone with e-bikes can use this program. We skewed this toward supporting delivery workers and the primary type of bike they use.

Community member questions/comments:

1. Is there any way to use existing citibike infrastructure?

1. Answer: Trying to colocate this as much as possible and use existing infrastructure as much as possible.

3. Low Traffic Neighborhoods [notes from August meeting]

6th Ave cut-through traffic; school-based pilot approach. - Catherine Vaughan (absent) proposed the blocks west of PS 282 and west of PS 39, using the Open Plans Neighborhood Needs Assessment Dashboard

From Catherine Vaughan - I've had a few conversations with David Scatterday, who built this amazing new tool for Open Plans, the Neighborhood Needs Assessment Dashboard. I highly recommend you check it out—it allows you to look at Census tracts across the city and filter on different variables to determine where a low-traffic neighborhood (or any types of street redesigns/treatments, etc.) might be needed. For example, you might toggle the filters to look for neighborhoods under a certain median income, over a certain pollutant level, with a higher than X percentage of people who commute by public transit, etc. — and it will show you which specific neighborhoods fit these criteria. You can overlay data on schools, crashes, Community Boards, etc.

I gave him some context on the 6th Ave cut-through traffic, and we used his tool to identify a few areas where we might pilot LTNs or, at the least, a few diverters. We should speak more with Open Plans about this (curious what Jackson Chabot said, for example), but David agreed that our suggestion of an incremental approach, starting with schools because they're likely to get the most buy-in, makes sense.

Here's a map I created with the tool, with two neighborhoods highlighted as places to potentially divert the cut-through traffic on 6th Ave: the blocks west of PS 282, and the blocks west of PS 39. The filters I used included:

- Median income <$200K

- % residents who primarily use public transit to get to work >50% (note: these %s are low / don't add to 100 because non-commuters are counted)

- % residents who primarily use car to get to work <20% (note: these %s are low / don't add to 100 because non-commuters are counted)

- Car-free households >40%

- PM2.5 mean pollution concentration level (μg/m³) >6.8
