IV. Committee Reports

A. Landmarks, Land Use & Housing 46 Nelson Street David Briggs presented a ULURP application for 46 Nelson St., featuring 96 units (10 studios, 56 1-bedrooms, 18 2-bedrooms, and 12 3-bedrooms), with 25% affordable units at 80% AMI. The Landmarks, Land Use & Housing Committee asked the developer (456 Nelson LLC) to reduce the number of studios and add additional 2- and 3-bedrooms for families. David presented a Motion to Approve the application by 46 Nelson LLC for a zoning map amendment from M1-1 to M1-2A/R7A (MX-5) and M1-1 to M1-2A/R6A (MX-5), along with a zoning text amendment to map MIH (Appendix F) to facilitate the development of the new 125 SF 7-story plus cellar mixed-use residential and commercial building containing 108 dwelling units at the site, with the following conditions: 1. Applicant agrees that building will be constructed as currently designed 2. Reduce the number of studios and add more 2- and 3-bedrooms. Board Member Daniel Kummer expressed concern that the massing of the building was not contextual with the neighborhood. 46 Nelson LLC offered to shift the bulk to the center of the building within the R7A envelope they're seeking. Daniel added a friendly amendment to the first condition in the motion above: "except as necessary to incorporate setbacks or other shifts of bulk in order to reduce massing of the building." The board discussed whether setbacks would reduce the number of units in the building, and Daniel and David explained that the building could go higher, or the developer could reduce the total number of units. David explained that the developer’s unit mix was based on a marketing study conducted. David Briggs abstained for cause; all others approved the motion with the friendly amendment above.

B. The Business Permits and Licenses Committee

520 Henry Street Mandi Spishak-Thomas presented this application at the site of the current Bar Bruno, which is passing from one owner to another. One community member came forward in favor of this license during the committee meeting. A motion from the board to approve an on-premises liquor license passed unanimously. 424 Van Brunt Street Mandi presented this application from the cocktail bar Pony's NYC (no outdoor space). The site is currently a yoga studio. A motion from the board to approve an on-premises liquor license passed unanimously. 291 Nevins Street Mandi presented this application next to the existing bar Liar Liar, currently the site of Tuned bike shop. The bar would be called Hidden Rivers BK, and would feature weekly rotating brewery spotlights. Katie Bishop expressed opposition to the proposal and explained that the landlord (who is proposing the new bar) is not renewing the bike shop’s lease due to the success of the neighboring bar Liar Liar. Tong Galaxy asked the board if we could consider that factor in this vote. Mike explained that there are better mechanisms to influence this type of action by a landlord. He explained that the Community Board is essentially a function of the State Liquor Authority, that it is unlikely that SLA would act on a recommendation to deny the license, and that denying this application would not compel the landlord to renew the lease of the bike shop. He noted that Board Member Brandon West is working with Assemblymember Emily Gallagher on commercial rent control legislation, which could be impactful in cases like this. Daniel Kummer suggested considering how the bar owners behave towards the community when the license comes before the Community Board for renewal? Daniel and Jules Block noted that the Community Board’s role was to create a relationship with future businesses. Jules noted that the landlord is at least putting a small business into its own space, as opposed to setting rent so high that a small business could not afford. A motion from the board to approve an on-premises liquor license passed, with Katie Bishop voting against the motion and Pauline Blake, Alan Mukamal, and Tony Melone abstaining. 56 5th Avenue Mandi presented this new wing bar from the owners of Uncle Barry’s. Pauline asked about the occupancy of the proposed bar and Mike confirmed that it would have 15 tables with a maximum occupancy of 74 people. A motion from the board to approve an on-premises liquor license passed unanimously minus one no vote from Pauline. Finally, Mandi noted that a representative of the NYC Department of Small Business Services (SBS) would join the Business Affairs & Licenses meeting on January 26th.

Finally, he shared that the MTA Mobile Sales Bus (formerly known as the “Metrocard Bus”) will be at his office at 416 7th Avenue on January 29th from 10:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. ● Councilmember Hanif: CM Hanif opened by acknowledging the tragic shooting of Renee Nicole Goode by Immigrations and Customs Enforcement (ICE) during an enforcement operation in Minneapolis. She announced that the City Council would be holding its first meetings of the year this week and noted that she is still awaiting her committee assignments. She reviewed progressive legislation that she would like to advance in the new term, including the NYC Trust Act, which would create a private right of action when agencies collaborate with ICE. The bill received a hearing, but did not pass in the last session. She also cited the Ban the Scan Bill, which has received renewed interest since the revelation that Wegmans is using biometric scanning technology in its stores. Her bill would prohibit the use of this technology in housing and businesses. She is also advancing another bill to share information about fertility care options. CM Hanif is excited to advance bike safety in her district as well, and is collaborating with Good Neighbors of Park Slope on a forthcoming traffic safety PSA. She is also promoting a bill that would require Citibike and other micromobility providers to post traffic laws. The Councilmember expressed approval of the appointment of Professor James DeFillippis (Board Member, Columbia Street Waterfront Association) to the Brooklyn Marine Terminal Development Corporation, as she believes that the Columbia Street Waterfront District was not previously represented in the effort. Finally, she provided an update on the closure of the Concrete Recycling at the BMT Facility: materials are being removed out and NYCDOT expects to fully exit the site by the week of January 19th. CM Hanif cited this closure as an excellent example of community power pushing for transparency and clean air. ● Councilmember Avilés: The Councilmember announced free tax services in her district office in Sunset Park in partnership with Brooklyn Grow and suggested that interested individuals call her office at (718) 439-9012 to register.
