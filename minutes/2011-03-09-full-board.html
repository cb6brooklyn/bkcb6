CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……..………………………...……..………….....…...DANIEL M. KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel M. Kummer began his report by reporting to the Board that last month he announced that we received a resignation from Board Member Greg O’Connell, who cited competing demands on his time given the work he’s been doing in Upstate New York as a reason to leave the Community Board. This month he introduced our newest Community Board member, Greg O’Connell, Jr. Greg has been a member of our Youth/Human Services/Education Committee and an active member of our Red Hook and Columbia Waterfront communities. That brings our total roster to 46 members.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that he recently met with Council Member Brad Lander and with State Senator Daniel Squadron as part of his introductory round of meetings with our elected officials. He has a meeting scheduled with Council Member Sara Gonzalez next week.

Chairperson Kummer thanked our Environmental Protection Committee Chair Gary Reilly for representing us at a Press Conference held yesterday by Council Member Lander in reaction to a lawsuit that was filed by the group Neighbors for Better Bike Lanes against the City over the Prospect Park West bike lanes.

Chairperson Kummer reported that our Transportation and Public Safety/Environmental Protection/Permits & Licenses Committees are conducting a joint public hearing tomorrow night and just wanted to remind our board members that since we, as board members, have an opportunity to discuss motions that come out of committees – and I am expecting one to come out of our committees on the DOT proposed modifications to the bike lanes – that it has been our practice to not testify at our own public hearings. The Chair mentioned this because he knows that some of our board members are very passionate about this issue, but since our hearing was set up to hear from the public and we will get to fully debate any motions coming out of the committees, it’s generally not good practice for us to take time away from the public at our own hearings. Also, since we are not anticipating that our committees will have time after tomorrow evening’s hearing to convene and formulate a recommendation, any board members who are interested in participating in the actual discussion and development of any motions are invited to attend the March 17th Transportation Committee meeting, which will be a joint meeting with Public Safety. We should have the meeting location secured tomorrow and the district office will send an email to all board members inviting them to come.

Chairperson Kummer and Council Member Lander announced that the Department of Education would not be pursuing a temporary co-location of the Brooklyn Prospect Charter School at the existing P.S. 32 and New Horizons School building at 317 Hoyt Street in our district.

Chairperson Kummer advised that Board that our district office learned today, after approaching the Department for the Aging, that the department is targeting 2 more senior centers in our district for closure, specifically, the Red Hook Senior Center at 6 Wolcott Street and the Wyckoff Gardens Senior Center at 280 Wyckoff Street. We really do not have any details yet since DFTA did not notify us of the closure, but we have 2

arranged to have a representative from DFTA appear at our next Youth/Human Services/Education Committee meeting scheduled for March 23rd. Last year, DFTA targeted the Gowanus Senior Center for closure but Council Member Levin was able to intervene and found funding to keep it open for another year. Therefore, basically, if DFTA’s plans were to go ahead, that would mean the simultaneous loss of all three centers by the end of this fiscal year, or June 30th. The loss of the Red Hook Senior Center would be particularly devastating to the Red Hook community, as the seniors would have very limited options for finding accessible programs and services elsewhere. Since I will be meeting with Council Member Gonzalez next week, we will make sure this matter is addressed and I would encourage anyone interested in learning more about this to attend our committee’s upcoming meeting on March 23rd.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by reminding our board members that each year at our May general meeting we present our annual Youth Acknowledgement Awards to youth in our district who either have been exemplary role models or have distinguished themselves through volunteer service in the community. I am sure everyone in this room knows of a young person – it could be a neighbor’s child, someone you know from your congregation, or someone you know from work or school. This is our opportunity to shine a bright light on our future generation of leaders and take time to acknowledge them for their efforts, which, ultimately, we all benefit from. Won’t you please consider nominating a candidate, or passing along our nomination form to someone else who could nominate a candidate, so we can continue to show our young people how very much we value their contributions to our community.

__________________________________________________________________________________________ DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT……………..................................................…CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by announcing that the New York City Economic Development Corporation (NYCEDC) will be issuing a request for proposals (RFP) for vessel tie-up space, or wharfage, at the Atlantic Basin at Pier 11 with some access also from Pier 12. We don’t have an exact date when the RFP will be released, but it should be within the next couple of weeks. When it goes public we will make sure to let everyone know.

District Manager Hammerman informed the Board that at the last Gowanus Community Advisory Group (CAG) meeting, they received an update from the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) on the Remedial Investigation report they issued back in February. It was a very interesting and technical type of meeting but for the individuals who participated they got a lot out of it especially an opportunity to interact directly with the EPA Project Manager. He posed a question regarding water classification as an issue the board and the community is very concerned about. EPA stated that the Federal Clean Water Act sets water classification and each State is responsible for carrying out the mandate of the act. Anyone who is interested in this issue should attend CAG’s water quality subcommittee, which is open to the public. For more information, please contact District Manager Hammerman at the district office.

District Manager Hammerman advised the Board that he attended a meeting organized by Borough President Marty Markowitz office and chaired by Council Member Brad Lander to address lingering issues about the Carroll Gardens courtyards. There were representatives from DOB, 76th Precinct, DOT, City Planning and Department of Consumer Affairs addressing issues such as whether or not the front areaways are City property or City easements. DOB has declared that those front areaways are in fact city property. We have asked the City’s Corporation Council to send us a letter stating such. Another issue is curb cuts; they will continue to work together in the near future on the best way to address old and new cuts in terms of enforcement. Lastly, what do you do about corner properties where there are public easements on properties that are behind private gates. These commercial usages are a special class. He believes that these discussions are healthy and long overdue and hopes they will help to put more predictability in what we should expect in these public spaces.

Chairperson Kummer recognized the representatives of the elected officials.

Tamar Smith, representing Assembly Member Joan Millman reported to the Board that in 2010, Governor David Paterson, the State Department of Health and all concerned stakeholders, agreed to apply funding from the State Health Care Efficiency and Affordability Law (HEAL) to allow LICH to remain open and a merger with SUNY Downstate Medical Center to go forward. In February 2011, word suddenly came from the office of Governor Andrew Cuomo that there might be a delay in releasing these HEALS funds, which would have spelled the nearly immediate closure of LICH. After meeting with various elected officials the governor has announced that he would commit to honor $62 million dollars in HEAL grants, which will keep LICH open and allow it to merge with SUNY Downstate. As Chair of the Assembly Election Law Committee, the Assemblywoman has introduced legislation to protect the voting rights for members of the military. Current 4

law does not allow adequate time for the finalization and transmission of military ballots to overseas servicemen and women – as well as Americans living abroad – by boards of elections, making it problematic for these individuals to cast their votes. The new legislation would increase the period between the governor’s proclamation of a special election and the date of that election by 40 days at a minimum. She ended by announcing a new program sponsored by St. Francis College, “The Future Women In Science” (FWIS) program. This program helps girls build confidence and get the support they need to pursue a career in science. FWIS begins March 26 with a six-week program on Saturdays at St. Francis where students will engage in interactive experiments. At the end of the program, girls will be able to share what they have learned at a career fair. Space is limited but there are still slots available for high school students who want to join the program.

Anthony Alexis, representing Council Member Sara Gonzalez stated that the Council Member was introducing legislation that would amend the administrative code of the city of New York, in relation to requiring snow removal from bus shelters, making it the responsibility of DOT.
