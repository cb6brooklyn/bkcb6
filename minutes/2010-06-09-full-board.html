CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……………………………...……..………….....…...RICHARD S. BASHNER

Chairperson Richard S. Bashner began his report by informing the Board that appointment letters were sent out by the Borough President’s office and we have a number of new Community Board members joining us tonight for the first time. I’d like to welcome them all and as I call their name ask them to stand up or wave so we can put a face to the name:  Noel Berk-Rauch, from Cobble Hill was appointed by the Borough President;  Linda Blyer, former Executive Director of Families First, Cobble Hill’s neighborhood parenting center and our neighbor at 250 Baltic Street, was appointed by the Borough President;  Norman Cox, President of the Columbia Waterfront Neighborhood Association, is joining us as a Council Member Lander recommended appointee;  Harriet Hughes, the President of the 572 Warren Street Tenants Association was appointed at the recommendation of Council Member Levin;  Martha Kenton from Park Slope is a Borough President appointee;  Elizabeth Shipley from Park Slope is a Council Member Lander appointee;  Gary Reilly, a member of the Carroll Gardens Neighborhood Association’s Executive Committee who has also been actively serving on our Transportation and PS/EP/PL Committee for several years, was appointed at the recommendation of Council Member Lander  Roger Rigolli, one of our Columbia Street business owners has been reappointed to the Board at the recommendation of Council Member Lander.

Chairperson Bashner reported to the Board that Bob Zuckerman, the new Executive Director of the Lower East Side Business Improvement District has submitted his resignation from the board because of the demands of his new position.

2

Chairperson Bashner congratulated Board Member Glenn Kelly, and his wife, neighborhood blogger Katia Kelly, for celebrating their 25th wedding anniversary on June 1st.

Chairperson Bashner thanked all the Board and committee members who turned out this past Monday evening to attend our Executive Committee meeting to hear the Department of City Planning’s briefing on their Comprehensive Waterfront Plan revision initiative. It was refreshing to see an agency coming to us to seek out our policy ideas in a forward-thinking way, and I know they took away a lot of notes based on our feedback. The project director actually called our District Manager the following day to say that they are happy to return to us sometime in the early fall so that they can reflect to us what they’ve heard, and what changes to the Comprehensive Waterfront Plan they are proposing, so we can offer another round of feedback when they have something more definitive to say.

Chairperson Bashner advised the Board that we received a letter from Commissioner Mattingly at the Administration for Children’s Services which was presumably sent in response to our April 27 th letter although he does not reference it directly. The Commissioner’s letter was sent in accordance with the City’s Fair Share criteria, and it goes on to outline how the agency determined the list of 15 day care centers to close citywide, and goes on to describe the measures the agency is taking to notify and help parents who relied on the services provided by those centers. The Commissioner does invite us to submit further comment to him by June 18 th, and we will be following up on his offer accordingly by reiterating the points he has not as yet responded to at a minimum. A copy of his letter will be posted on our website and circulated to the board via email. Anyone else wishing a hard copy of the letter can request it of the district office.

Chairperson Bashner ended his report by informing the Board that tonight will be the last meeting for Jamarah Harris, our representative from the Mayor’s Community Affairs Unit, who will be leaving New York City the end of this month. He thanked her for all her support and guidance over the past few months.

Tamar Smith representing Assembly Member Joan Millman stated that the Assemblywomen will be working hard to prevent the closings of the Day Care and senior facilities in her district. Oscar Jonas, representing State Senator Velmanette Montgomery. Oscar reported to the Board that the Senator was introducing various juvenile justice bills that would allow for restoration of parental rights in certain cases. Currently children of incarcerated parents go into the Foster Care System. Most of these children stay in the system until they “age out”. This bill will create a way for petitioning and granting of renewed parental rights with the best interest of the child in mind. She is also introducing a bill that would require development statewide to meet economic analysis standards to ensure “smart” development.

__________________________________________________________________________________________ LANDMARKS/LAND USE COMMITTEE.............................ROBERT LEVINE, LANDMARKS CHAIR

…..……..………........PETER FLEMING, LAND USE CHAIR

Land Use Committee Chairperson Peter Fleming reported to the Board that the committee reviewed a ULURP application proposing a citywide text amendment to the Zoning Resolution which will create regulations to allow car share vehicles to park in off-street accessory garages and lots and in public parking facilities in all zoning districts.

Motion: by Board Member Glenn Kelly, seconded by Board Member Steven Burzio; Recommendation to approve a ULURP application (#N100284 ZRY, referred to as the "Car Share Text Amendment") submitted by the Department of City Planning proposing a citywide

5

text amendment to the Zoning Resolution which will create regulations to allow car share vehicles to park in off-street accessory garages and lots and in public parking facilities in all zoning districts.

Board Member Glenn Kelly stated that the concern for the Borough President was that this amendment would not take away the required 20% of parking for new and existing developments.

Board Member Robert Levine stated that there is no need to distinguish between existing or new we need to add to the motion that they only can use the spots above the required 20% by law.

Board Member Monique Verret stated that we are trying to maintain the minimum residential parking required by law and if an existing building has a surplus after the required 20% than the could use those extra spots for the car share program.

Board Member Benjamin Atkins stated that we are unnecessarily complicating this issue. City Planning proposal is meant to legalize something that is already happening. He supports the motion as long as it does not affect the required residential parking of 20%.

Friendly Amendment: To support City Planning proposal to allow car sharing in parking facilities up to 20% of the spots available with the proviso that such spots does not take away the number of residential spots allocated by law.

The maker of the motion accepted the friendly amendment.

Board Member Mark Shames stated that we do not have the specific language from the Borough President on this issue. We should support it subject to that proposal.

Board Member Suzanne Turet spoke out against the motion stating that it is unfair to the community.

Board Member Lou Sones asked if there will be contracts with Car Share Companies. Chairperson Bashner stated yes.

Board Member Gary Reilly spoke in favor of the motion stating that our concerns are misguided because this proposal will reduce the number of cars on the streets.

VOTE: 25 YEAS, 4 NAY, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED

TIME: 8:12 P.M.

Land Use Committee Chairperson Fleming further reported to the Board that the committee discussed asking the Landmarks Commission to take into account the aesthetic of the existing area architecture for such altered use rather than attempting to mimic the prior use, when to do so would not enhance the area aesthetic, add to the value of the historic district, or preserve distinctive architecture.

Motion: by Committee;

Write a letter to the Landmarks Commission stating that when they review an application that contemplates an "as of right" change of use, such as is the case with the application for a Certificate of Appropriateness for 232 Court Street, they take into account the aesthetic of the existing area architecture for such altered use rather than attempting to mimic the prior use, when to do so would not enhance the area aesthetic, add to the value of the historic district, or preserve distinctive architecture.
