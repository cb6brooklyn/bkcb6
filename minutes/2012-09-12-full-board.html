CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by welcoming everyone back from the summer hiatus.

Chairperson Kummer announced that everyone should have received their committee appointment letters. If you did not, or if there was a problem with your appointment, please let District Manager Craig Hammerman or myself know immediately. I’m very pleased to announce that we have some additions to our Executive Committee. Paige Bellenbaum will now be co-chairing our Human Services Committee with Benjamin Solotaire; Norman Cox will be chairing our Housing Committee; Sayar Lonial will be chairing our Public Safety Committee, and; Mark Shames returns to our Executive Committee as the Chair of our Environmental Protection Committee. I want to thank these board members for agreeing to take on these new roles and I look forward to working with them, and all of you, as this busy year ahead of us progresses.

Chairperson Kummer reported that Board Member Peter Fleming will continue in his role as Chair of our Election Committee. Letters will be sent to every board member outlining our election procedures. We do have term limits, as officers are only permitted to run for a maximum of four consecutive one-year terms in the same office. All of our current officers are eligible to run again for their seats in 2013.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that in a series of meetings over the last few months we have been working with IKEA, the South Brooklyn Industrial Development Corporation, and the Red Hook Initiative, to facilitate stronger ties between IKEA and Red Hook so that more people can take advantage of potential employment opportunities locally. We’re pleased to hear that these groups have continued to meet on their own and are finding ways to work together and support one another. I’d especially like to thank our Economic Development Chair Rick Luftglass and Human Services Co-Chair Benjamin Solotaire for making time to attend these meetings and encouraging us to play a more pro-active role in this area.

Chairperson Kummer advised that he attended the State Liquor Authority’s (SLA) hearing on June 20th on the Barclays Arena and presented testimony on behalf of our board. As a follow-up, District Manager Hammerman attended the SLA hearing on August 29th where they closed the record and decided the matter. The applicant represented that they had met the conditions that we had listed in our conditional approval of their application, with the exception of the post-event service in the VIP areas of the arena which we had not considered as part of our review process. SLA Chairman Dennis Rosen questioned the applicant to see whether an absolute service cut-off time of 10pm, as suggested by some members of the community, would be harmful to the business model for the arena. The applicant stated that it would. The Chairman then asked how many events they expected would last past midnight and would, therefore require the need for them to be able to serve alcohol up to 2am. The Chairman ultimately suggested that instead of being allowed to serve up to 2am, a condition that we had proposed and the operators had accepted, that instead they only be permitted to serve up to 1am since it would not infringe on any of the planned events at this time. The Chairman also promised that the SLA would 2

be open to reexamining this restriction in as little as 6 months should it proves to be too constrictive to their operation.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the board that the NYPD arrested six alleged drug dealers and arrested six others in a joint sweep at the Red Hook Houses on Wednesday, September 5th, wrapping up a six-month investigation titled "Operation Red Dawn" by the NYPD and the Brooklyn District Attorney's Office. As a follow-up Kings County District Attorney Charles J. Hynes and NYPD Housing Bureau Chief Joanne Jaffe will be meeting residents and taking questions at a town hall-style meeting at the Red Hook Community Justice Center on September 19th.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the Board that Mayor Michael Bloomberg on behalf of Police Commissioner Ray Kelly has sent a letter to City Council Speaker Christine Quinn asking the City Council to formally change the boundaries of the 78th Police Precinct to include the entire Atlantic Yards development area and also gives it jurisdiction over the area between Vanderbilt Avenue and Flatbush Avenue, from Hanson Place in Fort Greene, all the way to (and including) Plaza Street East. There will be a broader and more robust opportunity to review all of this next year because that is the decennial review of the New York City Community District boundaries.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that the Department of Transportation has determined that a speed hump on President Street between Smith and Court Streets is a good candidate for installation, and denied a speed hump on Hoyt Street between President and Carroll Streets.

District Manager Hammerman informed the board of an emergency condition at 440 5th Avenue in the Park Slope section of our district. Department of Buildings has ordered the owner to remedy this situation by demolition the building.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the Board that on July 26th a lightning bolt struck the 16-foot northwest pinnacle of Christ Church causing the stone structure to collapse and bringing down part of its scaffolding. The debris struck Richard Schwartz, a prosecutor in the State Attorney General’s office. He was pronounced dead at SUNY Downstate University Hospital at the Long Island College Hospital. Due to an unstable building, the following streets will be closed indefinitely to both vehicular and pedestrian traffic: Clinton Street from Degraw Street to Kane Street; Kane Street from Strong Place to Court Street.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that the district office is now wired for wireless. We are also working on other forms of technology upgrades in the office before the end of the year.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by inviting everyone to the next The Economic/Waterfront/Community Development & Housing Committee Meeting on October 15th where we will hear a presentation by the consultants that the Friends of Brooklyn CB6, Inc. have hired to perform our Gowanus Brownfield Opportunity Area (BOA) Study. The BOA program provides municipalities and community-based organizations with assistance, up to 90 percent of the eligible project costs, to complete revitalization plans and implementation strategies for areas or communities affected by the presence of Brownfield sites, and site assessments for strategic Brownfield sites.
