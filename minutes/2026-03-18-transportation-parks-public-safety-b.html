CB6 Transportation/Parks/Infrastructure/Safety Committee

Agenda & Notes

6:30 p.m., 250 Baltic Street and on Zoom

March 18, 2026

COMMITTEE UPDATES, RECAP

Last meeting recap (transportation): CB6 Slow Zone resolution passed the full board. Full text: Recognizing CB6's commitment to safer streets and support for Sammy's Law, we recommend that NYC DOT institute a district-wide 20 MPH slow zone on all applicable streets, and adjust signal timing to reflect the new limit, with camera enforcement. We also call for adoption of a citywide speed limit of 20 mph on all applicable streets.

Mayor Mamdani just announced 15 mph slow zones around many NYC schools.

We spoke with Megan Flynn and Anthony Drummond of the MTA: asked for updates on the Flatbush busway plan; discussed ways to increase R train service, especially on weekends.

We discussed the initial responses to the Street Safety Issue Report form.

PARKS

Presentation from the Prospect Park Alliance. Questions: Why are new restrooms so expensive? The administration is looking into less expensive options. In redevelopment of the Vale, is it possible for the wood to be re-used by artists? Some trees are diseased but we'll look into it. How can we preserve the legacy of the Vale for the Queer community? Call for future agenda items.

TRANSPORTATION

Introduction, DOT Liaison Derek Walker. Updates on current DOT projects and priorities.

CB6 priorities: Columbia St. Waterfront traffic safety improvements; bus bulb-out at President St? Carroll St. Bridge pedestrianization? Completing the protected bike lane network — Bergen right turn to Smith is a challenge, eliminating parking on that block of Smith could help; could add protected lane on Bond St, or slow zone, complete street.

Julia flagged that potholes are not getting filled properly. Loading zones. Ask for updated timeline for the 5th Ave redesign.

Motion: "CB6 supports returning parking enforcement to the DOT, where it was before 1996, so it can get the attention it needs, and asks our elected officials to support this. This would allow the NYPD to focus on crime reduction."
