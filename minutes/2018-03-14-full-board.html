CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT…………………………………………......SAYAR LONIAL, BOARD CHAIR

Chairperson Lonial opened his remarks by commenting on Congresswoman Nydia Velazquez’s to-be-proposed Superfund Enhancement Act of 2018, an effort to reinstate the Superfund Tax on the chemical and oil industries in order to finance tax deductions, a disaster loan program, and economic injury loans for small businesses, home owners, and renters who were forced to move because of their proximity to a Superfund Site.

Mr. Lonial reported that Ms. Velazquez’s office sent a representative to the Economics, Waterfront, Community Development and Housing committee to report on the proposed bill and solicit the Board’s support. The Economics, Waterfront, Community Development and Housing committee ultimately decided not to vote one way or another, but to provide questions and feedback to Ms. Velazquez. From that feedback, the bill was altered to include home owners and renters in the tax deduction and disaster loan section of the bill.

Having provided strong feedback to the Congresswoman’s office and receiving appropriate responses, the Chair sought a motion from the floor to support the Superfund Enhancement Act of 2018.

Motion: By Board Member Peter Fleming; seconded by Board Member Dolly Williams To support the Superfund Enhancement Act of 2018.

VOTE: 29 YEAS, 0 NAYS, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED UNANIMOUSLY

TIME: 6:53 P.M.

Mr. Lonial continued his report by bringing forth another item for consideration, a proposed resolution addressed to the Mayor and the NYC Department of Transportation asking that swift action be taken to analyze and implement necessary safety measures along the Ninth Street corridor in Park Slope, paying particular attention to improving safety for pedestrians.

Motion: By Board Member Eric McClure; seconded by Board Member Peter Fleming To communicate to the Office of the Mayor and the NYC Department of Transportation a request to analyze and implement necessary safety measures along the Ninth Street corridor in Park Slope.

Discussion ensued, with various Board members requesting that various specifics be either added to or subtracted from the request. Some wished to see specific demands on truck traffic; others pointed out the economic importance of truck traffic.

After several minutes of back and forth, parties agreed that the original intent of the motion was to express a specific demand on the relevant government agencies, and that further considerations could be taken up by the Transportation & Public Safety committee in future meetings.
