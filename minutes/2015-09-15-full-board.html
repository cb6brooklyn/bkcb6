CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……………………………………………………….................GARY REILLY

Chairperson Gary Reilly began his report by welcoming the Board back and introducing the new Assistant District Manager, Ty Beatty. Chairperson Reilly reviewed Mr. Beatty’s background and qualifications, welcomed Mr. Beatty to the BrooklynCB6 team, and asked that Board members not hesitate to contact Mr. Beatty regarding municipal service delivery or general complaints, pointing out that there is no change to the contact information for the Assistant District Manager.

Chairperson Reilly informed the Board that the Borough President has re-appointed former member Deborah Buscarello to the board. Ms. Buscarello’s appointment brings Board membership up to forty-nine.

Chairperson Reilly thanked the Board for submitting their committee requests and stated that the district office sent out all committee appointment letters. Chairperson Reilly reported that the district office is currently updating all records and list servers to reflect relevant changes, and that the district office expects these to be up-to-date shortly. The Chair underscored his efforts to give members their preferred assignments, but explained that this is not always possible. He invited Board members with concerns or questions over their assignments to discuss them with him after the meeting.

Chairperson Reilly apprised the Board of the actions taken by the Landmarks Committee and Executive Committee over the summer months: • The Landmarks Committee reviewed and conditionally supported the proposed redevelopment of the Polhemus Building at 350 Henry Street, one of the former Long Island College Hospital buildings that is located within the Cobble Hill Historic District. The Chair outlined an expectation that the project would be coming before the Board in the form of a ULURP. Board Member Jerry Armer pointed out that the Landmarks Preservation Commission voted to conditionally approve the proposal, with a recommendation that the rear balconies be removed. • Chairperson Reilly thanked the district office, specifically District Manager Hammerman, as well as Board Member Jerry Armer, the Cobble Hill Association, and Councilmember Brad Lander’s office for gathering community input in preparation of an expected ULURP action for the former Long Island College Hospital site. • The Landmarks Committee also reviewed and conditionally supported a plan to redevelop the Pavilion Theater in Park Slope and the adjacent building, which used to be the Circles Restaurant. This redevelopment is also likely to come before the Board as a ULURP action this fall. • The Executive Committee weighed in on the proposed new school within the Atlantic Yards project, supporting Brooklyn CB8’s position that the school should be a middle school and that it should be situated in a way that prevents student exposure to dangerous traffic. Chairperson Reilly thanked the Youth/Human Services/Education Committee for their work regarding this matter.

Chairperson Reilly asked Board Member Peter Fleming to serve as the Election Committee Chairperson, tasked with overseeing the annual process for the election of Board officers. Chairperson Reilly pointed out that all our current officers are eligible for re-election. Chairperson Reilly stated that elections would be held in December if needed.

Chairperson Reilly stated that the annual Holiday Party planning process has begun. He reminded the Board that the Holiday Party doubles as a fundraiser for BrooklynCB6’s sister charity, Friends of Brooklyn Community Board 6, Inc. Mr. Reilly asked that any members interested in volunteering to help with the planning contact the district office.

Chairperson Reilly reminded attendees that Primary Day is September 10, and that poll sites are open from 6am to 9pm. The Chair noted that many of the election districts within BrooklynCB6 had only a single judicial race on the ballot, but underscored the importance of exercising the right to vote regardless. He pointed out that the General Election is November 3, and that there is still time to register to vote if one has not yet done so.

Chairperson Reilly informed the board that the Department of Sanitation has been invited to present on the expansion of the organics waste collection program to the Environmental Protection Committee. Two of our five sanitation sectors (in central and south Park Slope) have been participating since spring 2014, and the success of the pilot program has prompted the Department of Sanitation to expand the organics collection districtwide. Mailers will be sent to each household with more information, and the Environmental Protection Committee will host the presentation on September 28 at 6:30pm, location to be determined.

Chairperson Reilly concluded his report by stating that the typically scheduled November general board meeting falls on Veterans Day, so the meeting will be moved up one day to Tuesday, November 10 to avoid conflict with the city Holiday.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by welcoming the board back from their summer recess.

District Manager Hammerman offered updates on two on-going projects at the district office: • Notes for the by-laws update are still being assembled for distribution and review. • The addition of Mr. Beatty to the staff has prompted a return to the project of tracking liquor license applications and complaints, given his experience with liquor licenses in his previous position at Manhattan CB5.

District Manager Hammerman briefed the board on his visit with Council Member Menchaca to the PSA 1 satellite station at 80 Dwight Street in Red Hook. He commented that the frontage of the office is daunting and unwelcoming, and that once inside the situation is just as bad. The station is made up of former storage rooms that has operating as a makeshift police station. The work conditions are oppressive for personnel and uninviting to the general public. Brooklyn CB6 has for several years made Capital budget requests for beautification of the station, and the visit made it evident that more than a superficial beautification was needed. As such, we will add more substance to our previous Capital budget request for the upcoming cycle.

District Manager Hammerman announced that Coffey Park in Red Hook reopened at the end of June in time for the full summer season. The beautification project in Coffey Park took fourteen months to complete, and include new planting areas, new pathways, and other new features were added. Coffey Park was reopened in time to host National Crime Night Out, as well as several other festivities over the course of the summer.

3

District Manager Hammerman informed the board that the Red Hook ballfields were found to have elevated lead levels resulting from a prior industrial tenant adjacent to the site. The Environment Protection Agency forced the City to close a few ballfields for the next few years until the lead levels are mitigated via a cleanup. Hammerman noted that the EPA determined no evident risk of exposure for those who have used the ballfields in the past (or may continue to do so), as there were never any exposure pathways for the elevated lead levels, which were buried beneath the soil. The method for the cleanup and how will be paid for is still subject to discussion and further meetings. The Parks Department has held several meetings on the matter to date, and all user groups have been directly notified of the situation. The next Parks Department-sponsored meeting for the community relevant to this matter is scheduled for September 21.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by outlining the district office’s work on the Red Hook Community MicroGrid project, a grant project on-which the office is partnering with the Red Hook New York Rising CRP committee, a technical consultant called Smarter Grid Solutions, and a global financing consultant called IMG Rebel. Hammerman explained that a microgrid is a solar collection and distribution setup that can be isolated or “islanded” from the general electrical grid and provide power to a specific sector if needed. This project is important both as a resiliency measure and a sustainability concept. The current project, a Phase I feasibility study, is made possible by a $100,000 state grant. If the study shows viability, the project would move to apply for a Phase II grant, which would provide $1,000,000 for an engineering study. The potential third phase of the project offers $7,000,000 to successful competitors toward construction of a microgrid. So far, meetings have been held with elected officials and the community, while meetings with several other major stakeholders in the Red Hook community, including NYCHA and the Port Authority, are planned for the near future.

Chairperson Reilly recognized Council Member Brad Lander, who welcomed the board back from their summer recess and remarked on his excitement for the addition of the board’s high school students. Council Member Lander also introduced Susie Charlop, his new Director of Constituent Services.

Council Member Lander updated the board on his office’s work and the work of the council at large: • City Council passed the new budget in June, with some new items relevant to CB6 including o Cafeteria improvement at PS 124 o Gym renovation at the Brooklyn New School o Making Union Street and 9th Street greener o The Park Slope Library Garden o Open space projects in Gowanus, including two playgrounds, were fully funded, but will be rolled out in two phases o Seven Public Art projects in Gowanus were also funded • Citi Bike is expanding in the district and the Council Member’s office is looking for more feedback on possible dock locations, despite the initial feedback phase now being closed. • The Council Member thanked District Manager Hammerman for his work on Solarize BrooklynCB6 and co-sponsoring the town hall on climate change. The Council Member was impressed with the turnout for the town hall.

Council Member Lander remarked that expansion of ferry service from Brooklyn to Governors Island is still in the works. The budget is allowing for expanded service on some keys days (yet to be determined) throughout the year. The Council Member stated that there is a commitment from the city to work Governors Island more closely into the ferry service plans.

Council Member Lander thanked CB6 for hosting the public hearing regarding the redevelopment of the Pavilion Theater while noting his optimism about the project moving forward, particularly because of the commitment to keeping a movie theater use on site. Council Member Lander closed his remarks by again thanking CB6 for the continued partnership regarding the on-going review of the Long Island College Hospital 4

site. The Council Member remarked that the current proposal is wildly out-of-scale, as is the developer’s proposal for a rezoning action. The Council Member stressed that this is an on-going, evolving process, and that working together with the developer is the preferred approach. LANDMARKS/LAND USE COMMITTEE…………………ROBERT LEVINE, CHAIR, LANDMARKS

Landmarks Chair Robert Levine reported that the committee reviewed a Certificate of Appropriateness application at 929 President Street.

Motion: by Board Member Peter Fleming, seconded by Board Member Suzanne Turet; Recommendation to conditionally approve a Certificate of Appropriateness application submitted to the Landmarks Preservation Commission for the replacement of a 2-story rear yard extension, addition of a partial new story and new roof decks at 929 President Street (between 8th Avenue/Prospect Park West), Park Slope Historic District.

6

Board Members Daniel Kummer, Mark Shames, Joe Anne Brown, and Matthew Silverman all stated that because this is the only residence on the block with a rooftop deck, they will vote against the motion.

Board Member Ariel Krasnow requested clarification on the view of the deck from the street, and Landmarks Chair Robert Levine noted that the deck would only be visible from certain points on the street level.

Board Member Sayar Lonial clarified that the Landmarks rules state that visibility from anywhere along the street is in violation of the requirements. Conversation ensued, resulting in the explanation that the railing of the rooftop deck is, in fact, visible from certain points along the street. As such, it is agreed that the original motion should be voted down and an amended version, withdrawing support for the rooftop decks, should then be immediately considered.

VOTE: 4 YEAS, 26 NAYS, 3 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION DEFEATED
