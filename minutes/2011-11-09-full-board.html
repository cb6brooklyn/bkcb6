CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……..………………………...……..………….....…...DANIEL M. KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel M. Kummer began his report by reporting to the Board that we have received resignations from Board Members Terry Alexander and Yvonne Roman. Board Member Alexander has gone through a job transition and while he does not anticipate having the time to commit as a Board Member and Co-Chair of our Public Safety Committee, he has asked to remain on the committee as an active member. Board Member Roman has become more active in her church and Wednesday nights are a conflict for her so before her attendance became an issue for us she has voluntarily resigned. Chairman Kummer thanked them both for their selfless service to the Community Board for these past few years.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that the Borough President has appointed several new Members to our Board. He welcomed Sister Rosanna Cervone of the Koinonia John the Baptist Catholic order, appointed by the Borough President, who has been attending our meetings regularly since coming to Visitation Parish in Red Hook, and also Dominique Bravo of Park Slope and Sayar Lonial of Carroll Gardens who were both appointed at the recommendation of Council Member Brad Lander. We hope they find their new role as a Board Member fulfilling and would suggest that each of them reach out to our District Manager Craig Hammerman to help get them situated.

Chairperson Kummer thanked our Parks Committee Co-Chair Glenn Kelly, District Manager Craig Hammerman, and Board Member Yvonne Girela for attending an on-site meeting with our Parks Borough Commissioner Kevin Jeffrey and other members of the surrounding community of Ennis Park, which is located between 2nd-3rd Avenues and 11th-12th Streets, to talk about the landscaping in the park and several other park usage issues. Some of the issues involved the need for more Capital investment in the park, and some of them are more operational issues, so Ennis Park should appear on a future Parks Committee agenda for further discussion.

Chairperson Kummer also thanked our Environmental Protection Committee Chair Gary Reilly and District Manager Hammerman for meeting with DEP Commissioner Carter Strickland to discuss several issues of concern to our district. Toward the top of that list were the chronic flooding conditions that continue to affect us in the 3rd-4th Avenue corridors and along 9th Street by the canal. As a piece of good news, DEP has developed a Capital project to address the 3rd-4th Avenue flooding conditions. We are in the process of arranging a briefing to our committee so we, and the public, will have an opportunity to learn more about this important project which we have been advocating for through the City’s budget process for many years.

Chairperson Kummer further thanked our Transportation Committee Co-Chair Tom Miskel and District Manager Hammerman for attending the last meeting of the Atlantic Yards Service Cabinet meeting last week. I’m told that the long expected Transit Demand Analysis report will be issued sometime in the first quarter of 2012 so we will have a chance to understand more deeply exactly how the Barclay Arena developers intend to induce their patrons to visit the arena while minimizing the use of personal vehicles. It is our hope that the 2

Transit Demand Analysis will prompt a broader discussion with the City as well, on what we can do to improve traffic conditions in general around the 4th Avenue, Flatbush Avenue, and Atlantic Avenue triangle, which has historically been notoriously bad well before there was ever any talk of putting in an arena there.

Chairperson Kummer advised the Board that we would be adding an item to tonight’s agenda - To support the preliminary designs for the Sponge Park located at the 2nd Street dead end on the west side of the Gowanus Canal. This item was laid over from a previous meeting.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by informing the Board that Transportation Chairperson Tom Miskel and I attended a ceremony at Grand Army Plaza to celebrate the new renovations to the Plaza that have benefited drivers, pedestrians and bikers alike. Kudos to DOT for the improvements!

Tamar Smith, representing Assembly Member Joan Millman, reported to the Board that The Senior Citizen Rental Increase Exemption, called SCRIE, is an invaluable program for seniors (62 and older) living in New York City. SCRIE helps many stay in their apartments by freezing their rents at current levels while property owners receive tax abatements by the city to compensate for a loss of rental income. Increasing problems in the program's administration, currently run by the New York City Department of Finance, led to a planning meeting held recently by City and State elected officials to discuss strategies for improving the SCRIE program and increasing outreach and education efforts so that the many thousands of seniors who are eligible for the program actually are enrolled. One change is that the administration of SCRIE should be moved back to the Department for the Aging, where there were better outreach efforts and more sensitivity to the needs of seniors. The City Council will be proposing legislation to set up a program where a SCRIE specialist would be dedicated to one senior center in each Community Board in the City. This person would be responsible for education, outreach, assisting seniors with the application process, recertification, and working through any problems that arise. To be eligible for SCRIE you must be:  62 years or older  Live in a rent regulated apartment  Your income for the year must be $29,000 or below  You must spend at least 1/3 of your monthly income on rent

If you think you, a family member or a friend might be eligible, call 311 for a SCRIE application.

Chairperson Kummer recognized City Council Member Steven Levin who introduces a new staff person Rachel Fuentes, who will be in charge of community issues in his district office. The Councilman also stated that he would be sponsoring a bill that would to give Albany a green light to consider Residential Parking Permits in and around downtown Brooklyn. The bill, sponsored by Assemblywoman Joan Millman, would set aside spots for residents on their streets while leaving at least 20 percent of spaces for visitors and non-permit parking. Streets with commercial and retail space, and spots with existing meters and other restrictions would not be eligible for permitting — providing protection for small businesses and their customers. He invited everyone to attend upcoming hearings of the City Council’s Education Committee in relation to the notification of information related to polychlorinated biphenyls (pcbs) in schools and a local law requiring the reporting of information related to polychlorinated biphenyls (pcbs). He ended by stating that he also attended the ceremony at Grand Army Plaza and asked if anyone had any comments or suggestion about the changes they should contact his office at (718) 643-6620.

Dan Wiley, representing Congress Member Nydia M. Velázquez stated that the Congresswoman has introduced her section 3 legislation H.R.3254 - Affordable Communities Employment Act of 2011 that pertains to low income residents who live in HUD-subsidized housing. The act would amend the Housing and Urban Development Act of 1968 to ensure access to employment opportunities for low-income persons. She also introduced H.R. 3114, which would provide disadvantaged youth with the opportunity to serve their communities and acquire valuable job skills. The bill, the “Civic Corps Jobs Act of 2011,” would reduce recidivism among juvenile offenders by empowering them to serve their communities and acquire training for future careers.
