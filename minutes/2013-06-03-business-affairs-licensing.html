Update from representatives for National Grid on the company’s response to the Environmental Protection Agency’s Proposed Remedial Action Plan for the Gowanus Canal.

Q. Your delay on pilot test – what will result from that? A. Best practices will be discovered to deal with the input that the remediation will have on the community. The pilot test will assist to come up with flex.

Q. Hildegaard Link: Most of the test sites are extremely polluted – why only test hot spots if you have precision tools. There are various materials for sheeting – why metal not wood? A. If metal - sheets have an “interlock” to prevent seepage of tar into protected area. Wood sheeting is not strong enough to install without excavation. * Concentration of “Reach 3” is where hot spots would be targeted. Reaches 1 & 2 – these are greater contaminants so there would not be a reason to spot test.

Q. Glenn Kelly: Why not fix all the bulkheads?

A. Property owners are each responsible for their own bulkheads. Coordinating them is unlikely to occur within the next few years.

Q. Mark Shames: if you put in the sheeting and the bulkheads get destroyed, will the PRAP become responsible for replacing them? A. Good question! The parties responsible would assess the bulkheads to see if remediation is possible without destroying the bulkheads.
