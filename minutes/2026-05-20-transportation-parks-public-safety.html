CB6 TPPS Committee Agenda+Notes May 20

2026

● 6:45 Harvey LaRou from DOT presents plans for Woodhull St., discussion (we'll also

ask about plans for the Carroll St. Bridge)

○ Context

■ Many different projects happening in close proximity: BMT, Red Hook

Resiliency, Coastal Resiliency Project, Brooklyn Greenway

○ Issues

■ Weaving: Merging across several lanes

■ Speeding: Three lanes coming off BQE and entering turnaround back onto

BQE

■ Pedestrian crossing: 9 blocks between crossings

■ Right turn onto Richards St: High volume of vehicles making illegal right

turn (141 over 24 hours)

○ Proposed several treatments

■ One-way conversions, a potential new signal, curb extensions, lane

reductions, traffic delineators

○ Comments and questions

■ Concerns about speeding on Hicks Street from Union Street, on Woodhull

St, and the turns from highway onto Columbia

■ Requests for hardening to protect pedestrians, reduce parking

■ Safety at midblock crossings

■ Parking will not be reduced as a result of lane reductions

■ Estimated fall 2026

○ Pedestrianizing Carroll St bridge

■ DOT needs a formal letter from BK CB6

○ Motion to approve all changes presented by DOT on both sides of Hamilton St, with

the addition of hawk signals at the midblock crossings

■ Motion passes unanimously, except for Leroy who abstained for cause

● 7:15 Discussion with Brit Byrd from BP Reynoso's office about a potential Low Traffic

Neighborhood (LTN) in Gowanus

○ Brit Byrd, Senior Planner

■ Supports collaboration with BBP office in terms of exchanging data and

building proposal

■ Noted that advocacy is key to unblock LTNs; DOT does not have an LTN

program in place

○ Background on LTNs

■ LTNs appeal due to systemwide approach on neighborhood level

■ Evidence of success in Berkeley and London

■ 5th Ave concept: Different “nodes” at 3rd and Union, 9th St, Barclays

Center, Prospect Park

■ Note from Doug: BK CB6 has voted in support of an LTN in Gowanus

○ Goal: Figure out ideal boundaries of an LTN in Gowanus

■ This is a purely conceptual exercise

○ Potential boundaries

■ 9th St for southern boundary

○ Comments and questions

■ Transit-forward treatments are possible (e.g., Fulton St)

■ Concerns about congestion on Third Street, but pushback due to Whole

Foods parking lot

● LTNs can have some cutthrough streets

■ Caution against deciding boundaries without a high-exposure meeting,

especially with more Gowanus residents

■ Reminder that LTN boundary selection will unavoidably cause someone

an inconvenience, should not block the systemic benefits of a boundary

■ Asking about phasing to reduce pain of transition; Brit notes that

Gowanus is a naturally occurring LTN due to the canal

■ Idea to improve feasibility with piecemeal projects (e.g., progressively

targeting chokepoints, such as pedestrianizing Union St)

● 7:40 Discussion of this proposal from Thomas Neff for a redesign of 8th Avenue, and

consideration of a resolution on this

○ Proposal includes creating a protected bicycle lane and a dedicated bus lane,

which both would serve as EMV corridors and be supported by loading/school

drop-off zones

■ Includes a block-by-block breakdown

○ Discussion

■ Committee is generally in agreement

■ Could ask for signal timing

■ Should do more community outreach

■ Request for more research; want to balance between preventing induced

demand and avoiding pushing harms onto less-active communities

■ Reminder that car usage isn’t just from private ownership; delivery and

rideshare are drivers as well

■ Note that bus lane is on the left-side of the street, and that bike lanes can

go in front of a school

○ Resolution

■ Section 1: Low-hanging fruit on 8th Avenue. Reduced speed signal timing

to 15mph; painted curb extensions; leading pedestrian intervals; additional

hardened daylighting; camera enforcement for speed and red lights.

■ Section 2: Study a traffic redesign of 8th Avenue. Including lane

reductions, dedicated bike lanes, additional loading zones, new bus lane

configurations, additional traffic calming, additional pedestrian safety

features

○ Motion passed with two abstentions (Leroy and Pauline Blake)
