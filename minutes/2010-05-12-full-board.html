CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……………………………...……..………….....…...RICHARD S. BASHNER

Chairperson Richard S. Bashner began his report by informing the Board that since our last meeting, we've lost yet another Board Member. Tom Kamber, our former Human Services Committee Chair, citing personal reasons submitted his resignation to the Board. I’d like to thank Tom for his service to our communities and wish him well.

Chairperson Bashner reported to the Board that we have not as yet received a response from Commissioner Mattingly at the Administration for Children’s Services to the resolution we adopted at our meeting last month about the announced closure of 4 day care centers in our district, and it is unclear whether the agency intends to pursue this action in the Executive budget that was recently released by the Mayor. One thing that is certain, however, is that the Executive budget does propose to close 50 senior centers citywide, including the Gowanus Senior Center in our district. Previously, the Commissioner for the Department of Youth and Community Development had also announced that they would be cutting 33 Out-of-School Time (OST) programs (or “afterschool” programs) citywide, including 3 in our district at P.S. 282 run by Imani House, and 2 others at P.S. 27 in Red Hook and P.S. 32 in Gowanus both run by Good Shepherd Services. It is also unclear to us whether these cuts are reflected in the Mayor’s Executive budget. We have written to the Commissioners of both the Department for the Aging, and the Department of Youth and Community Development, and invited them to attend our upcoming Youth/Human Services/Education Committee meeting on May 26th to share their plans with us. Anyone interested in these cuts should consider attending that meeting.

Chairperson Bashner advised the Board that we will have a presentation by the New York City Transit at our Transportation Committee meeting this month to give us a comprehensive look at the cuts that the MTA Board has already approved that will impact our district. We could stand to lose many bus routes from our district, while others may be mashed up and reconfigured, and at the moment CB6 is not yet on record with a position reacting to these cuts. Anyone interested in this should attend our May 20th Transportation Committee meeting.

Chairperson Bashner informed the Board that the Mayor has released his Executive budget and did reconsider his previous crippling cut to the Community Boards’ operating budgets and instead of reducing our budget from $208K to $160K, he will only be cutting us down to $199K instead. Believe it or not, that $9K gap still means 2

that we will not be able to replace our former part-time position, and we’re still losing ground since we do not receive cost of living adjustments. I understand that Board Members Rick Luftglass, Suzanne Turet and Stephen Burzio will be sitting down with our District Manager to work on a strategic plan for fundraising for our non-profit organization, and while I am optimistic about our prospects, we really can’t afford to lose any ground with regard to our City budget. At this time we must rely exclusively on the City Council to restore our budget in whole, and I reiterate our urgent plea to our representatives for them to do so.

Chairperson Bashner ended his report by reminding the Board Members that we are expecting a visit tonight from City Council Speaker Christine Quinn, who is scheduled to arrive around 8pm. She wanted to address us on some work that the City Council is doing, particularly with regard to the expansion of the City’s recycling program. It is only fitting that she come here to make such an announcement since CB6 has been a decades- long recycling leader in the City of New York, and was the proving ground for the City’s recycling program when it was in the experimental phase 20 years ago.

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that over the next couple of days the district office will be undergoing computer upgrades. He asked everyone to bear with us for any unexpected outages.

District Manager Hammerman reported that on the district office recently had a meeting with the Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) and GreenSpace, the local gardeners who have been involved in the development of the community garden site located at Sackett Street and 4th Avenue, where DEP has a shaft that descends down to the third water tunnel leg that runs beneath our district. DEP will not be able to put the full garden in until 2013 but would like to give the community with some interim access to the site prior to the completion of the garden. The meeting was set up to discuss what type of access the community could have. A second meeting will be scheduled next month to work out some of the details.

District Manager Hammerman advised the Board that, according to the DDC Commissioner, the Reconstruction of Columbia Street (HWK-700A) will resume on May 10th. They will be completing the second half of this project, Van Brunt Street between Hamilton Avenue and Degraw Street. We have invited DDC to the next Transportation Committee meeting on May 20th so that they can properly brief the community since there has been such a long lapse of time from the last time there has been any activity out there.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the Board that the district office will be hosting its annual informational meeting on May 24th at the Public Safety/Environmental Protection/Permits & Licenses Committee in an effort to better understand the rules and regulations governing restaurants and bars in our district. Representatives from the State Liquor Authority and various City agencies will be on hand to answer any questions in respect to noise, smoking, backyard usage, garbage and other related issues.

We are making inquiries and hope to get more factual information to disseminate throughout the community.

Economic Development Co-Chair Rick Luftglass reported to the Board that he attended a public meeting held by the Department of City Planning regarding Vision 2020, their Comprehensive Waterfront Plan for the over 500 miles of New York City’s waterfront, defined as New York Harbor and its tributaries, creeks and bays. Vision 2020 will build on the original Comprehensive Waterfront Plan, published in 1992, and the city’s experience over the past 18 years in order to set forth a new long range vision for a 21 st Century waterfront. Specifically, Vision 2020 will identify key opportunities for improving NYC’s waterfront and outline strategies to realize this new vision. They will be holding public workshops in May and June in each borough to present an overview of each Borough’s waterfront resources and to discuss the future of specific waterfront sites. At these workshops, City Planning will present an overview of the borough’s waterfront resources and existing uses. We’ll then break into small groups to discuss particular segments of the Borough’s waterfront, what we refer to as Reaches. For each reach the gro ups will discuss opportunities and challenges for the future of specific sites along the waterfront. Brooklyn’s workshop will be at Brooklyn Technical High School on May 17 th from 6-8:30pm. __________________________________________________________________________________________

NYC Council Speaker Christine C. Quinn, announced legislation that would dramatically expand and improve recycling in New York City. The package of legislation would mark the first significant expansion of the city’s residential recycling program since it was created in 1989. The new recycling legislation includes the following:

Expansion of Recyclable Materials and Recycling Programs 1. Recycling all rigid plastic containers, including items such as yogurt tubs; take out containers, flower pots and medicine bottles. This component of the bill would take effect following the opening of a new recycling facility located in Brooklyn, which is scheduled to open in 2012.

2. Require DOS to site 300 new recycling bins throughout the City over the next three years, and a total of 700 bins within the next ten years.

3. Mandate at least one department-sponsored household hazardous waste collection event in each borough every year, with a long-term goal of increasing the number of events, or making such sites permanent.

4. Require DOS to establish a citywide textile collection program by placing deposit bins on city-owned or city-managed property throughout the city.

5. Establish a voluntary manufacturer and retailer take-back program for unwanted household paint, which makes up about 50% of household hazardous waste.

Changes and Improvements to Existing Programs 1. Require every school within the Department of Education to designate a recycling coordinator and to provide recycling receptacles in each classroom and other locations such as entrances and lunch rooms. Similar requirements would also apply to non-DOE schools.

2. Require each city agency to designate a recycling coordinator and implement plans to increase recycling in all city-owned and city-run buildings.

3. Extends the DOS’s Leaf and Yard Waste Composting collection period from March 1 – November 30, and requires the city to establish a new leaf and yard waste composting facility in Queens or Brooklyn.

9

4. Replaces Obsolete Tonnage Mandates.

Improved Enforcement, Outreach and Education 1. Makes Fine Structure Fairer for Small Property Owners.

2. Offers first-time offenders in 1-8 unit residences the option to attend recycling workshops (including online tutorials) in lieu of paying fines. Also requires DOS to provide trainings for owners and employees of buildings with 9 or more units that receive three tickets in one year.

3. Requires DOS to create a guide to the residential recycling program, to be distributed and made available to the public.

New Reports and Studies 1. Requires a study exploring recycling markets and opportunities to expand recycling facilities and recycling-related jobs in New York City.

2. Requires DOS to study methods for expanding capacity to compost residential and commercial food waste. Upon completion, DOS must conduct a commercial food waste composting pilot.

3. Requires the Commissioner to issue an annual report detailing the recycling totals for all materials recycled under City and State law.

4. Requires DOS to complete a commercial recycling study.

5. Requires DOS to conduct follow-up waste characterization studies in 2012 and 2018 and requires a comprehensive study by 2024.
