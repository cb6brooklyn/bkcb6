CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………...….THOMAS MISKEL

1st Vice Chairperson Thomas Miskel began his report by informing the board that Chairperson Daniel Kummer had a pressing family obligation tonight and is unable to attend the meeting.

Vice Chairperson Miskel reported that Board Member Steven Burzio has advised us that he recently began a new job as the Principal Law Clerk to NYS Supreme Court Justice Carl Landicino and, as a result, he is resigning due to the increased time and energy the new position is demanding of him. He stated that he would personally miss him as Co-Chair of our Transportation Committee and on behalf of the board and our Chairperson I'd like to wish him much success in his new position and thank him for his dedicated, selfless service to the board.

Vice Chairperson Miskel also reported to the board that Board Member Linda Blyer is opting not to apply for reappointment to the board when her term is up this year. He thanked her for her service and asks that she consider reapplying in the future if circumstance will allow for it.

Vice Chairperson Miskel advised the board that all members whose terms are up this year are advised that they will need to reapply and return their completed, notarized application to Borough Hall by February 27, 2012. If you have any questions or problems, please see Kai Feder, our new liaison from the Borough President's Office.

Vice Chairperson Miskel ended the Chairman’s Report by congratulating District Manager, Craig Hammerman, who was appointed by the Mayor's Office to serve on a roundtable that will be developing improvements to the Mayor's Management Report, the annual report of indicators which describes the quality and quantity of services delivered by the agencies of the City of New York. The Mayor is drawing experts from inside and outside of government to serve on this panel and we are especially honored that our own District Manager is included among them.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the board that the reconstruction work on the Nicholas Naquan Heyward Jr. Park located on Wyckoff Street between Hoyt and Bond Streets began this week.

District Manager Craig Hammerman reported to the board that on March 30th the United States Postal Service will be terminating services at their contract station located at 257 Columbia Street in our district. We are not sure of the specifics, but will be writing to USPS to ask them to reconsider the closing.

The District Manager informed the board that he and Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch recently attended a meeting with the Street Activity Permit Office to discuss the new changes they have implemented since instituting and online electronic application system 2 years ago. Highlights of the changes are as follows:  Applicant will receive a notification from SAPO directing them to our website to see our requirements for block parties. This will illuminate the office staff from calling all applicants to inform them of our requirements.  SAPO will email the applicant their permit immediately after it has received the recommendations from the required city agencies.  Price for the block party application fee has increased from $15.50 to $25.75.  The lead time for submitting applications has also increased from 60 days to 90 days.

2

 There is a new permitted event which is called a “Single Block Festival” which is a street activity requiring a single block street closure and contains vendors. The Sponsor shall be a community-based, not-for-profit organization, association, or the like, which has an indigenous relationship to the specific street, community or both. Starting next year all “Single Block Festival” applications must be submitted by December 31, 2012.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by advising the board of an emergency condition at 147 Dwight Street in the Red Hook section of our district. Department of Buildings has informed us that the owner has been notified to remedy this situation by demolition the building.

LANDMARKS/LAND USE COMMITTEE………………………...….ROBERT LEVINE, LANDMARKS

…………………………….….PETER FLEMING, LAND USE

Land Use Chairperson Peter Fleming reported that the committee reviewed the Department of City Planning’s Zone Green Text Amendment (ULURP #N 120132 ZRY), an application which seeks to revise the City’s Zoning Resolution to remove impediments to the construction and retrofitting of greener buildings.

Motion: by Committee;

4

Recommendation to approve the Department of City Planning’s Zone Green Text Amendment (ULURP #N 120132 ZRY), an application which seeks to revise the City’s Zoning Resolution to remove impediments to the construction and retrofitting of greener buildings with the following conditions:  That greenhouses be reviewed by the Community Board via ULURP.  Extension of façade in historic districts and contextual must be reviewed by the Community Board and the Landmarks Preservation Commission.  Compressor units in LPC or contextual historic districts are subject to Community Board and LPC review.

Board Member Richard Bashner asked if there was any discussion on sound restrictions for the wind turbines. Land Use Chairperson Fleming stated that the committee discussed the issue but did not take a position on it.

Board Member Mark Shames stated that he voted for this motion but had some concerns about the noise the wind turbines make. Also, he pointed out that 4th Avenue has a number of buildings over 100 feet, which would make it a prime location for a number of these turbines.

Board Member Robert Levine stated that he thought that these wind turbines would only be restricted to waterfront communities. Also, they would not go on buildings in height restricted neighborhoods. Land Use Chairperson Fleming stated that there are no restrictions on the location of the turbines but they could not go on buildings in height restricted neighborhoods.

Board Member Elizabeth Shipley stated that there are not that many buildings in our district over 100 feet and the wind turbines could be placed on commercial properties on the water front regardless of height.

Board Member Lou Sones asked how much noise the turbines make. Chairperson Fleming stated that we did not know.

Board Member Norman Cox asked why the committee felt this should go through the ULURP process. Chairperson Fleming stated that it would give the Community Board a formal way to review the applications and submit our recommendations.

Board Member Noel Berk-Rauch asked if these turbines could only be used by the property it was on or could someone enter into an agreement and use the turbines from one property to generate energy to another property. Chairperson Fleming stated that the committee did not discuss that issue.

Amendment: by Board Member Richard Bashner, seconded by Board Member Gregory O’Connell Jr; To include that our approval of the wind turbine portion of the proposal is subject to the promulgation of standards regarding sound and vibrations that will keep them at acceptable levels.

Board Member Levine stated that he was okay with the turbines as long as they meet current sound standards. Board Member Shipley stated that the turbines have to meet standards all we are doing with the motion is taking away the zoning prohibitions.

Board Member Bashner stated that we certainly should be considering wind turbines; the problem with the noise standards is that the City measures the noise at the point of impact at the person who hears the sound, rather than measure the output of the turbines. The City needs to come up with a standard for sound generation and vibration at the source. Board Member Shames spoke in favor of the amendment.

5

Board Member Roger Rigolli stated that these wind turbines are run by motors and are not as efficient as solar panels. Also, they would best be installed near the waterfront.

VOTE: 31 YEAS, 3 NAYS, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED

TIME: 7:21 P.M.

To require a special permit for the greenhouse that would include the definition that it could be only used for agricultural growing usages. Board Member Cox accepted the friendly amendment.

Parks Committee Co-Chairperson Glenn Kelly also reported to the Board that the committee reviewed a proposal for the development of a community garden (the “Children’s Garden”) at 204 Columbia Street.

Motion: by Board Member Peter Fleming, seconded by Board Member Hildegaard Link; Recommendation to support a proposal to be submitted to the Department of Parks & Recreation’s Green Thumb program for the development of a community garden (the “Children’s Garden”) at 204 Columbia Street (northwest corner Columbia/Sackett Streets).

Board Member Dolly Williams ask if this lot could be used for housing. Parks Committee Co-Chairperson Kelly stated that it could be used for housing but there are no plans for build housing at this time and the garden would not prevent housing being built on it in the future.

Board Member Bashner stated that although this garden would not preclude housing being built in the future getting land back from the green thumb program after it has been turned over by HPD will be very difficult.

Board Member O’Connell stated that there are other gardens in the vicinity and we should not limit this lot to a community garden.

Board Member Shames stated that we should not focus on only one usage of this lot an analysis should be done on other possible usages and/or if this area needs another community garden.

Board Member Rigolli spoke out against the motion stating that there are enough gardens in the area.

Board Member Benjamin Atkins spoke in favor of the motion stating that although it may be hard to get the land back to build housing on it, this proposal is better than what is there now.

Board Member Debra Scotto stated that we do not have enough information regarding this motion and should discuss it further in committee.

Board Member Link spoke in favor of the motion stating that she is not aware of any children’s gardens in the area.

Board Member Sayar Lonial spoke in favor of the children’s garden but asked if the dispossession of the land be taken out of the motion, encouraging HPD to allow the temporary use.

7

Board Member Peter Fleming withdrew his motion after hearing the discussion on the floor.

Motion: by Board Member Sayar Lonial, seconded by Board Member Hildegaard Link; Recommendation to support the concept of the children’s garden and encourage HPD to allow for its temporary use without transferring the land to the Parks Department.

Board Member Bashner asked if he could add a friendly amendment to the motion stating that HPD should do a feasibility study for alternative usages before transferring the land to Parks and return to the committee to discuss their findings. Board Member Lonial accepted the friendly amendment.

Discussion on original motion as amended continued.
