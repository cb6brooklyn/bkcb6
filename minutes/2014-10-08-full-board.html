CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by announcing that we received a letter of resignation from former Youth Committee Chair Mark Kolman. We wish him good health and all good things moving forward, and we thank him for his many years of selfless service on behalf of our community. That brings our total down to 45 board members.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that last month three of our committees co-hosted a meeting where representatives for the New York State Department of Corrections and Community Supervision (DOCCS) appeared to talk about their plans to open a borough-wide parolee reporting center at 15 2nd Avenue at the Gowanus Canal. Since then, the community organized another meeting which was facilitated by District Manager Craig Hammerman and Matthew Silverman our Public Safety Chair, and attended by our Human Service Co-Chairs Benjamin Solotaire and Paige Bellenbaum as well. At that meeting several community groups and elected officials made statements regarding their concerns about this project, which we still having nothing in writing about. At our committee meeting the State representatives committed to responding to our questions in writing, and also to return to us for a follow-up meeting. So we assembled all the questions from our meeting and the community’s meeting, and District Manager Hammerman and I wrote to the DOCCS Commissioner asking for a response by the end of this month. We also told them that we would like to reconvene and host the follow-up meeting sometime in November, assuming of course that we have their written responses in hand. Lastly, it was suggested by the community groups that we offer to give them a walking tour of the area around the new facility, which we also offered to do in our letter. We will share their response broadly when we receive it.

Chairperson Kummer advised the board that last month the Port Authority and Red Hook Container Terminal Operator were in the process of permitting a third party event organizer to hold an event at Pier 9 in the containerport. The event was billed as a weekend music festival, designed to attract roughly 8,000 people per day over the Saturday and Sunday of Columbus Day weekend, October 11-12. We have since found out indirectly that the event organizer has moved their venue from the containerport to Governors Island instead.

Chairperson Kummer ended by informing the Board that we have a new Commanding Officer here at the 78th Precinct, Captain Frank DiGiacomo, who took over command a couple of months ago. Our next Public Safety Committee meeting is going to be held in this very room at 6:30pm on October 16th and Captain DiGiacomo will be in attendance to introduce himself more formally. This would be a great opportunity to get to know him better and maybe even share your own ideas about what some of your public safety issues are here in the 78th Precinct. This precinct has been a model precinct in terms of embracing the City’s Vision Zero concepts and has really done an outstanding job helping to educate and enforce traffic laws that protect pedestrians, bicyclists and motorists alike. He invited everyone to attend the meeting and participate in the conversation with the new commander.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman announced to the Board that the district office will again have a Planning Fellow working during this school year through the Fund for the City of New York. This year we were able to secure two planning fellows. Each will be working on separate projects:

Project A – Feasibility Study for an Industrial Business Improvement District in and around the Gowanus Canal This initiative is one of the spin-offs from our now completed Gowanus BOA Project. What we are hoping to do within this academic year is reach out to the property owners in the Gowanus area to see what their needs are and whether we can help them develop a program and a possible budget. We would like to see if there is enough support for an industrial BID, or iBID, to move forward there. Councilman Lander thought that an iBID along the Gowanus was such a good idea to explore he has committed $11,000 of his discretionary funds for the study. The Department of Small Business Services has also agreed to partner with us on this endeavor.

Project B – Housing Opportunity Study of our district to respond and hopefully guide the new administration in developing a broader affordable housing development policy. The goal is to sift through all of the properties in our district within residential zones to see how much under built floor area ratio or bulk we have. From there we will see if reasonable assemblages could be put forward or suggested to the Administration where housing opportunities would be natural for them to seize upon.

If anyone is interested in participating in either of the projects please see me at the end of the meeting.

EXECUTIVE/BUDGET COMMITTEE………………………......RICK LUFTGLASS, BUDGET CHAIR

Budget Chairperson Rick Luftglass reported to the Board that the committee discussed the proposed CB6 FY 2015 Capital and Expense Budget Requests.

Motion: by Committee;

To approve the FY 2016 Capital budget requests.

Board Member Vilma Heraminia asked why there were no funding amounts on the forms. District Manager Hammerman stated that what the Board Members have in front of them are the titles and priority listings of the requests. When submitted to OMB each title will have a paragraph under it in which dollar amounts are included wherever we have been provided estimates from the agencies.

Board Member Roger Rigolli asked for an explanation of the code “CS”. District Manager Hammerman explained that “CS” stands for Continued Support and is generally applicable to items that have already been approved and funded even though work may or may not have begun.

Board Member Eric McClure asked why the Capital Budget requests must have a dollar value that exceeds $35,000. Budget Chairperson Rick Luftglass stated that is one of the City’s criteria—the project must be valued at greater than $35,000, typically cannot be completed during the course of one fiscal year, and should last at least 5 years as a physical asset/improvement. Board Member Peter Fleming asked for an explanation on the renovation of the Park Slope library. District Manager Craig Hammerman stated that they left things out because they primarily focused on the interior of the library except for the main entranceway. Perimeter improvements that were not done but are sorely needed include the condition of the sidewalk, the front entrance gateway, and the condition and foundation of the perimeter fence. Board Member Roger Rigolli requested that item #36, Construction of the Gowanus Canal Storm Surge Barrier be a higher priority. District Manager Craig Hammerman said it was a higher priority last year but when the city told us that they were not going to build it until after they built the barrier for Newtown Creek first we lowered it.

VOTE: 33 YEAS, 0 NAYS, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED UNANIMOUSLY

TIME: 7:51 P.M.

Motion: by Committee;

To approve the FY 2016 Expense budget requests.

Board Member Eric McClure asked how long item #1 – Restoration of Police Precinct Patrol Personnel has held that spot and, in such low-crime precincts where crime is trending lower, whether it can be relinquished. District Manager Hammerman stated that the item became #1 about 5-7 years ago when we saw a steady, sharp decrease in police personnel. At that time many of the Community Boards made a concerted effort to make it a collective #1 request. Board Member Roger Rigolli asked to what extent the NYPD does truck enforcement in the district especially trucks that are off the route and are making deliveries. District Manager Craig Hammerman stated that the Truck Enforcement Unit is a centralized unit that goes out based on complaints and issues violations to any trucks off the truck route who are not making deliveries.
