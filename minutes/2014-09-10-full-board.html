CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by announcing that over the summer we received a letter of resignation from long-time Red Hook Board Member and 2nd Vice Chair Lou Sones. Board Member Sones cited competing work demands and some personal health issues as his reason for leaving the board. We will miss him, and his representative voice, and we wish him good health and all good things moving forward as we thank him for his many years of selfless service on behalf of our community.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that owing to the loss of Board Members Elizabeth Shipley, our former Secretary, and more recently Lou Sones, our former Second Vice Chair, our bylaws states that it falls upon the Chairperson to appoint replacements to fill out the remainder of the term until the next regular election, subject to the ratification of our Executive Committee. The committee met this past Monday evening so we have 2 new officers to welcome to our dais. I’m pleased to welcome Board Member Pauline Blake as CB6’s new Second Vice Chair and our Housing Committee Chair Board Members Ariel Krasnow who joins us as Secretary. Both Board Members have proven their commitment to the Community Board many times over, and we are most fortunate to have such high caliber talent joining the ranks of our officers.

Chairperson Kummer advised the Board that he is now in the process of appointing an Election Committee to oversee our nominations and election process over the next few months. He has asked Peter Fleming to continue as Chair of our Election Committee since he has done such an outstanding job in this role. This year three of our officers including myself as Chair, Board Members Tom Miskel as First Vice Chair, and Gary Reilly as Secretary, will be term limited, thus preventing us from running for our current offices on next year’s slate of officers. At our November general meeting there will be open nominations from the floor, and the slate will be closed. Then, if any of our officer positions are contested, there will be an election at our December general meeting. All board members may vote at the December meeting but you must be present in order to vote and all votes are recorded and made public afterward. New officers take effect on January 1st and their term is one calendar year. Our bylaws permit up to four consecutive terms in the same office. The Chair also congratulated our Election Chair Peter Fleming on the occasion of his marriage last month to long-time partner Brian King.

Chairperson Kummer congratulated the following candidates who won their primary day races yesterday, starting with Assemblyman Felix Ortiz, and Jo Anne Simon and Jesse Hamilton who won the Democratic lines to run in the General election to succeed outgoing Assemblywoman Joan Millman, and former State Senator, now Borough President Eric Adams, respectively. The general election is on Tuesday, November 4th. There is still time to register to vote.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that they should have received both their letters of appointment to committees for the 2014-2015 session and an email from District Manager Craig Hammerman with instructions for how to participate in the committee’s list server. If you find your schedule or interest changes during the year, please let me or District Manager Hammerman know as soon as possible before attendance becomes an issue. It is always our preference to not have to intervene because of attendance and, more importantly, when attendance jeopardizes our ability to maintain a quorum it becomes more challenging for us to conduct business and continue to be an effective representative voice.

Chairperson Kummer took a moment to spotlight some upcoming meetings which some or all of you may be interested in attending and participating in:  On Monday, September 15, 2014 at 6:30pm at the 78th Precinct, 65 6th Avenue, three of our committees are co-hosting a meeting, being co-sponsored by State Senator Velmanette Montgomery and City Council Member Brad Lander, where representatives for the New York State Department of Corrections and Community Service will be presenting us with plans to open a borough wide parolee 2

reporting center at 15 2nd Avenue at the Gowanus Canal. When we first found out about this back in July, District Manager Hammerman immediate wrote to the DOCCS Commissioner in Albany to try to find out more about what this project entailed. In the absence of a response from the agency, State Senator Montgomery’s office intervened and arranged for the representatives to attend. City Councilman Lander’s office, like our office and the Senator’s, also fielded many calls from curious and angry nearby residents and businesses who wanted to find out more about this new facility. We’re thankful and grateful that our elected officials responded in such a supportive fashion, and are hopeful that we will be able to shine some light and make some sense out of what seems to be a pretty closed process.  On Wednesday, September 17th at 6:00pm at the Wyckoff Gardens Community Center, 280 Wyckoff Street, our Environmental Protection/Permits & Licenses Committee is hosting a meeting where Department of Environmental Protection Commissioner Emily Lloyd will present to us the department’s process and plans for siting retention tanks to address the Gowanus combined sewer overflow or CSO issues. DEP is under order by the EPA to address the CSO issues as part of the Gowanus Superfund listing, and the department has been looking at all of the properties in the Gowanus corridor to determine where these retention tanks can be built. The tanks will have the capacity to hold the mixture of sanitary sewage and storm water during and after rain events to prevent it from discharging untreated into the canal itself. The mixture will then be slowly released back into the sewer system where it is sent to the water pollution control plant for processing.  On Wednesday, September 17th at 6:30pm at the Miccio Center, 110 West 9th Street, our Parks/Recreation/Cultural Affairs Committee will be hearing two presentations related to the Columbia Waterfront and Red Hook waterfront. First, the Port Authority and Red Hook Container Terminal Operator are in the process of permitting a third-party event organizer to hold an event at Pier 9 in the container port, which is roughly parallel to Kane Street at the waterfront. The event is billed as a weekend music festival, designed to attract roughly 8,000 people per day over the Saturday and Sunday of Columbus Day weekend, October 11-12. District Manager Hammerman was invited to a meeting with the Port Authority and requested that they provide us with a fact sheet providing more detailed information about the event, and asked them to attend a meeting that we would host to give the public a chance to hear more about the plans directly. The fact sheet is available on our website calendar listed under the committee’s September 17th meeting. The second item on their agenda is a presentation from the Park Department on a $2mil Capital project to construct a comfort station – bathroom – at the Louis Valentino Jr. Park & Pier. Council Member Carlos Menchaca and Borough President Adams each contributed $250,000 to a pre-existing $1.5mil budget, and the department is beginning the process of talking to the community by presenting their initial thoughts on how that money can be used.

Chairperson Kummer ended by reminding the Board that every October the Community Board reviews and adopts its annual Capital and Expense budget priorities for the next City fiscal year. In our recent newsletter we highlighted this fact and encouraged our constituents to use our online Capital budget mapping tool to provide us with their suggestions for where they would like to see our local City assets improved. I’d invite everyone to visit www.BKCB6budget.org to put your suggestions on the map so that was can consider them next month, and in the future. Also, if you’d like to be involved in this conversation, please consider attending our Executive/Budget Committee meeting on Monday, October 6th where we will be discussing our budget items and putting together our recommendation that will be made to the full board at the October 8th meeting.

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that the New York Department of Design and Construction has informed us that AKRF, Inc., Consulting Engineers, has been retained to perform design services for complex, simple, and landmark pedestrian ramps at select comers to improve the safety of pedestrians and motorists at certain intersections in our district. District Manager Hammerman advised the board that we get a number of calls from residents asking what time they should put out their garbage for collection. The following is the rule for Placement for Collection:

Placement for Collection—Storage of Receptacles: Residential units may place receptacles out for collection on the sidewalk, right by the curb, no earlier than 5:00 PM the day before their scheduled collection, and no earlier than 4:00 PM from October 1st to April 1st. Residential units must remove receptacles from their collection place by 9:00 PM on their collection day. If collection occurs after 4:00 PM, receptacles must be removed by 9:00 AM the next day. §16-120(c) FINE: $100 - $300

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by reporting to the Board that we have been approached by the group that was working with the Red Hook community to develop an Emergency Preparedness Emergency Response Plan. They are looking for a location to house the plan, so they reached out to the district office. We would have to house the plan, have a preparedness drill at least once a year, and biannual check-ins with all the people/organizations who have agreed to preform different roles in the first 72 hours responding to a disaster. We have not finalized anything as of yet, but will get back to the board after we have decided if this is something we will be able to absorb. The same group has also been asked to help put together a similar plan for the Gowanus area of the district, and they’ve indicated that they would like us to think about taking over that plan too.

Dan Wiley, representing Congress Member Nydia Velázquez, stated that the Congresswoman hosted a roundtable discussion between federal, state and city officials aimed at finding ways to ensure New York City small firms impacted by Hurricane Sandy receive assistance they need to fully recover. During the discussions it was found that while both New York City and New York State use CDBG-DR funding for business loan and grant programs, one city initiative required entrepreneurs to secure a loan with strict criteria, before being eligible for a grant. At the meeting the Commissioner of the Department of Small Business Services explained their newly redesigned program allows businesses to apply for grants first with more flexible requirements and for higher amounts. By allowing businesses to apply for a grant first, more firms will be eligible for a loan, allowing them to strengthen their long term cash flow. At the conclusion of the meeting, the Congresswoman proposed the formation of SWAT (Sandy Work Action Team) with members of each agency, service providers, elected officials, and small business representatives committed to meeting regularly and working through remaining challenges. This will help ensure New York City small firms impacted by Sandy receive assistance 5

they need to recover. The Congresswoman plans to hold another interagency round table meeting in three months to gauge progress.

Motion: by Committee;

Recommendation to approve a new unenclosed sidewalk cafe permit application submitted to the Department of Consumer Affairs on behalf of Just Salad at 252 7th Avenue (between 4th/5th Streets) to permit 13 tables and 26 seats with the following conditions:  The removal of the 3 tables (6 seats) nearest the southeastern tree pit  A Commitment toward no enclosure of the sidewalk café.

Board Member Eric McClure asked what the rule was in regards to the amount of space a sidewalk café can use. Environmental Protection Chair Mark Shames stated that the rule is at least 50% of the width of the sidewalk or a minimum of 8 feet.

Board Member Pauline Blake spoke out against the motion stating that when you look at the number of sidewalk cafes, the number of families in Park slope with strollers, the number of individuals who need assistance with walkers we have to be very careful that we don’t change our rules (2:1 indoor: outdoor seating rule of thumb), just because we think the applicant needs a little help. Once you change your rules we can never go back.

Board Member Glenn Kelly agreed with Board Member Pauline Blake with regards to our standards, but to his recollection we have at least 2 or 3 times gone beyond that 50% limitation.

Board Member Matthew Silverman spoke out against the motion stating that the applicant made a statement at the meeting that if he cuts down the amount of seats in the sidewalk café, basically the fee for the seats would outweigh the worth of doing it at all. He looked up the fees and determined that the statement was not mathematically accurate.

Board Member Jerry Armer spoke out against the motion stating that he finds it interesting that the use of the financial burden was used because the sidewalk café is not there all year round, it is only there for a select number of months. He does not like the idea of bending the rule.

Board Member Richard Bashner stated that he really never understood the rule because it relates to the space inside the business not the space outside. Maybe what we should do is tweak the rule to make it clear when it should or not apply.

Board Member Eric Anderson stated that he asked at the meeting what the rationale was in regards to the rule and no one gave a satisfactory answer to the question.

Board Member Dolly Williams spoke in favor of the motion stating that business have a hard enough time to earn a living without us imposing restraints on them.

Board Member Glenn Kelly added that we also insisted that the establishment have a substantial food menu and not just a bar who serves liquor outside. He also asked why the inside had so few seats.
