CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……………………………………………………….................GARY REILLY

Chairperson Reilly opened the meeting by thanking those who attended the annual Holiday Party and remarked that the event was well-received and organized. He thanked the District Office staff and Board Members Glenn Kelly and Sayar Lonial for their roles in supporting the event.

Chairperson Reilly next remarked that he recently spoke with Board Member Tom Miskel, who was recovering from a recent heart ailment. The Chair reported that Mr. Miskel sounded well, and noted that the board is always stronger when Tom is around.

Chairperson Reilly next thanked Board Members Jerry Armer, John Heyer, Glenn Kelly, Sayar Lonial, Rick Luftglass, Eric McClure and Mark Shames for participating in an ad hoc group that is considering protocols for street co-naming requests. The Chair called on any other interested members to considering attending the next Executive Committee meeting where draft protocols will be reviewed.

The Chair announced that the City is holding a meeting at 6:30pm on January 21st at the Miccio Center, 110 West 9th Street, to discuss the Red Hook Integrated Floor Protection System study commissioned last year. The meeting is the first in a series of public meetings intended to solicit and report on measures than can be taken to make Red Hook more resilient with regard to future flood events.

The Chairperson concluded his remarks by announcing that the District Office received notice from the Department of Homeless Services that they were registering a contract with Children’s Community Services to continue to provide temporary and emergency housing and related social services for families with children at the Super 8 Motel at 267 3rd Avenue in Gowanus. The Chair stated that the notification provided to the District Office outlined the parameters of the agreement and showed that the facility has been used for these purposes since September 2015. Mr. Reilly stated that the office has received no complaints about the Super 8 or the agreement from the community.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by wishing the board a Happy New Year. He then commented on the on-going process of the Red Hook Community Microgrid project, which is wrapping up the Phase 1 feasibility study while preparing for the Phase 2 audit report. Mr. Hammerman remarked that one piece of closing out Phase 1 is a public meeting, which is tentatively scheduled to happen in February.

Mr. Hammerman noted that January marks the 1st anniversary of idNYC, and remarked that Councilmember Menchaca was one of the City Council’s spearheading forces for the municipal identification card. The District Manager noted that while idNYC serves as an acceptable form of photo ID at many locations, it also provides

2

museum discounts, library access, and a number of other memberships. Mr. Hammerman stated that a pop-up enrollment center is currently in place from January 6th through January 29th at the Red Hook Community Justice Center. Anyone wishing to get an idNYC must schedule an appointment and bring a number of relevant documents. Mr. Hammerman directed attendees to 311 as a means to schedule an appointment to receive an idNYC.

Mr. Hammerman closed his remarks by announcing that the District Office has recently established relationships with two organizations. The first is a CUNY program which is training students in surveying constituencies for information; the District Office plans to utilize CUNY as a means of surverying civic engagement within the district. The second organization, Advanced Recycling Solutions, is in the process of taking on the beverage redemption operation previously run by Phoenix Beverage, which is no longer in conducting this business. The District Office is working with Advanced Recycling Solutions as they attempt to gain different certifications from the State, and introducing them to the South Brooklyn Industrial Development Corporation in the hopes of better serving the local community in a number of ways moving forward.

Landmarks Chair Robert Levine reported that the committee considered a Certificate of Appropriateness application for 202 Warren Street.

Motion: Recommendation to disapprove a Certificate of Appropriateness application submitted to the Landmarks Preservation Commission for the rooftop installation of an elevated canopy solar energy system at 202 Warren Street (between Clinton/Henry Streets), Cobble Hill Historic District.

Conversation ensued, most heavily relevant to Landmarks Preservation when considering energy alternatives. A significant number of board members expressed their concern with setting a precedent that de-incentivizes homeowners in Historic Districts from upgrading their energy supply to more modern and environmentally friendly standards. The overwhelming majority of board members disagreed with the committee’s recommendation to disapprove.

Chairperson Gary Reilly called for a Roll Call vote after twenty-five minutes of discussion.

VOTE: 9 YEAS, 21 NAYS, 1 ABSTENTION

MOTION FAILED

TIME: 8:26P.M.
