CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer reported that on the morning of Monday, June 2, a 14-year old boy named Nicholas Soto who was rushing toward his school bus was struck and killed by a car while he was crossing Lorraine Street at the Hicks Street intersection in Red Hook. The driver stayed at the scene and police reported they did not suspect any criminality. Council Member Menchaca had an emergency community meeting later that evening and promised to follow up with NYPD and DOT to see what can be done to improve safety in the Lorraine Street corridor. I’d like to thank our Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch for attending on our behalf. Neckdowns had already been installed at the intersection, and since Lorraine Street is part of a bus route it would not be an eligible candidate for speed humps. Police have not confirmed whether the driver of the car was speeding. These senseless tragedies must end. Our thoughts and prayers go out to the Soto family and as we begin our meeting tonight, I would like to ask for a moment of silence in memory of Nicholas Soto.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that we have 6 new Community Board members who are here tonight. Some faces may be familiar to us as we welcome the newest additions to our board. Returning to CB6 after what we hope was a relaxing hiatus is Jerry Armer from Cobble Hill who was appointed at the recommendation of Councilman Lander. In addition to being a twice former Chairperson and longtime Land Use Chair, Jerry is also a former President and officer of the Cobble Hill Association and continues to serve as the President of our 76th Precinct Community Council. Pauline Blake of Park Slope is also returning as a former board Chairperson and Housing Committee Chair. She was appointed by Borough President Adams and continues to serve as the longtime President of the 78th Precinct Community Council. Eric McClure of Park Slope is also a longtime member of our Transportation/Public Safety Committee, as well as a former trustee of the Park Slope Civic Council and the founder and coordinator of Park Slope Neighbors, the Park Slope Street Safety Partnership and StreetsPAC. He was appointed at the recommendation of Councilman Lander. Victoria Hagman of Red Hook is new to the board, and was appointed at the recommendation of Councilman Menchaca. In addition to being a local realtor, Victoria has developed an impressive resume of volunteering with ReStore Red Hook, the Red Hook Coalition, PortSide New York and more organizations than even we are aware of. She continues to be a driving force behind Red Hook recovery and resiliency planning and we look forward to plugging her into the Community Board’s activities as quickly as we can. Jean Fritzner of Gowanus was appointed at the recommendation of Councilman Lander and, of course, there are so many things happening in and to Gowanus these days that adding another voice into the discussion can only be helpful. And, lastly, Scott Mathews-Novelli of Park Slope was appointed by Borough President Adams. Welcome and welcome back to all of our new board members. I’d encourage you all to use the summer to get up-to-speed on all things CB6 and to reach out and make an appointment with our District Manager who can spend some time with you answering your questions and helping to make sure you get satisfaction and fulfillment from your service. He also stated that he was saddened to report that when the new list of members came out that meant we lost our Secretary Elizabeth Shipley which I had reported on at last month’s meeting, and we also lost Yvonne Girela who because of redistricting was not reappointed to the board. I’d like to thank both of them for their selfless service to the board and our communities. For those of you keeping score, this means our total roster is now up to 48 Board Members. District Manager Craig Hammerman has done a quick analysis for us and we break 2

down along the following neighborhoods: we currently have 19 members from Park Slope, 12 from Carroll Gardens, 6 from Red Hook, 4 from Cobble Hill, 4 from Gowanus and 3 from the Columbia Waterfront.

Chairperson Kummer reminded the Board that applications for committee membership requests have been distributed and our office in the process of collecting responses. The yearlong term begins on September 1st, which means we will need to get appointment letters out to you in August. As a last reminder, please keep in mind that I do make every effort to give board members their top choices when possible. I would prefer not to guess what your preferences are because you did not submit a form. The district office will be following up with all of you over the next few weeks.

Chairperson Kummer congratulated Board Member Richard Bashner, not only for the recent bat mitzvah of his daughter Maya, but also on his recent election as a new trustee on the Park Slope Civic Council.

Chairperson Kummer advised the Board that District Manager Hammerman and I recently met with the Chairs and District Manager of Brooklyn Community Boards 2 and 8 to discuss the potential redrawing of community district lines at the Atlantic Yards footprint. We agreed that it would be best if we could get a memorialization from the developer and even, possibly, the respective oversight agencies that would preserve our common understanding that our community boards would continue to collectively qualify for any benefits deriving from the project such as preferential employment, preferential affordable housing access, etc. regardless of where the lines were drawn. We agreed that we should continue the dialogue within each of our boards, engage the agencies that provide municipal services in the area to see if they have thoughts for us to consider, and that we should regroup in the fall to pick up the discussion further, in the absence of any process-related announcement by the Mayor’s Office which could force us to take action sooner.

Chairperson Kummer thanked District Manager Hammerman for representing us at a recent hearing at the State Liquor Authority in the matter of Kemistry of 260 Flatbush Avenue who had petitioned the SLA to reconsider their application for a liquor license. Kemistry’s attorney had represented to the SLA that they were taking steps to work with the community toward the conditions we had outlined in our objection to their application notably, their refusal to prohibit bottle service, their refusal to block up the Park Place entrance to the space and their refusal to agree to curtailed operating hours. Craig advised the SLA that the applicant had not, in fact, been working with us in any capacity, that we had no specific knowledge that they were intending to honor our conditions and that aside from a single phone call from the applicant after the SLA had turned him down last fall that we have not heard from the applicant at all. The SLA commissioners were unimpressed by the applicant’s arguments and noted that in their view nothing material had changed to warrant a reversal of their prior decision. So on May 20th they voted a second time to disapprove the application.

Chairperson Kummer ended by reminding that Board that since this is the last general meeting before our summer break, if there are any time-sensitive issues that require Community Board action our by-laws empower the Executive Committee to act on behalf of the full board so we do not have to meet.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that the district office held a coordination meeting with various agencies and elected officials to discuss the city’s High-level Storm Sewer Project that is being planned for next year. It is intending to increase storm water carrying capacity in the 3rd and 4th Avenues corridor between Douglass and Carroll Streets to alleviate some of the chronic flooding that happens there. Recently the utilities began their phase of the pre-work which will be ongoing for the next year, where they will all be relocating their equipment to make room for the city’s project.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that work on the Diverter Park at Columbia Street on the west side between Kane and Degraw Streets is progressing. We helped arrange for the Department of Sanitation to 3

deliver 6 trucks full of compost to the site and thanks to the Brooklyn Greenway Initiative volunteers that compost was spread and the area seeded. It looks like the Department of Environmental Protection’s contractor at the site will be pulling the fence line back within the next month. After the fence is moved the Department of Transportation will be installing benches and bike racks at the street ends within that project. Our goal is to have an interim space that is usable by the community until we can get the full capital funding commitment to properly plan, design, and build out the new parks space that will be available there.

District Manager Hammerman informed the board that Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch and I recently met on site with DOT, 76th PCT, Cobble Hill Association, Atlantic Avenue Business Improvement District, and Community Board 2, to take another look at the traffic problems on Court Street south of Atlantic Avenue. Previously when we met out there with DOT they had committed to making some changes, which they have done, to curbside regulations to keep the curb free in front of Trader Joe’s in the hope that fewer cars would double park and congest movement into one lane there. While everyone agreed that DOT’s changes have made a positive impact in the area, there is still no easy solution to making permanent improvements there. For now we are going to have to continue to rely on enforcement to keep things flowing smoothly.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by advising the Board that solar energy is more expensive in Brooklyn than it is in all of the other boroughs. The Park Slope and Downtown cusp where there is a great demand for electricity and where the most active and organized civic and merchants groups in the city are has been chosen by EDC and CUNY to partner with them on a project that will help “solarize” our district. Using money from a US Department of Energy grant CUNY and EDC will be putting out a RFP this summer to get some approved solar installers and they have asked us to help organize our groups so that we can provide input on how the RFP gets written. Also, they want us to help interview the respondents and ultimately help market the service to our district residents and businesses. The RFP will go out in July and they hope to bring the installers on by the end of the summer. If all goes well installation of solar could begin as early as the fall. The overall goal is to reduce the boroughs cost average of solar generation by 10-20%.

Public Safety Chair Matthew Silverman reported that the committee reviewed the New York State Assembly Bill A.8478-A and Senate Bill S.6496-A.

Chairperson Daniel Kummer relinquished the Chair to 1st Vice Chairperson Thomas Miskel for the Transportation & Public Safety Committee Report.

PARKS/RECREATION/CULTURAL AFFAIRS COMMITTEE.……………...GLENN KELLY, CHAIR

Motion: by Board Member Richard Bashner, seconded by Board Member Benjamin Solitaire; Recommendation to adopt the Brooklyn Bridge Park Community Council resolution which states: Recommendation to call for a review of the general project plan of Brooklyn Bridge Park including but not limited to the upland of Pier 6 including the street grid beginning with an immediate solicitation of public comments at a hearing. Have a review by the Department of Transportation of the traffic flow, followed by a presentation to the public and public comment on an expedited basis including building envelopes, surrounding plazas and all related design elements of the Pier 6 uplands. This review does not mandate or preclude ULURP.

Board Member Robert Levine spoke in favor of the motion stating that any residential use should go through

ULURP.

Board Member Jerry Armer spoke in favor of the motion stating that the whole project needs to be looked at again in light of the new funding.

Board Member Lou Sones spoke in favor of the motion stating that in its inception this park was going to be funded by housing.

Board Member Pauline Blake stated that we knew there would be housing on other segments, but never envisioned housing on Pier 6. This Board should not change its position as related to no housing on Pier 6.

Board Member Matthew Silverman spoke in favor of the motion stating that we should be consistent with the CAC, but the letter should have some of CB6’s concerns in it.

Board Member Victoria Hagman stated that this is a state park with state rules. They can be very creative with how they raise funds.
