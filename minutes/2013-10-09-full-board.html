CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by requesting a moment of silence for Samuel Cohen-Eckstein, a 12 year-old boy who was killed while chasing a ball into the street at the intersection of Third Street and Prospect Park West in Park Slope.

Chairperson Kummer congratulated City Council Member Letitia James on her Primary Run-off election victory for the Democratic line in the Public Advocate’s race. Of course, that means we’ll also continue to be served by State Senator Daniel Squadron’s representation of our district in Albany. This Friday, October 11th, is the last day to register to vote in the General election which will be on November 5th. We have Voter Registration forms here tonight. Please take them to anyone you think could use them. There will also be 6 referendums on the ballot this year so you probably will want to take a few minutes to look them over before heading to the polls. The district office will send out a link to them on the Board of Election’s website.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that the Environmental Protection Agency released their Record of Decision (ROD) on September 30th, which effectively spells out the remedial strategy they will be pursuing with regards to the cleanup of the Gowanus Canal Superfund site. In the 105-page decision, with over 3,000 pages of supporting documentation, the EPA outlines the general dredge and cap process they intend to use along the canal. They also said they intend to include aggressive measures to mitigate the Combined Sewer Overflow (CSO) issues, specifically by forcing the City to build tanks at two locations near the highest volume outfalls to retain CSO solids. Lastly, the EPA will not be using Red Hook for a Confined Disposal Facility, one of the other controversial elements of their proposed remedy. Now that the ROD has been issued, they will be spending the next 2 years designing the actual remedies. This is where we’ll actually get into the planning for elements like how the dredged materials will be transported and where they will set up staging and processing areas. The dirty work is still ahead of us. We have encouraged the EPA to work with our district office as a good way for them to obtain insight and help keep the community informed. He thanked Board Member Mark Shames our Environmental Protection Committee Chair and our District Manager Craig Hammerman who were there to represent us, and also acknowledged Board Members Glenn Kelly, Gary Reilly and Ariel Krasnow were also in attendance.

Chairperson Kummer encouraged all of our members to consider attending the upcoming meeting of our Economic/Waterfront/Community Development & Housing Committee where we will be hearing from the consultant who has been working on our Gowanus Brownfield Opportunity Area program grant. At this meeting you’ll have a chance to learn about what we’ve discovered about the Gowanus, why it has become such a hotspot for artisans, craftspeople and artists, new media and new manufacturers, what the challenges are that the area is currently facing, and where there are some strategic sites that could make a difference in the area. The committee is meeting at 6:30pm on Monday, October 21st at PS 58, on Carroll Street at Smith Street in Carroll Gardens.

Chairperson Kummer advised the Board that in July we co-sponsored an Informational Meeting with the Park Slope Civic Council regarding New York Methodist Hospital’s new, state-of-the-art facility on an assemblage of properties they own which front on 5th Street, 8th Avenue and 6th Street. At the meeting Methodist representatives were able to share their concepts with us and gather some early feedback from the community. The hospital returned to our Landmarks/Land Use Committee at their September 26th meeting to present us with the most current version of their project plans which incorporated community feedback. They have redistributed bulk from the building away from the nearby residential uses and eliminated the proposed traffic pass-through from 6th to 5th Streets. We are anticipating receiving their Board of Standards and Appeals application any day now, so we can finalize the planning for a Public Hearing.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that James Proscia, BK6 Superintendent has retired and is being replaced by Superintendent Nando Sala. He further reported that the Department of Sanitation will be instituting their annual shift to night collection hours in mid-November, continuing through mid-April, to ensure that personnel are available around the clock in the event of emergency snow removal. Each year one of the five Sanitation sectors within the district is shifted to nighttime collection. This year Sector 62 which encompasses Cobble Hill, North Carroll Gardens, and the Columbia Street areas of our district will receive night collections. Also, the Commanding officer of the 76th Police Precinct Deputy Inspector Jeff Schiff was transferred to a Queens command and our new commander is Captain Justin Lenz who will be introducing himself and talking about his strategies, priorities and initiatives for the precinct at our October 17th Transportation/Public Safety committee meeting.

District Manager Hammerman informed the board that the district office has been recently awarded a $29,250 grant from the State Archives department. This grant will enable us to take all of our Land Use records, digitize them and put them on microfiche. In a future grant we envision applying for next year we hope to take those records and create an online content management system making it publicly accessible on the Internet.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by introducing the new Planning Fellow in the district office Mia Brezin. She will be working with us this year on a special project with a cutting-edge firm called Open Plans (the organization that created the DOT crowdsourcing tool that was used to acquire the bike share program docking station location suggestions). They are working with us to develop a Capital budget mapping tool that will allow us to gather information from the public at-large who will be able to make suggestions to us in regards to our budget requests.
