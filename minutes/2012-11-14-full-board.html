CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by reminding everyone that this year's annual holiday party is going to be held on Monday, December 17th from 6-9pm, at the Sheep Station on the corner of 4th Avenue and Douglass Street. The cost is $30 per person and we will get hors d'oeuvres and non-alcoholic beverages included. This event is also an end-of-the-year fundraiser for our non-profit organization so tax-deductible donations above the $30 are encouraged. To that end we usually have a raffle of prizes that board members collect and donate for that purpose. Please let District Manager Craig Hammerman know if you'd be willing to solicit raffle prizes and help work at the event itself. This is a great opportunity for us to socialize, and I'd highly recommend that everyone make a special point of attending especially our newer board members.

Chairperson Kummer reported that Hurricane Sandy recovery efforts are still underway in our district. As of today there are still a few buildings without electricity in Red Hook Houses and all of Gowanus Houses has been restored. The tidal surges experienced in our community had a devastating effect on some of our businesses, many of whom are not fully insured and are wondering how they will be able to continue. Thankfully, the flow of information from our district office, from our local elected officials - particularly Council Member Lander's office - has enabled us to plan, act and react accordingly. The tremendous outpouring of volunteer efforts and donations - contributions of time, talent and resources - has been nothing short of extraordinary. While the experience is still fresh in our minds, I've asked our Economic/Waterfront/Community Development & Housing Committee to host a discussion on the hurricane impacts and particularly the responses we saw from our public agencies and organizations so that we could put together some recommendations in areas that can be improved. For example, it took NYCHA over one week to have information posted on their website about which of their buildings was without electricity, heat and elevators. Clearly, they could have done a much better job managing the public's expectations if they were faster getting information out. I would encourage anyone who is interested in participating in this discussion to come to their next meeting which will be at 6:30pm on Monday, November 19th at SUNY-Downstate LICH, Atlantic Avenue & Hicks Street.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by advising the board that at last month's meeting we discussed the developing issue regarding a proposal to establish a 170-bed single adult male shelter at 165 West 9th Street in Carroll Gardens. We held an extremely well-attended meeting on October 24th in the largest venue we could find in Carroll Gardens and it was a standing room only event. He thanked our Human Services Co-Chairs, Benjamin Solotaire and Paige Bellenbaum who made sure our meeting was both informative and orderly since this issue has evoked a strong emotional reaction by many neighbors. We will be hearing from them when they give their committee report, but I thought you should know that as a result of Hurricane Sandy, another contracted shelter facility at Borden Avenue in Long Island City was affected so on Tuesday, November 6th the Department of Homeless Services temporarily relocated 120 homeless male veterans into the W 9th Street building. We have been told that they expect to be there "less than a month" while the Borden Avenue facility 2

is under repair, and the department is in the process of setting up a Community Advisory Committee meeting for some time next week.

2nd Vice Chair Lou Sones stated that the outpouring of volunteers to help with the restoration of Red Hook after Hurricane Sandy from Red Hook Initiative, National Guard, Community Board 6 district, and neighbors has been extraordinary. There are still families in Red Hook Houses without heat and hot water and very spotty electricity. There are many seniors and sick people who cannot get up and down in the buildings. Red Hook GAGS is looking for donations to hire a person to visit 30 families for the next two weeks who have medical conditions to check on them and bring water, food and medication.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the board that the Lower East Side Ecology Center's electronic waste recycling program is a unique and innovative program allowing residents to dispose of working and non-working electronics in an environmentally responsible way. Their e-waste warehouse is located at 469 President Street (at Nevins Street) in our district. Anyone can drop-off unwanted electronics for free. If your equipment works, let them know and they will try to reuse it, if not they will recycle it. They will guarantee data security. Drop-off hours are as follows: Tuesdays, Thursdays and Fridays – 10am-5pm; Wednesdays 12pm-5pm; Saturdays 10am-4pm. They will be hosting an electronic waste (e-waste) recycling event at P.S. 321,180 7th Avenue between 1st and 2nd Streets in Park Slope on December 8th from 10am-4pm.

District Manager Hammerman reported that the SLA public hearing for Kemistry Entertainment Group LLC located at 260 Flatbush Avenue in the Park Slope section of our district has been rescheduled for November 21 at 11am. The hearing will take place at 317 Lenox Avenue in Manhattan.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that the Department of Transportation has sent us a list of approved CityBench sites in our district. The CityBench program is a three-year federally-funded initiative to increase the amount of public seating on New York City’s streets, particularly for elderly and mobility-impaired individuals. The locations in our district are as follows: 7 Wolcott Street, 315 4th Avenue, 248-250 4th Avenue, 35 4th Avenue, and 250 9th Street.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by informing the Board that some of the messages that we were sending regarding the storm on the various social media tools were helpful in responding to the hurricane. It allowed everyone to disseminate accurate and timely information to residents and business owners regarding hazardous conditions, street closures, service changes, the need for volunteers, etc. In a normal week Community Board 6 has about 900-1,200 constituents who receive our Facebook postings or tweets. During the entire week of the hurricane that number jumped to 25,000, an increase of over 2000%. This shows that social media is a powerful tool to get information out there in the district. We at the district office would like to have a focus group discussion to develop a comprehensive plan for using the various social media tools. It would be useful to have a number of people to talk to inside, and outside, of the board to discuss this topic. If you are interested in participating in this discussion please let our District Manager know.

YOUTH/HUMAN SERVICES/EDUCATION COMMITTEE

………..……JAMES BERNARD, YOUTH SERVICES CO-CHAIR

............BENJAMIN SOLOTAIRE, HUMAN SERVICES CO-CHAIR

.…...…….PAIGE BELLENBAUM, HUMAN SERVICES CO-CHAIR

………………….……..MARK KOLMAN, EDUCATION CO-CHAIR

..….…………..………SUZANNE TURET, EDUCATION CO-CHAIR

Human Services Co-Chairpersons Paige Bellenbaum and Benjamin Solotaire both reported to the Board that the committee reviewed an application for the use of 165 West 9th Street as a 170 bed shelter for single adult males.

Motion: by Board Member Richard Bashner, seconded by Board Member Peter Fleming; Recommendation to oppose the use of 165 West 9th Street as a 170-bed shelter for single adult males, and call upon the Department of Homeless Services to reject the application based on the lack of both process and merits.

Board Member Hildegaard Link stated that she has had direct experience with Aguila Inc., she has done a lot of lighting and mechanical inspections and find their facilities to be fairly restrictive. Quite frankly she is horrified at the restraint of personal freedom that the residents experience. It seems as though there is some degree of security and structure, but having said that she would not want this facility near her.

Board Member Dolly Williams asked if the men would come from the surrounding area. Human Service Co- Chairperson Bellenbaum stated no, they would be referred via the intake center in Manhattan.

Board Member Noel Berk-Rauch spoke in favor of the motion stating that this vote is only advisory because we do not have a formal role to play in this process. It was also brought up at the committee meeting that there was alleged conflict of interest between the landlord and the housing group. For that reason the Board should take a more pro-active and aggressive role in this process.

Board Member Roger Rigolli asked if the homeless men would be working. Chairperson Kummer responded that they would be employable, and some would be working.

Board Member Neena Dutta stated that we should be also sending a letter to the NYS Office of Temporary and Disability Assistance (OTDA). District Manager Hammerman stated that if the motion passes we will be sending a letter to all City and State agencies that are involved in this decision.

Board Member Judith Thompson stated that the current lot is 45ft wide by 100ft deep with a floor area ratio (FAR) of 3. The maximum size of building that should be there is 13,500 square feet. At the meeting they mentioned that the current size of the building is 25,000 square feet. She does not see how it can be legal to have a building that large. She also asked where the beds will be placed in the facility and it was stated that they would be placed all over the building not just in the apartments but also in the commercial space. How can this be a wise use of the City’s money to pay $110 a night to house men in a dormitory-style facility. Also, how can this be a safe environment for men because some of the men could have a criminal background since they do not do background checks. 5

Board Member Suzanne Turet stated that when the members created the motion it was implicit that we had major concerns with the process. We also wanted to bring out that there were going to be too many men in that space and that they should not be subjected to inhumane conditions.

Board Member Monique Verret stated that short of over-crowdedness these homeless men will be employable and hopefully will receive some training so they would be working. She understands that there are a number of facilities in our district but we must not lose sight of the concerns of those less fortunate than us that need these services.

Board Member Rigolli stated that we as a board should really be asking pertinent questions about the homeless men quality of living in this shelter.

Board Member Sr. Rosanna Cervone asked about the ratio between men to showers. She does not believe that 15 men per shower is a good ratio.

VOTE: 31 YEAS, 1 NAY, 3 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED

Chairperson Kummer recognized Assembly Member Felix Ortiz who gave an overview of the cleanup in Red Hook since Hurricane Sandy. He first thanked everyone for their donations and especially for volunteering. He stated that 90% of electricity has been restored to NYCHA developments. There are still some buildings without heat or hot water. They will be installing three generators and by Saturday all buildings will have electricity and heat and hot water. He is working with the Governor, Mayor, FEMA and the Secretary of Labor to get the necessary resources to Red Hook residents as well as the business that have been destroyed. He is arranging for the Secretary of the Commerce to come to Red Hook and speak to the business owners. He invited everyone to come to Borough Hall next week where there will be a summit to discuss the response to the hurricane by various agencies and utility companies. One of the reasons why it’s taking so long to get everything in working order is because so many boilers were destroyed. There will be a march tomorrow to let NYCHA know it is taking too long to get the vital services back to the residents. He ended by informing the Board of the passing of Angela Wardlaw long-time representative from his office.

Senator Kevin Parker thanked all the board members for their support to get him elected. He stated that he will fight not to raise the taxes for the middle class. Some of the issues he will be working on are hydrofracking and minimum wage to name two. He introduced his representative that will be attending our meetings, Peter Weiss. He ended by thanking the Board for all they do in serving the residents of the district.

Dan Wiley, representing Congress Member Nydia Velázquez, stated that the Congresswoman feels the disaster relief money for small businesses should be a grant not a loan.
