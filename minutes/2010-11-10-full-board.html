DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT……………..................................................…CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the board that the Department of Transportation has determined that a speed hump on 8th Street between 7th and 8th Avenues is unsuitable at this time because of the number and placement of utilities on the street.

District Manager Hammerman informed the Board that the Department of Buildings (DOB) has advised us of emergency conditions at 341 Sackett Street and 108 5th Avenue. DOB has informed the property owners that the premises have to be made safe by demolition, repair, sealing or whatever means necessary to protect public safety.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that Transportation Alternatives will be hosting a summit to address the pervasive effect speeding has on myriad aspects of New Yorkers’ lives. This full-day summit will bring together elected officials and leaders in the fields of transportation policy, public health, medicine, traffic safety and enforcement to discuss the extent and impact of speed-related crashes and best practices in enforcement and policing. The summit will be held on November 19th at 295 Lafayette Street in Manhattan.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the board that on October 18th the Department of Design and Construction (DDC) began a distinctive sidewalk contract in our district. They will be replacing tinted concrete and bluestone sidewalks at select locations. This contract is site specific for violations already issued. For more information, please call the district office.

District Manager Hammerman informed the board that on November 18th the Department of Transportation (DOT) in conjunction with Congresswoman Nydia M. Velázquez will have a community meeting to kick-off the start of a five-month study to determine the feasibility of a streetcar network linking Red Hook with Downtown Brooklyn and surrounding neighborhoods. This initial analysis is the first step in determining if this mode of transportation is a viable option to connect the residents and businesses of the rapidly growing Red Hook neighborhood with Brooklyn's broader transportation system and support economic development.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that on October 26th community members, representatives from elected officials and government agencies gathered for the first meeting of the Gowanus Canal Superfund Community Advisory Group (CAG). Organized by the EPA, this group will be responsible for bridging the gap between the EPA, who will be performing the Gowanus Canal cleanup, and neighborhood groups who need to know what is going on. While the CAG has no direct authority, it is the consensus of public opinion the CAG provides that can influence decisions during the long cleanup process.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the board that on November 4th he attended the first meeting of the Atlantic Yards Service Cabinet at Brooklyn Borough Hall. The meeting was hosted by the Brooklyn Borough President and Council Member Letitia James with representatives from the three effected Community Boards, City and State agencies, other elected officials offices, Forest City Ratner and Empire State Development Corporation (ESDC). The cabinet is expected to meet quarterly to discuss construction progress, parking, and traffic.

2

District Manager Hammerman informed the board that he attended a scoping meeting with the Parks Department to discuss the use of a $1.7 million grant from Council Member Sara Gonzalez for Coffey Park in the Red Hook section of our district. The budget will be used primarily to reconstruct the paths at the north end of the park, add additional shrubbery/plantings around the perimeter, and perhaps some additional lighting. Once they have advanced to a design they will be making a public presentation to our Parks Committee.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that for the next two weekends 10th Street from Hamilton to 3rd Avenue including the intersection of 2nd Avenue and 10th Street will be closed due to crane operations and the MTA contractor transporting equipment to be used for the rehabilitation of the Culver Viaduct.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the board that the Landmarks/Land Use Committee meeting has been rescheduled for December 2nd due to the Thanksgiving holiday.

District Manager Hammerman informed the board that the Department of Sanitation instituted their annual shift to night collection hours in mid-November, continuing through mid-April, to ensure that personnel are available 24 hours a day in the event that they are needed for emergency snow removal. Each year one of the five sanitation sectors within the district is shifted to nighttime collection. This year Sector 65 which encompasses South Park Slope and Gowanus from 6th to 15th Streets and Prospect Park West to the Canal will receive night collections.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by announcing that on Saturday, November 11th there will be a Veterans Day Ceremony in Carroll Park to honor our American Veterans for their patriotism, love of country and willingness to serve and sacrifice.

YOUTH/HUMAN SERVICES/EDUCATION COMMITTEE

….....…DERRICK C. VITO, YOUTH SERVICES CO-CHAIR

………..JAMES BERNARD, YOUTH SERVICES CO-CHAIR

……………..MARK KOLMAN, HUMAN SERVICES CHAIR

……………………SUZANNE TURET, CHAIR, EDUCATION

Youth Services Co-Chairperson James Bernard reported to the Board that the committee reviewed City Council Intro 332 a local law to amend the Administrative Code of the City of New York, in relation to prohibiting smoking in pedestrian plazas and public parks.

Motion: by Board Member Suzanne Turet, seconded by Board Member Nica Lalli; Recommendation to support City Council Intro 332, a local law to amend the Administrative Code of the City of New York, in relation to prohibiting smoking in pedestrian plazas and public parks and to repeal subdivision b of section 17-513 of the Administrative Code, in relation to requiring a study regarding the prevention of second-hand smoke circulation in restaurants.

Board Member Sones spoke out against the motion stating that this is a slippery slope and this amendment goes too far.

Board member David Reiss stated that banning smoking indoors is good because there is a risk of second hand smoke. He then asked if there was any studies done that would prove scientifically the effects of second hand smoke outside. Board Member Sones stated that there is.

Board Member Elizabeth Shipley stated that there is no way to determine the effects of out door smoking in all cases because factors such as proximity and wind come into play. She further stated that this amendment also goes a bit too far and would support a ban in children’s play areas.

Board Member Mark Shames spoke out against the motion stating that this issue should have been given to the Health [sic] committee.

Board Member Roger Rigolli spoke out against the motion stating that smoking outside in an open area should be allowed.

9

Board Member Anthony Pugliese stated that all smokers should stay in their homes and we need to do something about the smokers who hang out in front of bars, especially on Smith Street because their smoke enters resident’s windows.

Board Member Monique Verret stated that this amendment should be focused on protecting children’s health, so they should change it to ban smoking in playgrounds, sports activity areas and any other areas where children congregate.

Board Member Norman Cox spoke in favor of the motion stating that they should focus on public parks since there are already other rules posted like radio playing, etc.

Board Member Noel Berk-Rauch stated that smoking indoors should be banned. Outdoors someone could walk away from a person who is smoking.

Board Member Levine spoke in favor of the motion stating that we should also be looking at the some from barbeque pits.

Board Member Suzanne Turet stated that it is wrong to ban smoking outdoors; it could be construed as discrimination.

Board Member Burzio spoke out against the motion stating that it is excessive.

Board Member Reilly spoke in favor of the motion stating that it distracts from the experience of being outside, especially with children in parks and beaches.

Motion: by Board Member Glenn Kelly, seconded by Board Member Raymond Lohier; Recommit for further consideration

VOTE: 18 YEAS, 11 NAYS, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED

TIME: 8:25 P.M.
