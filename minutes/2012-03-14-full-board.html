CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by informing the board that 3 of our members have opted not to reapply for Board Membership – specifically, Linda Blyer, David Reiss and Derrick Vito. The Chairperson thanked all of them for their exemplary, selfless service to our communities. Their presence at our general meetings will be missed, and their contributions to the board will not be forgotten.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that in response to the concerns raised by various board and committee members, he’s taken a long, hard look at the challenge of how best to rebalance the workload of our Public Safety/Environmental Protection and Permits & License Committee to free up more space for our Public Safety and Environmental Protection items. The Chair, with the consent of the Executive Committee, has reorganized the committee as follows: Public Safety will merge with Transportation and Environmental Protection/Permits & License will remain together. The new committee configuration will be reflected in the Committee Membership Applications that we distribute in June and will formally take effect September 1st for the 2012-2013 meeting session.

Chairperson Kummer advised the board that in response to the motion we adopted last month regarding the proposed community garden at Columbia and Sackett Streets, District Manager Craig Hammerman has spoken to a representative from HPD’s Brooklyn Planning Office who did confirm that this lot was slated to be developed under their New Foundations RFP that was issued several years ago but the City has put those plans on hold indefinitely because they do not have the money for development in their budget at this time. The department also confirmed that they did not foresee that budget becoming available in the immediate future either. The representative made it clear that his agency is in the business of building housing but given that they will not be able to advance this project they suggested that it would be more beneficial to have a positive, active community-engaging use like a community garden than a vacant lot which the City would be challenged to maintain until development plans could move ahead. He was also clear that the terms of any community garden lease are month-to-month and that they have exercised their development rights in the past when they have had money to build on community garden sites.

Chairperson Kummer invited everyone to tomorrow night’s Transportation Committee meeting that will be run by the Department of Transportation on the Bike Share plan for our district. Bike Share is the program being rolled out by the City this summer that will involve setting up 600 bicycle docking stations throughout Manhattan and portions of Brooklyn, to make 10,000 bicycles available for rent. The City is not paying for the program. The company that will be operating Bike Share under contract with the City will be funding it themselves, primarily through advertising revenue. This company has already operated successful Bike Share programs in Washington DC, Boston, London, and other major cities around the world. There will be a modest annual membership fee, then for rides that last longer than either 30- or 45-minutes, a small hourly charge will be billed to the rider. DOT had come to our committee several months ago to introduce the program concept, and said they could come back to engage the community in a discussion about where the docking stations would be located. Tomorrow night’s meeting will offer us, and the community-at-large, an opportunity to weigh in directly with the agency over where we think the docking stations should and should not be located.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by informing the Board that next month, at 6pm on April 12th at our office at 250 Baltic Street, we will be holding our annual Bar & Restaurant meeting which is an open forum designed to educate the community and business owners about the rules that govern the operation of bars and restaurants. We have been hosting this annual meeting for many years now and have found it to be an excellent way to promote better relationships between bar/restaurant owners and their neighbors by making sure that everyone knows what the rules are. We will have representatives from the State Liquor Authority, local 2

precincts and every other city agency that plays a role in regulating these types of businesses. The meeting is not designed to focus on specific problems at specific locations but rather to broadly educate the community on the roles that the various agencies play in helping to balance a thriving and competitive business environment without unduly sacrificing the community’s quality of life in the process.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the board that the recent story reported in the New York Post stating that the NYPD has chosen the 78th Precinct to police the Barclays Center when it opens in September, bypassing the 88th Precinct is not true. No decisions have been made and the police are unable to identify who leaked the information to the press. CB’s 2, 6 & 8, the Police Department, the Borough President's office, the Mayor's office and City Council will all be involved in the discussion, but ultimately it is up to Police Commissioner Ray Kelly to put forward a recommendation to the Mayor. The Mayor will make the final decision, consulting with the City Council. We should be receiving a decision sometime in July.

District Manager Craig Hammerman reported to the board that he sent a letter to the United States Postal Service expressing the Boards objections to the closing of the small postal station at 257 Columbia Street. He is hopeful that we will get a written response soon.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by advising the board that New York City Transit will not reopen the under-construction Smith-9th Street station until the fall at the earliest, even though they had originally scheduled to reopen it by the end of March 2012.

Chairperson Kummer asked if anyone is interested in chairing the “Cultural Affairs” committee component of our Parks/Recreation/Cultural Affairs committee please contact Chairperson Kummer or District Manager Hammerman.

PARKS/RECREATION/CULTURAL AFFAIRS COMMITTEE……...............NICA LALLI, CO-CHAIR

Parks Committee Co-Chairperson Nica Lalli reported to the Board that the committee again reviewed the plan to rehabilitate the Red Hook football/soccer field and to support the proposed schedule for the allocation of field use time.

Motion: Committee;

Recommendation to reiterate support for the plan to rehabilitate the Red Hook football/soccer field and to support the proposed schedule for the allocation of field use time with the following conditions:  15 hours of prime time – Monday thru Friday – 3 to 6pm for fall and spring schedule.  3 hours on Saturdays and/or Sundays for 18 hours of play or scrimmage time during both seasons.

Board Member Robert Levine asked how long this will agreement last and who will be responsible for the maintenance on the field. Parks Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated 15 years and Parks will be responsible for the maintenance of the field.

Board Member Gregory O’Connell asked if the City paid for the renovations how much would it cost. Parks Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated $6 million.

Board Member Judith Thompson spoke out against the motion asking how much Xavier was investing in the rehabilitation. Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated that we did not have a figure, but the work will be done to the standards/requirements of the Parks Department.

Board Member Douglas Giuliano asked how many weeks out the year will Xavier be using the field. Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated that they will be using it 18 hrs a week for the spring and fall.

Board Member Elsie Felder asked what other organizations were at the meeting. Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated that we have heard from the Titans, Red Hook Rise, Parks Department, Xavier, Red Hook soccer leagues – they have all participated at meetings. 5

Board Member Richard Bashner asked how much is Xavier investing in the rehabilitation and what is the difference in the number of hours they will get once the field is complete. Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated Xavier was committing $3 million to this project and they would get only 3 additional hours out of the agreement.

Board Member Paige Bellenbaum asked if the community would have access to the track while Xavier is using the field. Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated only during practices, not games.

Board Member Mark Shames stated that with the addition of lights to the field it will increase the amount of hours the community can use it. He then asked if the lights were in Phase 1 of the project. Committee Co- Chairperson Lalli stated yes.

Board Member Sayar Lonial asked if Xavier was required to use union labor on this project and if they could get more hours than the agreement at a later date. Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated that they did not have to use union labor and it would be up to the Parks Department through their normal application process, whether Xavier could get additional hours down the road.

Board Member Debra Scotto stated that we have to make sure the lights are part of the deal. If they do not install lights for nighttime play them we should not support the proposal.

Board Member Mark Shames asked if the field could be used for anything other than sports. Committee Co- Chairperson Lalli stated that it could but with the existing demand by sports teams it is highly unlikely.

Board Member Richard Bashner asked if Xavier was going to be paying for the electricity for the lights. Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated no, the City will pick up the cost.

Amendment: by Board Member Robert Levine, seconded by Board Member Dolly Williams; To include in the conditions that Xavier be given additional hours on the field only if no other youth organization want it.

Board Member Glenn Kelly spoke out against the amendment stating that it is not the Boards place to tell the Parks Department how to do business.

Board Member Bashner spoke in favor of the amendment.

Board Member Hildegaard Link asked if this was one football field and one track. Committee Co-Chairperson Lalli stated yes.

Board Member Lou Sones spoke in favor of the amendment stating that they should be placed at the bottom of the list after the initial 18 hours.

VOTE: 20 YEAS, 10 NAYS, 0 ABSTENTIONS

AMENDMENT PASSED

Chairperson Kummer recognized Council Member Steven Levin.

Council Member Steven Levin stated that he is working with the Park Slow Civic Council on creating neighborhood slow zones, a community-based program from the city's Department of Transportation that reduces the speed limit to 20 mph (down from 30 mph, the citywide speed limit) within a select area and adds safety measures to change driver behavior and help improve safety. He has requested 2-3 slow zones in his district. He is advocating for the inclusion of the North Slope in the historical district. LPC should be voting on this issue very soon. He will also be advocating the restoration of Community Board’s monies in the upcoming budget. The Councilman is working on a bill that will require DEP to provide real time information regarding Combined Sewer Overflow (CSO) discharges. Lastly, he would like to get NYPD’s Accident Investigation Squad (AIS) to investigate all serious physical injuries, between bikes and motorist; Right now AIS only investigates if someone is killed. He wants the City to use NYS standards for investigating such accidents.
