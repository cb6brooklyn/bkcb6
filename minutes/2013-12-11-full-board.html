CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by congratulating our Public Safety Committee Chair Matthew Silverman and his wife, Rachel, who gave birth to a son – Adam Jay Silverman – on November 24th.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that on Tuesday, December 3rd District Manager Hammerman and I attended a neighborhood forum on Street Safety in Park Slope, the kick-off meeting of a new group called Park Slope Street Safety Partnership which includes the neighborhood’s elected officials, DOT, 78th Precinct, the Community Board, Park Slope Civic Council and Park Slope Neighbors. The group’s purpose is to capture information about unsafe street conditions in the neighborhood and to lobby for increased safety measures called for by Mayor-elect de Blasio in his “Vision Zero” campaign which envisions reducing if not eliminating fatalities and injuries caused by crashes. I’d like to commend the group, particularly Council Member Brad Lander who played an important organizing role, for raising awareness and engaging the community. The Park Slope United Methodist Church was a packed house demonstrating that this issue is foremost on people’s minds.

Chairperson Kummer advised the board that Council Member Lander held his first public meeting called “Bridging Gowanus” which invited all of the people and groups having an interest in the Gowanus Canal corridor into a room for an open and honest discussion about the future of the neighborhood. The process has been facilitated by Pratt, which has a long history of involvement in the area, and is intended to produce a current position paper to present to the new Administration so they will have a good roadmap for the issues and concerns related to the Gowanus. Rather than run from controversy, this group is confronting some very tough questions, not with the expectation that they will all be resolved, but so they could be framed out and presented to the new Administration.

Chairperson Kummer reminded everyone that the holiday party is going to be from 6-9pm on Tuesday, December 17th at The Red Rose Restaurant, 315 Smith Street in Carroll Gardens. We’ve already lined up some generous sponsorship from IKEA, Astoria Federal Savings Bank, and the Law Office of Sclar Adler LLP, but we are still looking for prize donations for the raffle. Small, easy-to-carry items like gift certificates, tend to work well. I’m encouraging all of our members to either solicit or donate a prize so we’ll have lots of prizes to give away. Also, I’m told that our guests who prepay will greatly speed up the entry process at the door so if you have a check with you tonight, feel free to give it to Craig or Leroy so we can add you to the list. As a reminder, we’re asking everyone to pay what they can, typically somewhere in the $40-100 range, but more is even better. Checks made out to Friends of Brooklyn CB6, Inc. are tax deductible to the extent allowed by law.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that we have received a resignation from Board Member Greg O’Connell Jr. He wished him well and thanked him for his selfless service to the Community Board.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by updating the board on the status of the variance application of New York Methodist Hospital which is before them. The hospital has made an application to the BSA to build an ambulatory care facility on 5th Street-8th Avenue-6th Street. There are actually 3 sets of zoning rules that are involved in this application. When the rezoning of Park Slope was done in 2003 the hospital’s campus was largely carved out causing it to retain mostly its R6 zoning status. The hospital is proposing to build a U-shaped building in which the bottom half is within the R6 zoning and the upper two arms of the building are in R6B and R7B zoning areas. At the November 21st Transportation/Public Safety and Landmarks/Land Use Public Hearing, hospital representatives made a presentation in which they stated that it was exploring possible changes to the design in the R6B and R7B sections on the building. Unfortunately, they did not provide details on the proposed changes that night. As a result the committees did not have complete clarity on what they were 2

asked to vote on that night. There were many residents at the meeting speaking for and against the proposal, most were against, with concerns about the bulk and general appropriateness of the proposed building in the Park Slope neighborhood. They also were concerned about traffic and environmental impact. Those speaking in favor of the application included a number of our elected officials and their representatives, as well as several local clergy members all of whom stressed the hospital’s need to expand in order to survive. After the hearing the committee asked the hospital representatives if they were willing to withdraw or delay its application to allow for provision of more information to the community board and further discussion of the design changes that the hospital had alluded to in its presentation. The hospital was unwilling that evening to withdraw or delay its application. The Landmarks/Land Use Committee did pass the following motion as a recommendation to the board which is before us tonight. The motion is that Brooklyn CB6 disapproves the New York Methodist Hospital's Center for Community Health application since the proposed building would alter the essential character of the neighborhood and is not the minimum relief that could be requested. Further, that New York Methodist work with the Park Slope residents, the area's elected officials and the Community Board to amend the variance request in such a way that would reduce the bulk in the R6B and R7B portions much closer to compliance with the zoning limitations, if not fully into compliance. Following the hearing there were communications between the hospital, Community Board 6 and Councilman Lander in which the hospital agreed to do the following: 1. The hospital would return to the committee in early January to make a further presentation on the following three subjects:  A detailed explanation of the need for a building of the configuration that makes necessary the proposed variances. This would include a description of the functions in the building, their current locations and the projected growth in the near future. The hospital would present the relevant standards and guidelines with respect to size and adjacency for the new facility. The community board requested that they provide us with the information that led them to conclude not to build over the parking lot behind the Rite Aid.  The traffic impact of the development as compared to the present traffic conditions with traffic counts and statistics.  Detailed changes to the building in the R6B and R7B sections.

2. The hospital would write a letter to BSA concerning the fact that extending the community board process would result in our not getting our recommendation to BSA within the normal 60-day allotted period. The BSA does have discretion under its rules explicitly to include late filed recommendations from community boards in its hearing record on an application. The hospitals letter would specifically ask BSA to exercise that discretion in this case to allow our recommendation to be included in the record even if we get it in after the 60 days.

Since the hospital agreed to the above in writing, Chairperson Kummer asked for a motion to recommit.

Motion: by Board Member Peter Fleming, seconded by Board Member Roger Rigolli; To recommit matter to Committee.

VOTE: 31 YEAS, 0 NAYS 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED UNANIMOUSLY

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the board that the Department of Transportation has sent us a list of approved CityBench sites in our district. The purpose of the three-year,

3

federally funded CityBench program is to install benches throughout New York City to support transit use and walking, particularly for elderly and mobility-impaired New Yorkers. If anyone has any concerns with these locations, please let him know before January 17th. Following are the locations: 610 Baltic Street, 211 8th Street, 426 4th Avenue and 504 4th Avenue.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that the community board has been advised of a demolition project at 388 Carroll Street in the Gowanus section of our district.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by reporting to the board that the district office has been contacted by the representatives of The Lightstone Group who is constructing a large-scale project at 363-365 Bond Street. They would like to meet with us on a monthly basis to go over coordination of construction activities at the site since they would be planning this over a 24-month construction cycle. Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch and I met with them last week and we will be meeting with them again in January. What you should expect to see happening at the site between now and the end of the year is a lot of site preparation work which includes, but is not limited to, the removal of asbestos containing materials and demolition of current structures on the site. They will be putting a gate up at the Bond and 1st Streets intersection where that will become the primary access point for the construction site since the project is bifurcated by 1st Street. That should not impact any other active businesses in the area. The 2nd Street work will allow ultimately for the construction of the Sponge Park, which is a separate project that the City is funding at the foot of 2nd Street at the canal. It will also involve the design and installation of a formal launch for small watercraft that will be primarily used by the Gowanus Dredgers and friends. The Carroll Street end of the property is not expected to have any kind of major impacts during construction, which is good since that is one of the few east/west crossings of the canal. In January we have invited DOT and the NYPD to sit down with us and The Lightstone Group to discuss truck route movement and traffic regulation changes that will be a part of this construction package. As we continue to learn information we will let you know and put it in our newsletter as appropriate. We have also encouraged The Lightstone Group to develop their own newsletter format so that they too can start posting this information and letting the community know what’s going on. They will also in the near future be establishing a hotline which will be monitored daily and will take messages after hours so that people that have concerns will have a direct way of getting access to the people at the site.

 COMMUNITY SESSION 

Chris Hammett, resident from Red Hook, thanked the board for its vote on the proposed private school located at 556 Columbia Street. He asks the board to take a look at the 197a plan that this board created and follow its precepts when discussion anything in Red Hook. He encourages to board to be partners with the community in regards to all development in the area.

Pierre Devand, representing the Brooklyn Chamber of Commerce, stated that in July of 2013 the Governor announced contract awards for the New York State Department of Health's In-Person Assistor/Navigator program, which will serve the state as the primary facilitator of enrollment in health insurance through the newly established Health Benefit Exchange. The Brooklyn Alliance was awarded the contract to provide these services in Brooklyn to both individuals and small businesses. For more information visit http://www.ibrooklyn.com/business_support/ACA_IPA_Navigator_Program.aspx.

Marvin Ciporen, Park Slope resident, thanked the Board and New York Methodist for postponing the process so further conversation can continue with the community. He further stated that he hopes NYM will release the proposed changes to the original designs prior to the January meeting so the community has an opportunity to review them and better be prepared for discussion at that meeting.

Lyn Hill, representing NY Methodist Hospital, thanked the board for agreeing to defer their recommendation until further discussion could be had by all parties. NY Methodist believes this project will be important in providing first class healthcare to Brooklynites in years to come and they will provide the additional information about the proposal that the board has requested.

Phaedra Thomas, consultant to the Gowanus Bay Terminal, thanked the board for its vote on the proposed private school located at 556 Columbia Street. She further stated that one of Red Hooks largest employers (JoFaz school bus Company) is right down the block from this proposed site and they employ 700 individuals, with 200 coming from the Red Hook Houses. These are the exact jobs that will be displaced if we continue to erode our IBZ. All of the properties around the site are occupied even the site itself is being used to park trucks.

 NEW/OTHER BUSINESS 

To purchase tickets go to http://smithstreetstage.org/Smith_Street_Stage/Welcome.html. He also invited everyone the Christmas tree lighting ceremony at Carroll Park on December 13th at 6pm.
