COMMISSION ON HUMAN RIGHTS

Presentation and discussion with a representative for the Commission on Human Rights on the mission, programs and services offered by the agency.

The presentation was made by Paula L. Sanders, Deputy Director for the Community Service Center in Brooklyn.

- Each borough has one

- Law enforcement agency under the Mayor

- Strongest human rights laws in the country

- Jurisdiction in housing, employment, public accommodation — anyplace providing goods or services to the public.

Protected Classes: Gender (includes sexual harassment), Color, Race, Creed (religion), Alienage (citizenship), Age, Marital status, Nationality, Partnership status, Disability, Sexual orientation.

Familial Status (children, housing). Arrest and conviction record (employment). Lawful occupation (housing). Lawful source of income (housing). Status of domestic violence victims/survivors.

- Income is not a protected class

- Takes on cases by case basis, not class action suits.

Housing — jurisdiction for apartments of 6 floors or more or 2-3 family homes.

Employers — it is illegal in NYC to ask if you've been arrested. Can ask if you've been convicted, however.

Agency sends out testers to make sure employers and housing authority are being fair.

Judge decides based on merits of complaint.

No jurisdiction over NYPD — no "stop and frisk" control.

No jurisdiction under NYCHA (NYC Housing Authority).

Last year, the City Council added cyber-bullying to the list of protected classes.

Discrimination complaints can be filed either with the city or the state but the city covers more categories.

Commission can go after other city agencies but not NYPD or NYCHA.

As of June 11th, another protected class is being added — Unemployment status. It is illegal for employers to not hire if someone had been unemployed for a year or more.
