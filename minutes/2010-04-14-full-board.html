CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……………………………...……..………….....…...RICHARD S. BASHNER

Chairperson Richard S. Bashner began his report by informing the Board that since our last meeting, we've lost yet another Board Member. Betty Lester, our Housing Co-Chair recently took a job working for Council Member Steve Levin, which I would like to congratulate her for, but the City Charter specifically prohibits the staff of Council Members to serve on their respective Community Boards so, sadly, Betty was forced to resign. That brings our official count down to 43 Board Members. I'd like to thank Betty for her selfless service to our Community Board and am grateful that we'll still have a chance to work closely together on neighborhood issues.

Chairperson Bashner reported to the Board that the Borough President's Office is still actively working on this year's appointment list and that they are expecting it to be released before our next general meeting.

Chairperson Bashner congratulated our Environmental Protection Committee Chairperson Bob Zuckerman, who recently began a new job as the Executive Director of the Lower East Side Business Improvement District. We know firsthand how important BID's are in helping to support and promote local businesses and neighborhood character.

Chairperson Bashner thanked our Economic Development Co-Chairperson Rick Luftglass for attending the Department of City Planning's kick-off meeting on their Comprehensive Waterfront Planning initiative on April 8th on our behalf. We are hopeful that by remaining active and engaged we will continue to help shape the City's policies with regard to the waterfront and broaden the initiative to include planning for climate change in the discussion and the other policy implications that flow from that as well.

Chairperson Bashner reported to the Board that this morning he was invited to be a presenter at a special program that featured international guests from the City of Rotterdam to give us a chance to share some ideas and best practices regarding waterfront planning.

Chairperson Bashner advised the Board that it was recently announced by Leslie Koch, the Executive Director of the Governors Island Preservation and Education Corporation (GIPEC) that we will once again have free weekend ferry service between the Fulton Ferry Landing in Brooklyn and Governors Island this year from June 5 through October 10. GIPEC is also working with the Brooklyn Bridge Park Development Corporation to finish work on a ferry landing at Pier 6 which, once complete, will be the new waterfront access point replacing Fulton Ferry. This new, closer access point will cut travel time down to 3 minutes so we will have nearly continuous service between Brooklyn and Governors Island when Pier 6 is opened. We also learned that GIPEC will be transitioning from a State to a City entity and that its board will be affected as a result. He will be resending a copy of our board's previous position taken which had suggested that Brooklyn be given a proportionate share of seats on the GIPEC board in the hope that the Mayor will factor that into his decision- making moving forward.

Chairperson Bashner informed the Board that our Parks/Recreation/Cultural Affairs Committee will be hosting a visit from Regina Myer, the Executive Director of the Brooklyn Bridge Park Development Corporation, who will update us on the recent announcement that control over Brooklyn Bridge Park will be ceded from the State to the City, and also fill us in on the Pier 6 development plans.

Chairperson Bashner thanked the Department of Transportation and Council Members Lander and Levin for inviting us to co-host the Open House event this past Monday which showcased the latest plans for bicycle lanes on Prospect Park West. DOT has been largely responsive to our suggestions so yellow flashing traffic 2

signals for the bicyclists, north and southbound, will be included in their initial plans and, assuming the new configuration works as it is expected to, with funding from our Council Members they are also open to the idea of building out the traffic medians to separate the parked cars from the two-way bicycle traffic. For those of you who were unable to attend the Open House, DOT has agreed to attend our upcoming meeting of our Transportation Committee to share their implementation plans. They are targeting a summer installation.

Chairperson Bashner ended his report by reminding the Board Members that we will be giving out our Youth Acknowledgement Awards next month so in consideration of our guests please do your best to be prompt so we can start at 6:30pm.

_________________________________________________________________________________________ DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT……………..................................................…CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that he was invited on March 12th along with city agencies and our elected officials to go on a walk through of the Columbia Street district with the folks from the Columbia Waterfront Neighborhood Association to take a look at some potential and real problems that the neighborhood is experiencing at various intersections. DOT is working on a comprehensive response to all of the suggestions; as soon as we get it we will pass it along.

District Manager Hammerman reported that on March 22nd he also participated in a meeting with the Columbia Waterfront Neighborhood Association, Cobble Hill Association, Carroll Gardens Neighborhood Association and the consultants brought on board by EDC to do the “Fix the Ditch” study (The BQE trench that runs parallel to Hicks Street). He thanked Congresswoman Nydia Velázquez for securing $300,000 for the study. The will be a series of three public meetings to discus this study and the first one will be May 4th at Long island College Hospital from 6:30pm – 8:30pm.

District Manager Hammerman advised the Board that on March 15th he attended a meeting with National Grid and the State Department of Environmental Conservation who had done a targeted outreach to the people who live around the Lowe’s and Pathmark site which by State DEC is referred to as the Metropolitan Manufactured Gas Plant (MGP) site. We have several MGP sites along the Gowanus Canal. The site was partially remediated when they built the Lowe’s, but the portion under Pathmark remains to be remediated and the State is embarking on a remedial investigation now which is a process in which they will be taking boring, air, soil and ground water samples in order to find out what needs to be done to clean up the area. The Friends of Community Board 6 has applied for and received a Technical Assistance Grant (TAG) from the State to hire a consultant to help explain to the community the results of this type of study.

District Manager Hammerman reported that we have received an answer to our February 11th letter to the State Department of Public Service which expresses concern about the electric rates of Con Edison as they relate to the possible use of Con Edison's system to provide shore power to passenger cruise ships docking in our district. The use of shore power would allow cruise ships to obtain electrical supply from Con Edison's local grid instead of relying upon the ship's diesel electric generators which would encourage and promote the use of cleaner and more efficient forms of energy. They are in the process of looking at the rate that Con Edison charges to see if they could bring it down and make it more competitive. They have invited us to be apart of the conversations as they take place and expect to have a final report out by July 1st.

District Manager Hammerman informed the Board that PortSide New York, New York City Economic Development Corporation (NYCEDC), and Manhattan By Sail Inc. are making available 550 free tickets for sails on the Clipper City Tall Ship, a 158' long topsail schooner from Pier 11, Atlantic Basin in Red Hook. Beginning on Friday, April 16, 2010, 240 free tickets will be distributed via the web at http://portsideclippercitv.eventbrite.com. An additional 120 tickets will be available beginning on Friday, April 16th at the district office from 10:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Tickets are limited to two per person. Tickets at Community Board 6 that have not been picked up 3 days before a sail will become available on line. The 3

Clipper City Tall Ship will depart from Pier 11 at the Atlantic Basin; next to the Brooklyn Cruise Terminal at 10:00 a.m. Rides are about one and a half hours long and will depart on April 30th, May 1st, 7th & 8th. (Update: All events were quickly sold out on Friday, April 16, 2010.)

District Manager Hammerman advised the Board that the Department of Buildings (DOB) has advised us of emergency conditions at 267 6th Street. DOB has informed the property owners that the premises have to be made safe by demolition, repair, sealing or whatever means necessary to protect public safety.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by informing the Board that on Sunday May 23rd from 10am – 4pm the Department of Consumer Affairs will be hosting it’s 3rd annual “Shred Fest NYC” which is a free shredding event, held to increase public awareness of identity theft prevention and to remind New Yorkers about the importance of shredding documents that contain personal information before throwing them away. In addition to having their documents shredded in truck mounted industrial shredding equipment, New Yorkers will receive information on how to prevent identify theft. All of the shredded paper generated by Shred Fest NYC will be recycled.

Youth Service Chairperson James Bernard reported to the Board that the committee reviewed the Administration for Children’s Services proposal to terminate leases at 4 day care centers in our district.

Motion: by Board Member Daniel Kummer, seconded by Board Member Derrick Vito; Recommendation to oppose the Administration for Children’s Services proposal to terminate leases at 4 day care centers (viz.; AMICO Court Street DCC at 292 Court Street, Bethel Baptist DCC at 242 Hoyt Street, Strong Place DCC at 242 Hoyt Street and Salvation Army Fiesta DCC at 76-80 Lorraine Street) in the Brooklyn Community Board 6 district.

Board Member Suzanne Turet spoke in favor of the motion stating that one of the things that was brought up in the meeting by District Manager Hammerman was that the representatives of ACS had no knowledge of the “Fair Share” criteria, whereby City agencies must open or close facilities throughout the City fairly across the board and must not do so unfairly to one district over another. Of all the 15 centers they were closing throughout the City 4 were in our district. One of the reasons they were going to close the 4 in our district was that their leases and the maintenance of the facilities were expensive.

Board Member Lisa Cirando spoke in favor of the motion stating that attendance was not taken into account when making a decision about closing the facilities. No one from the City, particularly DCAS who is responsible, approached any of the landlords regarding renegotiating the leases. For example, AMICO’s landlord had the right to charge the City for facility maintenance, but has not.

Board Member David Reiss spoke in favor of the motion stating that we should begin to address with the agency the poorly structured process by which they decided to close these facilities as well as Fair Share criteria brought up by District Member Hammerman.

Board Member Judith Thompson stated that whether or not the City renewed their least with AMICO, the landlord could still bill the city for maintenance for up to six years after the fact.

VOTE: 33 YEAS, 0 NAYS, 0 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED UNANIMOUSLY

TIME: 8:03 P.M.

Permits & Licenses Chairperson Daniel Kummer also reported to the Board that the committee reviewed a presentation on two bills which would amend the State Liquor Authority's regulations that relates to calculating the 200- and 500-foot rule's measurements for a proposed on-premise liquor establishment.

Motion: by Committee;

Recommendation anticipated on Assembly Bill A 7364-C and Senate Bill S 3775-C, a bill to amend the State Liquor Authority's regulations that relates to calculating the 200- and 500-foot rule's measurements for a proposed on-premise liquor establishment.

Board Member Glenn Kelly asked from which point to which point would the 200 foot rule be calculated. Permits & Licenses Chairperson Kummer stated that instead of using the distance between the entrance doors of two establishments, the rule would be from property line to property line.

Board Member Anthony Pugliese asked what defines the property line of a school. Permits & Licenses Chairperson Kummer stated that it is the lot line that defines it.

Board Member Nica Lalli asked if this bill covers hard liquor licenses or beer and wine licenses. She further asked if existing establishments would be grandfathered in. Permits & Licenses Chairperson Kummer stated that this bill covers all licenses and that existing establishments would be grandfathered in.

VOTE: 26 YEAS, 0 NAYS, 7 ABSTENTIONS – 2 FOR CAUSE

MOTION PASSED

__________________________________________________________________________________________ LANDMARKS/LAND USE COMMITTEE.............................ROBERT LEVINE, LANDMARKS CHAIR

.…………………........PETER FLEMING, LAND USE CHAIR

Landmarks Committee Chairperson Robert Levine reported to the Board that the committee reviewed a proposed long-term lease for the Brooklyn Philharmonic Symphony Orchestra, Inc. to occupy the former quarters of FDNY Engine Company 204 at 299 Degraw Street.

Motion: by Committee;

Recommendation to conditionally approve a proposed long-term lease for the Brooklyn Philharmonic Symphony Orchestra, Inc. to occupy the former quarters of FDNY Engine Company 204 at 299 Degraw Street (between Court/Smith Streets) with the following condition, that the rent should not be $1.00, but $60,000, and it be kept in a fund to determine its use after the 10-year lease is up.

Board Member David Reiss asked what standard the Committee had used to evaluate the Philharmonic Symphony Orchestra in comparison to other organizations. Landmarks Committee Chairperson Levine stated that the Committee did not have a choice as to the organization since an RFP was released. We only looked at the issue of the lease terms.

Board Member Lou Sones spoke out against the motion because he feels that this Board’s position was that we wanted to keep this facility as a firehouse or a CBO related to fire services. Also, it was not stated up front that the city was going to be giving $2.8 million for the renovation of this facility. That money could have been used to hire more firefighters instead of fixing up this facility for a CBO that has nothing to do with fire services.

7

Board Member Bob Levine stated that we should be supporting the arts and he also agreed that we wanted to see a fire services related CBO in there. He further stated that we should not be voting on the amount of money that the City should be charging for rent. We should be asking for a formal review when the lease is us for renewal.

Board Member Elly Spicer stated that the $2.8 million would be coming out of Capital money not Expense money in order to renovate the facility. We should be supporting the Philharmonic paying more than a $1.00 a year, but should not be asking for a specific amount.

Board Member Robert Zuckerman asked if anyone knew what it cost to run the firehouse before it was closed. Landmarks Committee Chairperson Levine stated that we did not.

Board Member Mark Shames stated that we should not be micromanaging this issue since the Philharmonic Symphony Orchestra already received the RFP. We are spending to much of this Board’s time on this issue.

Board Member Jordan Dressler asked if anyone knew where the Philharmonic Symphony Orchestra was located now and what is there financial situation. Landmarks Committee Chairperson Levine stated that they are located in DUMBO and the financial officer of the organization stated that it was viable although they did not give us specifics since they were in the process of redoing their financial plan.

Board Member Judith Thompson stated that we should ask them to make the facility available to the community and the fire department for meeting and lectures related to fire concerns.

Board Member Glenn Kelly asked who would be paying for the maintenance of the facility. Landmarks Committee Chairperson Levine stated that the organization will be paying the maintenance for the facility.

VOTE: 24 YEAS, 4 NAYS, 5 ABSTENTIONS

MOTION PASSED
