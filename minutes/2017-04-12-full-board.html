CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………….………………….....SAYAR LONIAL

Chairperson Lonial opened his report by offering some procedural reminders: only board members are allowed to comment and vote on any matters before the board unless an individual is specifically called upon. He also asked that as a policy moving forward, all board members sit in the first few rows when possible.

Mr. Lonial offered his personal support of the Transportation Committee’s Link NYC resolution that is up for consideration later in the meeting, noting that, while there is still work to be done, the Link NYC program will be a step toward diminishing lack of digital access to parts of the district.

The Chair thanked Council Member Lander, Assembly Member Simon, Senator Squadron, Congresswoman Velazquez, and Comptroller Stringer for all co-signing a letter sent to the Landmarks Preservation Commission regarding the board’s recent vote to oppose an application for 113 Congress Street in Cobble Hill.

Mr. Lonial thanked CB6 Transportation Committee Co-Chairs Eric McClure and Tom Miskel as well as Environmental Protection Committee Chair Mark Shames and District Manager Craig Hammerman for attending a stakeholders meeting at Borough Hall to discuss the next phase of the planning for the Brooklyn Greenway Initiative.

The Chairperson updated the board that the formerly laid over Transportation Committee issue pertaining to the bike corral at 341 5th Avenue (from October 2016) was back before the board tonight having met all of the stipulated requirements for letters of support and public buy-in.

Mr. Lonial closed his report by wishing all in attendance a Happy Passover and an early Happy Easter.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Hammerman offered a short report, updating attendees on the upcoming Formula E electric car racing event to be held at the Red Hook Cruise Ship Terminal in July. Mr. Hammerman reported that Formula E is expecting crowds of 15-20,000 attendees each day. The District Manager stated that the promoters of the event are working to provide a number of transportation options to access the Cruise Ship Terminal and all public correspondence relative to the event is highlighting that there will be no parking provided at the venue. Additionally, the event will result in some permanent improvements to the Cruise Ship Terminal, including new landscaping and signage at the entrance and roadway repaving. The promoters have already reached out to both the Brooklyn Chamber of Commerce and Red Hook Initiative, and will be working with NYC schools to offer access to the cars and drivers before the event.

2

Mr. Hammerman reported that the district office got perfect marks at the Comptroller’s Community Board Audit Exit Conference at the end of March. The audit was an inventory of office equipment, and Mr. Hammerman noted that CB6 was one of only five Brooklyn Community Boards found to be in compliance. The next audit is likely to occur in roughly four years.

Mr. Hammerman closed his remarks by updating the board on his previous correspondence with the Governor regarding a request for the restoration of a two-way toll on the Verazzano Bridge. The District Manager reported that Congressman Donovan has requested a study of the matter, similar to ours, and that Staten Island Community Boards 1 and 2 are lining up to support the request. Mr. Hammerman reported that the MTA Bridges and Tunnels division will be conducting a study to consider the proposal.
