CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by congratulating our long-time Landmarks Committee Chair Robert Levine who was recently honored by the Prospect Park Alliance for his invaluable contributions toward their ongoing efforts to archive Prospect Park history. Landmarks Committee Chair Robert Levine has an impressive collection of postcards and ephemera specializing in Brooklyn which is envied by historians, nostalgics and enthusiasts alike and has been loaned to and borrowed by many institutions and businesses. Anyone who visited the Pavilion movie theater in Park Slope, for example, may have noticed that they had poster-sized enlargements of historic postcards of local scenes; those were taken from his collection. He also thanked him for representing us at a recent Landmarks Preservation Commission hearing. Our letters are factored into the decisions made by bodies like the LPC but when someone can actually represent us in person that can often have an even greater impact.

Chairperson Kummer also thanked our Environmental Protection and Permits & Licenses Committee Chairs, Mark Shames and Gary Reilly, who along with myself, our District Manager Craig Hammerman, Hildegaard Link, Glenn Kelly and Bette Stoltz, attended a working session to begin the process of organizing our response to the EPA’s Proposed Remedial Action Plan (PRAP) for the Gowanus Canal as part of the Superfund process. We intend to have a draft to be discussed at their next committee meeting which will take place on April 1st, so anyone wishing to contribute to this effort is welcome and encouraged to attend this upcoming meeting. I announced last month that the EPA had extended the deadline for response to April 27th so it is my intention to have a recommendation coming out of the April 1st committee meeting to be presented to the full board for discussion and adoption at our general meeting next month.

Chairperson Kummer additionally thanked our District Manager Craig Hammerman who hosted the group of 7 students from NYU that attended our general meeting last month in a visit to our district office to field questions about their project on the Gowanus neighborhood. Craig later informed me that he sees at least one class of students every semester at the district office who come from a variety of disciplines but typically representing the fields of urban planning, journalism, political science, and public administration. As I understand it, keeping up with the inquiries from students and researchers is one of the reasons why we initially posted a website 12 years ago and continue to use it as a repository for our minutes and other documents which make it easier for the public to have ready access to the information in our office without having to inconvenience or disrupt our hard-working staff members.

Chairperson Kummer advised the Board that we will have at least one of the two new business improvement districts under formation in our district present at our next Economic/Waterfront/Community Development & Housing Committee meeting on Monday, March 18th. Representatives for the Park Slope 7th Avenue BID which is currently in formation will be joined by representatives of our 3 existing BIDs, namely, Atlantic Avenue, North Flatbush Avenue and Park Slope 5th Avenue. I’d encourage anyone who’s interested in the

commercial health of our district to consider attending to hear about all of the exciting programs and services these local business organizations’ offer.

Chairperson Kummer informed the Board that representatives for the Brooklyn Public Library system will be introducing themselves at an upcoming meeting co-sponsored by our Parks/Recreation/Cultural Affairs and Youth/Human Services/Education Committees on March 20th. As I reported last month, the Brooklyn Public Library is looking to dispose of the Pacific Branch Library which could, ultimately, be demolished as it lacks landmarking protection. The building is the first Carnegie-designed branch to open in Brooklyn and dates back to 1904. If you’re interested in speaking directly with the Brooklyn Public Library to find out what’s going on in any of our 4 branches and the BPL system in general please consider attending this meeting.

Chairperson Kummer reported to that our Transportation Committee will be featuring an update from New York City Transit on their ongoing work on the Culver Viaduct, including updates on the Smith-9th Street and 4th Avenue subway stations at their meeting on Thursday, March 21st.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by wishing everyone a Happy St. Patrick’s Day, Passover, Easter, spring and anything else that may be cause for celebration over the next month.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by advising the board that there have been a few blog and newspaper reports about a dispute taken place between neighbors in our Gowanus neighborhood, the Marriott hotel and Spoke the Hub at the Gowanus Arts building next door. They both reached out to the district office and he held a mediation session with them last week. The first meeting went well and he will be following up with them in the near future. His goal is to at a minimum have both parties figure out a way to peacefully co- exist with one another and, ideally, leverage the others presence in the community. We ended the meeting by agreeing to continue to keep the lines of communication open. Anyone who would like more information should call him at the district office

District Manager Hammerman informed the Board that he has been attending the Mayor’s Special Initiative for Resiliency and Rebuilding Task Force which has been meeting at City Hall on a monthly basis since January. They had a public meeting in Carroll Gardens in which they got some valuable feedback from the community. This initiative is the Bloomberg Administration’s efforts to put together a comprehensive report, largely motivated by Hurricane Sandy but taking a broader view of climate change and the impact that it could potentially have on New York City and how we should best prepare and respond to that. They will be issuing a report by the end of May and we will keep everyone posted as the information becomes available.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the Board that our Gowanus BOA team recently had a meeting with representatives of the State DEC, National Grid and the Parks Department to talk about the three manufacturing gas plant sites in our district; Public Place Site at Smith and 5th Streets, Fulton manufacturing gas plant at Douglass/Degraw Pool, Metropolitan manufacturing gas plant at Lowes/Pathmark. Both the Fulton and Metropolitan sites are in the process of being evaluated for remediation, they are in the feasibility study phases and there will be some testing going on at the Public Place site within the next couple of months. Some of the remedial approaches that will take place at that site will include: securing the bulkheads, installing monitoring wells, remediation of the soil and the installation of barriers to prevent in further migration onto any of the new areas that they would be remediating. The Public Place site is over six acres and before they begin an all-out remediation effort they wanted to do a smaller portion of methodology testing first. We have ask them to come out and brief the community within the next few months so that before any major work is done on the site so we will have an idea of what to expect in terms of the impact to the surrounding area.

At the hearing, the judge again blocked the hospital's closure. LICH is vital to Brooklyn and we're now pushing for critical life support and urging the Department of Health to work with us to save this critical hospital. LANDMARKS/LAND USE COMMITTEE………….…...….ROBERT LEVINE, LANDMARKS CHAIR

…………………...….PETER FLEMING, LAND USE CHAIR

Land Use Committee Chair Peter Fleming reported that the committee reviewed an application requesting a variance to allow the construction of 2 three-story residences 96-98 Degraw Street.

Motion: by Committee;

Recommendation to approve an application (BSA Calendar Nos. 13-13-BZ and 14-13-BZ) submitted to the Board of Standards and Appeals on behalf of The Green Witch Project LLC and The Other Half, LLC requesting a variance to allow the construction of 2 three-story, approximately 3,100 square foot single-family residences pursuant to Zoning Resolution section 72-21 at 96-98 Degraw Street (Block 329, Lots 22-23), between Columbia and Van Brunt Streets.

Board Member Judith Thompson asked if there was a reason why they got an R6A designation. Land Use Committee Chair Peter Fleming stated that it was giving that designation because the closest residential zoning designation was R6A. They are not seeking a change in the zoning; all they want to do is build something that matches the surrounding R6A zoning in a M1-1 building.

Board Member Robert Levine asked how many cars will be in the driveways. Land Use Committee Chair Peter Fleming stated that there will be one driveway per townhouse which could only fit one car.

6

Board Member Elizabeth Shipley stated that M1-1 designation includes cabinetry and carpentry shops, set painters and print makers. A 3,100 square foot footprint seemed to be plenty for a lot of those businesses. Land Use Committee Chair Peter Fleming stated that the committee really did not discuss those usages; by and large owners are looking for more aggressive uses than those businesses in order to pay them the rent to make a profit.

Board Member Gregory O’Connell asked what is the ratio of commercial use -vs. - residential use on this block. Land Use Committee Chair Peter Fleming stated that we did not know. He also asked if the developer had built any other buildings in CB6. Land Use Committee Chair Peter Fleming stated yes, at 107 Union Street between Columbia and Van Brunt Streets.

Amendment: by Board Member Judith Thompson, seconded by Board Member Hildegaard Link; To specify in the motion R6B designation.

Board Member Norman Cox spoke out against the amendment stating that this amendment is not relevant since the original motion is not a zoning issue.

Board Member Glenn Kelly stated that this application is going before the Board of Standards and Appeals. The applicant will present what their plans are, if they are successful in getting an approval than that is what they must build. They are referring to R6A as the nearest residential zoning, but they are not building to the full extent that they would be allowed to under R6A, but perhaps a little bit bigger that R6B but they are matching the buildings on either side of the lot. So it seemed appropriate to the committee to approve the original motion.

Board Member Sayar Lonial spoke out against the motion stating that this matter is not a zoning issue.

Board Member Judith Thompson withdrew her proposed amendment.

Discussion on original motion continued.

Board Member Elizabeth Shipley spoke out against the motion stating that the time for developers to evaluate the feasibility of making money off developing a particular site is before you buy it not after. If the goal here is to go around snatching up manufacturing sites that are small so you can present one variance application after another to convert them into high end residential I don’t think this will help our community. I think we will lose the fabric of the neighborhood.

Board Member Hildegaard Link spoke out against the motion asking the Community Board to work with the owners to find alternative usages for the lot.

Board Member Lou Sones spoke in favor of the motion stating that this lot is between to residential properties and to put a M1-1 business there would be intrusive to the residents on both sides of the lot.

Board Member Norman Cox spoke in favor of the motion stating that that part of Degraw Street already has a row of residential properties on it and the sentiment of the neighborhood is that it should stay that way.

Board Member Roger Rigolli spoke in favor of the motion stating that they met the 5 criteria for the variance.

Chairperson Kummer recognized Councilman Steven Levin

Councilman Levin stated that the Pacific Street Library on Fourth Avenue being the first Carnegie library in Brooklyn is currently looking to be sold by the Brooklyn Public Library for the purpose of moving to a new site to be built by the developer Two Trees. On March 5 he participated in a community meeting with other elected officials to discuss the future of the Pacific Street Library and what residents that can do about it. In 2004 the Park Slope Civic Council tried to get the building landmarked and have resubmitted this year to not only protect the library from future harm but to obtain federal funding to help repair the library in the future. He is in favor of having the building landmarked. He invited everyone to attend a City Planning Commission Public Hearing on March 20th at 10am at 22 Reade Street in Manhattan. For more information visit http://citizensdefendinglibraries.blogspot.com/. The Councilman also asked everyone to participate in his Participatory Budgeting Expos. A Project Expo is the Community's chance to learn about the various projects that have been proposed by the participatory budgeting community delegates. The delegates will be there to explain their proposals. The Participatory Budgeting vote will be held the entire first week of April. The closest expos to CB6 will be held on March 19th at the YWCA located at 30 3rd Ave from 6pm-9pm and March 23rd at City Tech Atrium located at 300 Jay St from 2pm-5pm. For further information visit http://stephenlevin33.tumblr.com/pbnyc. He is working on a Resolution (A.4327), which would permit the City of New York to set up a demonstration project involving speed camera enforcement. He has also sent a letter to NYPD Commissioner Ray Kelly questioning the practice of assigning the Accident Investigation Squad only in instances where someone is killed or is believed likely to die. Currently, crashes that result in injuries that are not considered fatal are handled by precinct cops who are not trained to conduct full-scale investigations. This policy is inconsistent with state traffic code. He is also preparing legislation to amend the NYPD patrol handbook to conform to state law. However, the bill will move forward as a resolution because it has been determined that the City Council does not have jurisdiction to amend the NYPD Patrolman’s handbook. NYPD has agreed to have all serious collisions investigated whether someone dies or not.
