Committee Reports

A. Landmarks, Land Use & Housing 152 Atlantic Avenue David Briggs presented an application for a Certificate of Appropriateness for a rooftop addition. A motion to approve the application with the condition that the railing facing Atlantic Avenue be set back an additional two feet was approved by the committee with two abstentions. The committee had a quorum, so no floor motion was required. The motion was approved by the board, with Daniel Kummer and David Briggs abstaining. 175 3rd Street David Briggs presented a motion to approve the certification to transfer development rights at 175 3rd Street. He explained that this project involves 971 units permitted as-of-right. Additionally, Charney Companies and Tavros plan to acquire zoning floor area (ZFA) from the neighboring Powerhouse Arts, which would enable construction of 100 additional units. This transfer of floor area from a landmarked building is allowed under the City of Yes zoning changes and the request before the Community Board concerns modifications to building bulk. Under City of Yes changes, this application will go directly to the City Planning Commission (CPC), bypassing the local council member and Borough President, with a CPC hearing scheduled for March 17. David explained that the developer did not clearly indicate the difference between the as-of-right envelope and the requested modifications, but noted that one of the two proposed towers would exceed the zoning envelope. The project includes a waterfront park as required under the Waterfront Access Guidelines. The proposal also includes widening “2nd Street” (an unmapped street between the building and Powerhouse Arts). No parking is included in the proposal, which is permissible under City of Yes rules. The committee has made three requests to the developer: 1) 100% affordability for new units, 2) inclusion of public bathrooms, and 3) negotiation with Powerhouse Arts to improve promenade access along the Gowanus Canal. David noted that the developer is paying Powerhouse Arts approximately $15-20 million for the ZFA, which he argued should allow funds for Powerhouse Arts to build a public promenade. He also argued that a change in use designation would allow Powerhouse Arts to benefit from city indemnification for the promenade, which should make the effort feasible. After six months of discussions initiated by the committee, the developer has not acted on these requests and has not engaged Powerhouse Arts in discussions of promenade accessibility or improvements. During the committee discussion, members opposed rejecting a project that would include 230 affordable units. As a result, the committee moved to approve the certification to transfer development rights and bulk modification, with the conditions that 1) the Powerhouse Arts promenade be opened to the public as a direct result of the ZFA and 2) 30% of the 1,071 units meet affordability requirements, averaging at 60% AMI (under Mandatory Inclusionary Housing Option 1). The committee approved the motion unanimously, with a quorum present and one abstention. During the full board discussion, Mandi Spishak-Thomas asked what the impact of a no vote would be, and Pauline Blake asked whether the motion would slow the project to allow more time for discussion. While project approvals are expedited under City of Yes, CB6 will submit their resolution and can argue their case before the CPC vote. Catherine Vaughan expressed support for this approach to avoid standing in the way of housing development. She argued that the board could include an addendum to the resolution showing the developer the value of investing in the promenade. Daniel Kummer confirmed that these points can be communicated in the letter to the agency characterizing our vote. The full board vote approved the motion with Karyn Broughton, Mandi Spishak-Thomas, and Reema Sharma voting no and Pauline Blake and Tiffany Reid abstaining. B. C. The Transportation, Parks & Public Infrastructure Resolution to Introduce a District-Wide 20 MPH Slow Zone Tony Melone presented the following resolution: Recognizing CB6’s commitment to safer streets and support for Sammy’s Law, we recommend that NYC DOT institute a district-wide 20 MPH slow zone on all applicable streets, and adjust signal timing to reflect the new limit, with camera enforcement. We also call for the adoption of a citywide speed limit of 20mph on all applicable streets. Tony explained that Safe Streets and Make McGuinness Safe were presented at the last committee meeting. He explained that the district-wide speed limit would apply only to streets with no more than two lanes in one direction, and that similar motions have been passed by Brooklyn Community Board 1 and Queens Community Board 2. The committee passed the resolution unanimously with one abstention for cause. Marc Torrence noted that while the reduction from 25 MPH to 20 MPH seems small, it significantly improves survivability in crashes. Tony noted that enforcement typically begins 10 MPH over the limit, meaning tickets would start at 30 MPH. The resolution passed unanimously, with Pauline Blake abstaining. Tony also highlighted that the CB6 website now has a traffic safety request form for specific improvements. He explained that the next committee meeting includes a discussion with NYCDOT on unsafe driving on Columbia Street, reopening the Carroll Street Bridge, daylighting initiatives, slow zones, and a northbound bike route to complement the Court Street bike lane.
