CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT………………………………………………………....….DANIEL KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel Kummer began his report by first extending condolences on behalf of the Community Board to our fellow Board Member Dolly Williams whose mother recently passed away, and also to further extend our condolences to the family of Albert Cabbad, our former Board Member, outspoken advocate for harmony in our community, and longtime store owner on 5th Avenue in Park Slope. Al’s message of tolerance and his celebration of diversity are timeless, and his active representation of the interests of small businesses is something we could always use more of on the Community Board.

Chairperson Kummer reported to the Board that we have recently received a resignation from Board Member Dominique Bravo who explained that she has recently taken a new position with a non-profit organization called Girl Be Heard, which uses theater as a vehicle to empower young women to become brave, confident, socially conscious leaders, and as a result she has less time available to attend evening meetings. We have also received a resignation from Board Member Philip Mindlin who has recently taken a work assignment abroad and finds that he is no longer able to regularly attend general meetings. We wish them both well in their respective endeavors, and thank them for their service. We currently have 45 Board Members.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by advising the Board that last Friday State Senator Velmanette Montgomery organized a roundtable meeting with New York State Empire State Development Corporation President Kenneth Adams to talk about the future of manufacturing and industrial businesses, especially in Brooklyn. I’m told that Adam Friedman from Pratt made a brief presentation which highlighted the fact that manufacturing, especially artisanal production, fashion, specialty foods and other new manufacturing, are on the rise in Brooklyn, as we also learned through our Gowanus BOA initiative. I’d like to thank Board Members Ariel Krasnow, Rick Luftglass, Paige Bellenbaum and District Manager Craig Hammerman for attending and making sure that CB6 was amply represented.

DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT………………………………………….…….CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the board that the Department of Buildings (DOB) has advised us of an emergency condition at 136 Atlantic Avenue. This building was inspected on February 20th and declared unsafe. DOB has informed the property owner that the premise has to be made secure to protect the public safety.

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that the FDNY wants to remind all New Yorkers that they should have changed the batteries in their smoke and carbon monoxide alarms when they changed their clocks for the start of Daylight Saving Time. The Fire Safety Education Unit has left alarm batteries and fire safety information on the side table for distribution. 2

District Manager Hammerman reported that he, Parks Committee Chair Glenn Kelly and Assistant District Manager Leroy Branch attended an on site scoping meeting with the Parks Department to discuss the renovation of St. Mary’s Playground along Smith Street. Parks has received $850,000 from the MTA for the rental of the playground for a staging area during the Culver Viaduct rehab work. The design will be ready hopefully by the end of the year and the work is projected to begin in 2 years.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by informing the board that along with the other Brooklyn/Staten Island Community Board District Managers, he met with the new Commissioner of Community Affairs Unit, Marco A. Carrión. He delivered to the Commissioner a 5 page list of outstanding service delivery items that the Brooklyn District Managers had developed and given to the Borough President so that his office could start addressing these items at the Borough Service cabinet venue. Now the Mayor’s office has the list and they can begin to address these issues directly with the agencies themselves. We also pressed them to take a close look at the Community Boards budget, which has not been increased in 20 years and open up a dialog about other resources that could help out the Community Boards. In-kind resources and other forms of contributions that could help offset some of our on-going costs. The third item that he raised was a reminder that as of last October 1st the Mayor’s office was supposed to initiate a 10-year review process of the Community District boundaries. He suggested that the Community Boards be included in this conversation during the earlier part of the process before they issue a report.

Zeeshan Ott, representing State Senator Daniel Squadron, stated that the Senator is looking forward to working with the City to end the unfair agreement that makes NYCHA pay for police protection. This agreement has locked NYCHA in a perpetual financial crisis and essentially forced NYCHA residents to pay double for policing. A 1994 agreement between NYCHA and the City requires NYCHA to pay the New York Police Department for ongoing law enforcement services for NYCHA residents through Police Service Areas. Currently, NYCHA pays over $70 million a year to the NYPD for these “special” police services. This year Mayor de Blasio will end NYCHA's payments to the NYPD. The Senator is also pushing to create a Lunar New Year school holiday since a huge number of kids celebrate it and end up missing school. He ended by inviting everyone to the Senators 2014 Community Convention to get residents input on issues throughout the 26th Senate district.

Peterson Napoleon, representing Assembly Member Felix Ortiz, stated that the Assemblyman supported the efforts to pass a bill named for Briana Ojeda a Brooklyn girl who died of an asthma attack after a NYPD officer did not administer CPR. “Briana’s Law” would require cops to get CPR retraining every two years.

Josh Levin, representing NYC Comptroller Scott Stringer, stated that there was a milk contract negotiated back in 2008 through an audit initiated by the comptroller it appears that there may have been some collusion amongst 3 companies who bid for the contract totaling $130 million. The comptroller has referred this case to the Department of Justice. His office is setting up an online tracking service to monitor where Sandy dollars go and make sure the Build It Back program is truly helping residents. They will be doing an audit of the New York, Queens and Brooklyn Public Libraries because of the generous raises the executives have received in the mist of cut backs and the move to sell some of their buildings. Specifically the Comptroller will be looking at capital improvements, executive pay and operations in his audit.

Patrick Rheaume, representing Congress Member Yvette Clarke, stated that the Congresswoman has urged Governor Cuomo to have a special election to fill 4 State Assembly and 1 State Senate vacancies. The Governor is not planning to hold a special election. The Congresswoman is now lobbying NYS Assembly Speaker Sheldon Silver and Senate Leader Dean Skelos to act as stewards for the interest of the residents in Brooklyn without representation until an election is held.

Dan Wiley, representing Congress Member Nydia Velázquez, stated that two of the Congresswoman’s bills (H.R. 4121 and H.R. 2452) have been approved by the House Committee on Small Business. A third bill, H.R. 3370, Homeowner Flood Insurance Affordability Act of 2013 would prohibit the Administrator of the Federal Emergency Management Agency from: (1) increasing flood insurance risk premium rates to reflect the current risk of flood for certain property located in specified areas subject to a certain mandatory premium adjustment, or (2) Reducing such subsidies for any property not insured by the flood insurance program as of July 6, 2012, or any policy that has lapsed in coverage as a result of the policyholder's deliberate choice (Pre-Flood Insurance Rate Map or pre-FIRM properties).

Oscar Jonas, representing State Senator Velmanette Montgomery, stated that on March 7th the Senator hosted a roundtable discussion with Empire State Development Corporation Chair Ken Adams, representatives of Comptroller Scott Stringer, members of the City Council, and the participants of the Industrial/Development/Employment Group. The discussion focused on finding ways that the City and the State working together can encourage the use of industrial lands in our City for “Real Jobs for Real People”. She has also introduced a bill (S.6777) which will allow community boards to apply and receive grants for waterfront revitalization programs for coastal areas and inland waterways in the same manner that local governments currently do.

Human Services Co-Chairs Benjamin Solotaire and Paige Bellenbaum and Economic Development Chair Rick Luftglass reported to the Board that the committee reviewed a proposal by Urban Recovery House to establish a 25-30 bed methadone-free rehabilitation facility for self-pay clientele at 110 Beard Street.

Motion: by Board Member Suzanne Turet, seconded by Board Member Mark Shames; Recommendation to approve a proposal by Urban Recovery House to establish a 25-30 bed methadone-free rehabilitation facility for self-pay clientele at 110 Beard Street (between Richards/Van Brunt Streets) with the conditions stipulated in the Community Benefits Agreement, with the addition of the formation of a Community Advisory Board to be in place prior to the opening of the facility.

Motion: by Board Member Noel Berk-Rauch, seconded by Board Member Ariel Krasnow; Recommendation to send a letter to the Department of City Planning, Department of Buildings and our Elected officials expressing concerns about the future use and development of 5

manufacturing/industrial properties and vacant lands on Block 604 in Red Hook, 110 Beard Street, with additional addresses on Beard, Van Dyke, Richards and Van Brunt Streets. The property which is zoned M1-2 is part of an industrial business zone which is an important anchor in a manufacturing area that has been subject to increasing development pressure for uses that are inconsistent with the goal of industrial support and jobs retention in Red Hook. The property has been on the market, should any City agency or elected official become aware of any development plans we request that they notify the Community Board immediately.

After a lengthy discussion by several Board Members it was suggested that more discussion was needed for the board to make an informed recommendation. It was also suggested that the Economic/Waterfront/Community Development & Housing Committee work with the Landmarks/Land Use Committee in framing such a motion.

Board Member Noel Berk-Rauch withdrew his motion after hearing the short discussion.

Discussion continued on the Urban Recovery House motion.

Board Member Judith Thompson stated that there are going to be a number of high quality jobs associated with this facility. This will be good for the local economy.

Board Member Roger Rigolli spoke out against the motion stating that since we lost Snapple, Red Hook is losing more and more manufacturing/industrial jobs. We should be looking at Red Hook as a whole in terms of keeping these businesses alive and thriving not just one lot at a time.

Board Member Robert Levine agreed that we should be taking a comprehensive look at Red Hook, but he is also comfortable with looking at individual actions and asking for a comprehensive look at the same time.

Board Member Suzanne Turet spoke in favor of the motion stating that the group that is developing this facility has been very forthright and is trying to do something good. This will be the first facility of its kind on the East coast and since it is such a small portion of the entire lot we should support it.

Board Member Ariel Krasnow stated that although everything about the business is fine, they could not give us any agreement he had with the owner/developer in terms of what they might do with the rest of the building, i.e. residential.

Board Member Mark Shames stated that hotels can be built in a manufacturing district but residential housing cannot. So we should not be using that to inform our decision.

VOTE: 28 YEAS, 7 NAYS, 1 ABSTENTION

MOTION PASSED
