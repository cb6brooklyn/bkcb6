CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……………………………...……..………….....…...DANIEL M. KUMMER

Chairperson Daniel M. Kummer began his report by reporting to the Board that we received a resignation from Board Member Greg O’Connell since our last meeting who cited competing demands on his time given the work he is involved in in Upstate New York as a reason to leave the Community Board. That brings our total roster to 45 members.

Chairperson Kummer reminded the Board Members whose terms are up this year, and to anyone wishing to become a Board Member, that the Brooklyn Borough President’s Office will be receiving applications for Board membership up until February 25, 2011. Remember, the forms are returnable to the Borough President’s Office directly, not to our district office. If you have any questions about the appointment process, you can contact Luke DePalma, our liaison at Borough Hall.

Chairperson Kummer informed that Board that he recently met with Council Member Steven Levin as part of his introductory round of meetings with our elected officials. He was very pleased to hear that the Council Member was most receptive to our need to put our heads together to come up with a way to motivate property owners and developers to make their properties safe and remove blighted conditions from our community. It was reassuring to hear that their office was already in touch with the owner of 187 7th Avenue at the corner of 2nd Street, which has sadly become a poster child for exactly this type of challenging situation. We look forward to working with the Council Member in coming up with an effective strategy for dealing with this situation, which we’ll quickly be looking to replicate.

Chairperson Kummer announced that the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency recently released their Remedial Investigation report as part of the Gowanus Canal Superfund process and will be hosting a meeting for the public to ask questions about it on Wednesday, February 23 rd from 6:30-9:00pm at P.S. 32, 317 Hoyt Street (at Union Street). The document itself is available on the EPA’s website and a hard copy is on file at the Carroll Gardens Library for public viewing. He asked District Manager Craig Hammerman to send out an email with a link to the document so that everyone can familiarize themselves with it in preparation for the meeting.

Chairperson Kummer ended his report by thanking everyone who came out to the Transportation Committee meeting on January 23rd to hear a presentation by DOT regarding their findings and recommendations regarding the Prospect Park West bicycle lanes and traffic-calming project installed in summer 2010. We will be having another public hearing in the future to further discuss this item.

__________________________________________________________________________________________ DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT……………..................................................…CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the board that he had the opportunity to address eight new officers at the 76th Police Precinct. These officers were not incoming rookies but part of NYPD’s “Operation Impact” in which officers were sent to patrol high-crime pockets that have resisted the overall crime drop.

District Manager Hammerman informed the Board that on February 10th he and Board Member Tom Miskel would be attending the second meeting of the Atlantic Yards Cabinet set up by Borough President Marty Markowitz and Councilwoman Letitia James to be an ongoing venue to address issues related to Atlantic Yards throughout its construction and implementation periods. The biggest issue he has heard in our district that he will be bringing up at the meeting is the weather-related loss of the fifth travel lane on Flatbush Avenue, the 2

reversible lane. Given the abundance of snow we have received over the last month and the fact that there is no place to put the snow we have loss some capacity on Flatbush Avenue and he hopes that he will hear at the meeting how they intend to address this issue in the future. Anyone with any issues related to Atlantic Yards should contact him at the district office so he can raise them at these meetings.

District Manager Hammerman advised the Board that the Brooklyn Public Library would be installing an Emergency Generator and Cooling Tower at the Central Library at Grand Army Plaza. Since the generator and cooling tower are visible from a small section of Eastern Parkway, the library is in discussions with Landmarks Preservation Commission staff and will be making a presentation to Brooklyn Community Board 9.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by informing the Board that the MTA NYC Transit has informed the Board that the have secured $800,000 from Assembly member Joan Millman and $2 million from Brooklyn Borough President Marty Markowitz for the restoration and capital improvement to the 4th Avenue F and G station which are currently planned to commence this year. Specifically, they are currently planning 'on using these funds on the following capital improvements:  Repair and restoration of exterior tower stone and brickwork  Repair and painting of the viaduct underside over 4th Avenue  Replacement of lighting beneath the viaduct over 4th Avenue and the sidewalks, which would double the existing illumination  Restoration of all four entrance globes (4th Avenue and 10th Street)  Restoration of all entrance doors (4th Avenue and 10th Street)  Restoration of all storefront windows (4th Avenue and 10th Street) in preparation for new retail tenants by 2012

The impacts of balancing the budget just with cuts could be very severe, with devastating reductions of billions of dollars in health care, education, public safety, transportation, and other vital services in the City of New York. EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE……………………………………..……………DANIEL KUMMER, CHAIR

Chairperson Daniel Kummer reported to the Board that the committee discussed adding new language to the Responsible Development Policy of Community Board 6.

Motion: by Committee;

Recommendation to approve the various additions to the Responsible Development Policy of Brooklyn Community Board 6, i.e. Environmental Impact/Sustainability.

Board Member Roger Rigolli asked if this policy applies to only ULURP applications or all developers and how do we enforce them. Chairperson Kummer stated that this is for any Land Use matter involving new construction, substantial renovations or additions totaling at least 10,000 square feet that comes before us for findings and recommendations in connection with an application for a variance, a zoning change, an amendment of an urban renewal plan, a disposition of City-owned property or another action under ULURP. In terms of enforcement, this policy is only “categories of evidence” that we are asking developers to present as part of their presentation to our Land Use/Landmarks Committee for approval under the ULURP process. These standards can help us in the process of making our recommendation decision.

Board Member Lou Sones asked if this policy applies to as-of-right development. Chairperson Kummer stated no.

Board Member Richard Bashner spoke in favor of the motion stating that we can use these standards in helping us make our decisions for a ULURP application.

Board Member Elizabeth Shipley asked whether the developer’s compliance is based on them meeting the threshold of the criteria of the rating systems or do they have to be formally certified or going through the 4

certification process for the various green programs. Chairperson Kummer stated that this is about showing a record of accomplishment that you have met standards on past projects, and are committed to certain values moving forward.

Board Member Shipley asked if we could add language “compliance with” to signify that our intent is for developers to commit to the standards of applicable green rating programs… Chairperson Kummer stated that he would accept the change as a friendly amendment

VOTE: 28 YEAS, 2 NAYS, 0 ABSTENTIONS

AMENDMENT PASSED
