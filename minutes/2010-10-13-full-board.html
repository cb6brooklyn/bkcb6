CHAIRPERSON’S REPORT……………………………...……..………….....…...RICHARD S. BASHNER

Chairperson Richard S. Bashner began his report by introducing a new Board Member, Lisa Napolitano from South Park Slope, appointed at the recommendation of Council Member Stephen Levin. That brings out total headcount up to 49 members.

Chairperson Bashner reported to the board that he attended a meeting at Borough Hall called by Senator Squadron on October 1st regarding the truck traffic being generated by Phoenix Beverage’s use of Piers 7 and 11. Because of the reconstruction of Van Brunt Street north bound, part of the truck route is closed and some of the truck drivers are using undesignated residential streets. For this reason, Phoenix Beverage has asked the drivers to use the BQE northbound to get from pier 11 to pier 7 instead of using unauthorized streets.

Chairperson Bashner informed the board that there would be a very important announcement made tomorrow afternoon regarding the Long Island College Hospital and SUNY-Downstate merger, which has now been the subject of discussion for past two years. We do not have the details of the announcement, but the Governor is supposed to be delivering the information himself from LICH, which means it is likely something substantial. He has asked District Manager Hammerman to send out an email with the specifics when they become available.

Chairperson Bashner advised the board that we’ve been invited by the Department of Transportation to attend a kick-off meeting on October 18th for the Brooklyn Streetcar Study, a trolley and light rail study being funded by an allocation secured by Congresswoman Velázquez. The study will be exploring the feasibility of the installation of trolley/light rail service between Downtown Brooklyn and Red Hook. We had previously included this item among our annual budget requests, the money had been set aside for several years, and DOT is ready to proceed. We are looking forward to participating.

Chairperson Bashner ended his report by updating the board on last month’s motion to recommit the idea of the Minerva statue view corridor. Land Use Chair Peter Fleming and District Manager Hammerman had a preliminary meeting with representatives of Green-Wood Cemetery and Brooklyn Community Board 7. They will also be meeting with the Department of City Planning and CB7 in a couple weeks to get a better idea of the role we would be taking on if we decided to go ahead with the sponsorship of a 197-a plan.

_________________________________________________________________________________________ DISTRICT MANAGER’S REPORT……………..................................................…CRAIG HAMMERMAN

District Manager Craig Hammerman began his report by informing the Board that the Department of Transportation has determine that a speed hump on Plaza Street between Lincoln and Berkeley Places and Butler Street between Court & Smith Streets are good candidate locations for the installation of speed humps.

District Manager Hammerman informed the board that National Grid would be installing and sampling a monitoring well on Luquer Street between Court and Smith Streets, beginning about mid-October for one month. The wells will help measure the chemical characteristics and flow direction of ground water, upgradient from the former Manufactured Gas Plant site, Public Place, located between Smith Street and the Gowanus Canal. Additional information is available at www.citizensmgpsite.com.

2

District Manager Hammerman advised the board that on September 27th he was invited by the director of the 311 Call Center to take a tour of some improved 311 reporting mechanisms that have been set up on-line. This new system will provide the community more transparency and better information about the type of calls that are coming into 311. We will have the ability to download spreadsheet data and be able to manipulate them.

District Manager Hammerman reported to the board that on October 1st he, along with other leaders from Park Slope, met with Deputy Mayor Stephen Goldsmith, the deputy mayor for operations, to talk about the delivery of City government services. They spoke about the advantages and disadvantages of management centralized and decentralized systems, the role of community boards, the City’s 311 system, and how technology is changing government. The Deputy Mayor invited our District Manager to submit further ideas on how government services are delivered for them to consider.

District Manager Hammerman ended his report by announcing that this Saturday at 10am in front of M.S. 51 is the Park Slope Civic Council’s fall Civic Sweep event. Anyone interested should show up prepared to work.

Dan Wiley, representing Congresswoman Nydia M. Velázquez invited everyone to the next community- planning workshop for the Brooklyn Waterfront Greenway, led by NYC Department of Transportation. The workshop will be for Red Hook October 14th. DOT is also moving forward with the five-month study, funded through a Federal Transportation Administration grant secured by the Congresswoman, to determine the feasibility of a streetcar network linking Red Hook with Downtown Brooklyn and surrounding neighborhoods. The study will include community meetings and a technical analysis of the area's needs and demographics, as well as a look at the engineering advantages and limitations of any prospective project.

Economic Development Co-Chair Rick Luftglass reported to the Board that the committee discussed the Clean Ports Act of 2010.

Motion: by Board Member Gary Reilly, seconded by Board Member Suzanne Turet; Recommendation to support the Clean Ports Act of 2010 (H.R. 5967) which would amend the Federal Motor Carrier Act to allow ports to enact and enforce clean truck programs and implement environmental programs above the current federal requirements.

Board Member Lou Sones stated that most truckers would not be able to upgrade their equipment to comply with new regulations and it might force them out of business since it is so competitive.

Board member Gary Reilly spoke in favor of the motion stating that it will allow us to do something about the environmental issues on the ports. 5

Board Member Roger Rigolli spoke out against the Federal government playing a role in someone’s business. They should pit their efforts in building a tunnel and get the trucks off the roads. They should also introduce methanol gas and provide subsidies to any station that puts these tanks in. They should concentrate on those things instead of hurting the truckers with extra costs because they are the backbone of our country.

VOTE: 32 YEAS, 2 NAYS, 1 ABSTENTION

MOTION PASSED

TIME: 8:05 P.M.
