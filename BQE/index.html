BQE traffic on Carroll Gardens and Gowanus streets

NYC DOT street counts, 311 complaints and agency reports. Every figure links to its source.
The questionHow much BQE traffic, particularly trucks, makes its way onto the streets of Carroll Gardens and Gowanus?
Short answer
Mostly cars, relatively few trucks. None of the sources reviewed here publishes a count of vehicles leaving the BQE for specific CB6 streets, so the answer comes from NYC DOT street counts.

  • Hicks Street northbound is the cut-through. Streetsblog reported that Queens-bound drivers "get off the highway before the Carroll Gardens trench and cut through Columbia or Hicks streets, before getting back on the BQE at Atlantic Avenue."
  • Gowanus streets carry hundreds of trucks a day, not thousands. 3rd Avenue, Baltic to Butler, counted 389 trucks northbound and 147 southbound on January 19, 2021; Union Street, Hoyt to Bond, counted 134 eastbound that day.

Carroll Gardens: Hicks Street carries the cars

Northbound Hicks Street carried 676 to 1,074 vehicles an hour from 6 AM to 8 PM, highest from 6 to 7 AM, in DOT's April 2025 count. Southbound on the same block carried 1,386 for the whole day.

  • It has grown (calculated). DOT's automated count on the same block found 10,312 northbound and 2,662 southbound a day in October 2012, averaged over two full count days per direction. Northbound 2025 is 47% above that. The 2012 and 2025 figures come from different DOT count programs.
  • Hicks trucks next to BQE trucks (calculated). The 502 northbound Hicks trucks equal 8.9% of the 5,628 Queens-bound BQE trucks; the 72 heavy trucks equal 3.0% of the BQE's 2,406. The counts are two years apart (2025 and 2023).
  • Columbia Street has the highest truck share of the local streets on this page. DOT's Julie Bero told a civic committee, "Those trucks will always come out onto Columbia as long as that operation is there," referring to trucks from the nearby freight ports (Streetsblog, March 20, 2023).
  • Speeds. Streetsblog reported "Department of Transportation officials saying that the extra traffic on side streets slowed travel speeds by as much as 30-50 percent in neighborhoods near the Robert Moses-era roadway following the conversion" to two lanes (Streetsblog, March 20, 2023).
  • Truck route status. Hicks Street is a DOT local truck route, and Columbia Street has both through and local truck route segments (DOT truck route data, CB6 split, bkcb6.app truck routes map).

Off-route trucks and weight enforcement

There were 102 "Truck Route Violation" 311 complaints in Community Board 6 from April 20, 2020 to September 9, 2026, and 55 of them came in 2025 and 2026 (calculated).

  • By year: 8 (2020), 9 (2021), 6 (2022), 12 (2023), 12 (2024), 29 (2025), 26 (2026 to date).
  • By street: Henry Street 22, Columbia Street 13, Van Brunt Street 10. Henry Street is not a designated truck route in DOT's truck route data.
  • Where weight enforcement is. "The sensors are only deployed on the city-owned section of the BQE between Atlantic Avenue and Sands Street in Brooklyn" (amNY, November 9, 2023). "Beginning November 13, 2023, overweight trucks will be subject to a $650 fine per weight limit violation" (NYC DOT press release).
  • Results Queens-bound. "In the first seven months of enforcement, the agency recorded a monthly average of 2,769 overweight trucks, down from 7,777 in the seven previous months," a 64% drop (Gothamist, July 30, 2024).
  • Staten Island-bound. "Enforcement under the new system began June 22 after a 90-day period that resulted in more than 3,000 notices sent to trucks exceeding state weight limits" (Transport Topics, June 24, 2025).
  • Rerouting is unmeasured. A spokeswoman told FreightWaves, "We are working with the NYPD to enforce this on surrounding streets to prevent trucks from hopping off the BQE and back on again" (FreightWaves, August 1, 2024). ENR wrote that Tanvi Pandya, executive director of DOT's bridge division, "says that from that data alone, it's hard to know if drivers are simply rerouting, as opposed to reducing their weight" (ENR, August 1, 2024).

What the data cannot show, and what to ask DOT for

The counts show a large one-way flow on northbound Hicks and heavy truck flows on the truck routes. They do not identify which vehicles came off the BQE; none of the sources reviewed here publishes an origin-destination study for these exits.

  • Ramp counts by vehicle class at Hamilton Avenue, Columbia Street, Hicks Street and the Atlantic Avenue on-ramp. The Expert Panel reported for the Atlantic ramp that "approximately 700 to 800 vehicles used the ramp during the AM peak hour and about 500 during the PM peak hour," with no truck split (BQE Expert Panel Report, p. 46 of the PDF).
  • An exit-to-reentry study matching vehicles leaving at Hamilton or Columbia with vehicles reentering at Atlantic, the route Streetsblog described.
  • Both analyses in the BQE Central EIS. DOT says "The EIS will study topics such as construction, traffic, noise, air quality, parks and open space, historic resources, neighborhood character, and other potential effects" (NYC DOT, BQE Central Project).
  • Newer classification counts. In DOT's classification dataset, the latest counts are from 2019 on Columbia Street and 2015 on Hamilton Avenue, and no Henry Street count was found.
