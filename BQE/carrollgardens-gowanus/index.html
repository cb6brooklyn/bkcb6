Layers
The questionHow many trucks make their way from the BQE onto the streets of Carroll Gardens and Gowanus?
Short answer
No public count follows trucks from the BQE onto these streets, so nobody can say exactly how many make that trip. What the counts do show: the BQE beside Carroll Gardens carries about 11,700 trucks a day (calculated from 5,628 + 6,101). The streets DOT has counted carry far fewer: 46 to 351 trucks a day in Carroll Gardens and 134 to 594 in Gowanus. The biggest spillover off the BQE nearby is cars on Hicks Street, not trucks.

The BQE next to Carroll Gardens and Gowanus

"Built between 1937 and 1964, the BQE is Brooklyn's only interstate highway and plays a major role in moving people and goods locally and regionally" (NYC DOT, BQE Central Project). DOT says "130,000 vehicles travel on the BQE daily, including 13,000 trucks" (NYC DOT).

  • Which part of the BQE this is. Carroll Gardens and Gowanus sit along BQE South, which NYC DOT describes as running "from Atlantic Avenue to the Verrazzano Bridge" and which the state owns (BQE Corridor Vision). Along Carroll Gardens the highway runs below street level in what Streetsblog calls "the Carroll Gardens trench" (Streetsblog, March 20, 2023); to the south it continues as the highway DOT's truck route data names the Gowanus Expressway, along the southwest edge of Gowanus, as the map shows (NYC DOT's truck route data).
  • Trucks on this stretch. DOT's count on the BQE between Kane Street and Sackett Street, at the north edge of Carroll Gardens, found 5,628 trucks a day heading to Queens (2,406 of them heavy) and 6,101 heading to Staten Island (2,210 heavy), on April 4 to 5, 2023.
  • Where the trucks come from. "Of all morning rush-hour truck trips going north on the triple-cantilever, approximately 50 percent originate from the Southwest Brooklyn Industrial Business Zone alone" (BQE Expert Panel Report, January 2020, p. 9 of the PDF). The triple cantilever is in BQE Central, "between Atlantic Avenue to Sands Street" (BQE Corridor Vision), north of Carroll Gardens, so trucks coming up the BQE from the south pass these neighborhoods on the way.
  • Two lanes on the cantilever. DOT says it has limited stress on the structure by "reducing traffic to two lanes on the triple cantilever" (NYC DOT, BQE Central Project); Streetsblog wrote in June 2024 that "former Mayor Bill de Blasio reduced the BQE from three lanes in each direction to two three years ago" (Streetsblog, June 20, 2024). Streetsblog reported "Department of Transportation officials saying that the extra traffic on side streets slowed travel speeds by as much as 30-50 percent in neighborhoods near the Robert Moses-era roadway following the conversion" (Streetsblog, March 20, 2023).
  • Weight sensors are north of here. "The sensors are only deployed on the city-owned section of the BQE between Atlantic Avenue and Sands Street in Brooklyn" (amNY, November 9, 2023). A spokeswoman told FreightWaves, "We are working with the NYPD to enforce this on surrounding streets to prevent trucks from hopping off the BQE and back on again" (FreightWaves, August 1, 2024).
  • What the city says would happen without repairs. DOT: "Further delay would result in removing trucks or all traffic from the highway and diverting them to local streets, creating unsafe conditions and severely impacting quality-of-life in surrounding neighborhoods" (NYC DOT, BQE Central Project). The Mayor's Office says every year of inaction "threatens highway closures that would force dangerous through traffic," including "13,000 daily truck trips," onto local streets (NYC Mayor's Office, August 2026). In a 2024 federal application DOT wrote that closure would mean "displacing 13,000 daily trucks onto local streets" (Streetsblog, March 21, 2024).

How trucks get from the highway to these streets

Trucks leave the highway at exit ramps and are supposed to stay on designated truck routes until they are close to where they are going.

  • The rules. NYC DOT says "The Through Truck Route Network is primarily composed of major urban arterials and highways and must be used by trucks that have neither an origin or destination within the borough," and "The Local Truck Route Network is designated for trucks with an origin and destination within a borough" (NYC DOT, Truck Routing).
  • Leaving a truck route to make a delivery. "An operator is allowed to travel on a street that is not a designated truck route for the purpose of arriving at his or her destination. When accessing such a location, the operator must leave a designated truck route at the intersection that is nearest to his/her destination, proceed by the most direct route, and then return to the nearest designated truck route using the most direct route" (NYC DOT, Truck Routing).
  • Ways off the highway within about 150 meters of Carroll Gardens ("ET" and "EXIT" mean exit): BROOKLYN QUEENS EXPWY ET 26 WB; GOWANUS EXPRESSWAY EXIT 25 EB; GOWANUS EXPRESSWAY EXIT 26 EB (NYC DOT's truck route data).
  • Ways onto the highway within about 150 meters of Carroll Gardens ("EN" and "ENTRANCE" mean entrance): BQE EB ENTRANCE HAMILTON AVE NB; BQE EB ENTRANCE HAMILTON AVE SB; GOWANUS EP WB EN HUGH CAREY TNNL; GOWANUS EXPWY WB EN HAMILTON AVE; HUGH L CAREY TUNNEL NB EN BQE (NYC DOT's truck route data).
  • Truck routes inside or along Carroll Gardens: through routes: Brooklyn Queens Expressway, Gowanus Expressway, Hugh L Carey Tunnel Approach plus 9 highway ramps; local routes: 9th Street, Clinton Street, Hamilton Avenue, Smith Street, West 9th Street (NYC DOT's truck route data, CB6 segment file).
  • Ways off the highway within about 150 meters of Gowanus ("ET" and "EXIT" mean exit): GOWANUS EXPRESSWAY EXIT 24 WB (NYC DOT's truck route data).
  • Ways onto the highway within about 150 meters of Gowanus ("EN" and "ENTRANCE" mean entrance): GOWANUS EXPWY WB EN PROSPECT AVE (NYC DOT's truck route data).
  • Truck routes inside or along Gowanus: through routes: Gowanus Expressway; local routes: 3rd Avenue, 4th Avenue, 9th Street, 9th Street Bridge, Hamilton Avenue, Hamilton Avenue Bridge, Smith Street, West 9th Street (NYC DOT's truck route data, CB6 segment file).
  • Hamilton Avenue, next to the Hamilton Avenue ramps. It is a DOT local truck route along the southern edge of Carroll Gardens (NYC DOT's truck route data). DOT's Red Hook study reported "Highest Truck Volume on Hamilton Ave" in the AM peak hour, with "Local Streets vary from 10 to 35 trucks" and trucks at "8% ‐ 20% of total traffic on the corridor" (Red Hook Traffic and Truck Study, Dec. 7, 2023, slide 17). DOT's automated count on Hamilton Avenue inside the Carroll Gardens outline found 25,865 vehicles a day northbound between Centre and Court streets (July and August 2023); that count does not separate trucks.

Next door: the cut-through on Hicks Street

The clearest sign of traffic leaving the BQE near Carroll Gardens is on Hicks Street, just north of Degraw Street. That block falls inside the Cobble Hill outline on the bkcb6.app maps.

  • What Streetsblog reported. Queens-bound drivers "get off the highway before the Carroll Gardens trench and cut through Columbia or Hicks streets, before getting back on the BQE at Atlantic Avenue" (Streetsblog, March 20, 2023).
  • What DOT counted. Hicks Street northbound, Degraw to Kane, carried 15,142 vehicles a day in April 2025, against 1,386 southbound in March 2025. Of the northbound vehicles, 502 were trucks and 72 were heavy trucks.
  • Mostly cars (calculated). Those 502 trucks are 3.3% of the Hicks Street northbound traffic and equal 8.9% of the 5,628 Queens-bound BQE trucks; the counts are two years apart (2025 and 2023).

311 truck route complaints

People can report trucks off their routes to 311. Of the 102 "Truck Route Violation" complaints in Community Board 6 from April 2020 to September 2026, 15 were made inside the Carroll Gardens outline and 11 inside the Gowanus outline (calculated).

  • Carroll Gardens by street: Henry Street 4, Carroll Street 2, 4th Street 2, Court Street 2, Union Street 1, Smith Street 1, Clinton Street 1, Degraw Street 1, 4th Place 1. Henry Street is not a designated truck route in NYC DOT's truck route data, so under DOT's rules a truck may use it only to reach a destination, by the most direct route (NYC DOT, Truck Routing).
  • Gowanus by street: 3rd Avenue 3, 12th Street 2, Nelson Street 1, 14th Street 1, Garnet Street 1, 4th Avenue 1, Carroll Street 1, Hoyt Street 1.

What nobody has counted yet

  • Trucks on the exit ramps. None of the sources reviewed here publishes truck counts on the ramps near Carroll Gardens or Gowanus.
  • Where the trucks go after they exit. Only an origin-destination study, following vehicles from a ramp to a street, would answer the question directly. DOT says its BQE Central environmental impact statement "will study topics such as construction, traffic, noise, air quality, parks and open space, historic resources, neighborhood character, and other potential effects" (NYC DOT, BQE Central Project), and "The agency expects to complete an environmental impact statement in summer 2028" (NYC Mayor's Office).
