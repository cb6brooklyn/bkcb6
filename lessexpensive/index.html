Housing and its price are often discussed, directly and indirectly. In every corner of New York City, even more so. Those discussions can be indirect, like with the countless articles pondering “why are so many young adults living with their parents?” I haven’t read all of them, but the bottom line is it’s due to housing costs.

This page aims to simplify and clarify what housing costs are based on what’s available at a given time. I used all listings from 2010 to today based on StreetEasy.com’s listings. I broke it down from micro (cb6/ neighborhoods) to macro (borough and citywide).

Rest assured, it’s not just staring at numbers that I’d say are too high; it’s also got every active “affordable housing” lottery open across NYC. I put “affordable” in quotes, as that may be better described as “less expensive housing”, but I digress. I’ll keep making updates, but check it out and let me know what you think.

How eligibility is decided
Only two things decide eligibility: headcount and total counted income. Who earns it does not matter. Two adults earning $30,000 each and one adult earning $60,000 with a baby are the same household to HPD: 2 people, $60,000. Both qualify from the same AMI band. But that same $60,000 with only one person in the household qualifies from a higher band, because the limits rise with household size.

Everyone who will live in the unit counts toward headcount, including infants, children, and dependents with no income. Income counted is generally the gross (pre-tax) income of household members 18 and over.

Unit size follows occupancy standards. Every one of the 16 open lotteries uses the same ranges: studio 1–2 people, 1-bedroom 1–3, 2-bedroom 2–5, 3-bedroom 3–7, 4-bedroom 4–9. These are the household-size ranges printed in each lottery's own Available Units table on Housing Connect. They set who may apply for a size, not who must: a household of three can apply for a 1-bedroom, 2-bedroom or 3-bedroom. HPD sets these in its Marketing Handbook and revised them upward by one person per unit size, so older published ranges may differ.

AMI is set each year by the federal government (HUD) for the whole New York metro area, so it can be higher than the actual median in many neighborhoods. Figures here are the NYC HPD 2025 chart. A household usually must fall between a unit's minimum and maximum income, so earning too little can also make you ineligible for a given unit.

How to apply
Applying through NYC Housing Connect
Housing Connect is the city's official portal for affordable housing lotteries, run by HPD and HDC. It is free to apply.
  1. Create a free profile at Housing Connect with your household size and income.
  2. Search open lotteries and filter to units that match your household and income band.
  3. Apply to any you qualify for. Applying to multiple lotteries is allowed and encouraged.
  4. If your application is selected, you will be asked to verify income and household documents.
  5. Never pay a fee to apply. The lottery and application are always free.
