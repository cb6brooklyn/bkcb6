Nothing here is calculated. Every tile opens the citywide address search, which reads zoning and land use from PLUTO and districts from Geoclient. All six addresses were run through that search and the card’s own answer recorded.
Four are individual landmarks. The Empire State Building, the Chrysler Building, Radio City and Grand Central each carry that designation on their own. The Williamsburgh Savings Bank Tower and the Brooklyn Academy of Music are protected instead as part of the Brooklyn Academy of Music Historic District, the same district covering both.
Landmark status is not zoning. Grand Central is C5-3, ordinary Midtown commercial. The Chrysler Building straddles C5-3 and C5-2.5, the Empire State C5-3 and C6-4.5. What protects these buildings is the Landmarks Preservation Commission, not the zoning map.
Grand Central and the Empire State Building appear here and on the 27 buildings page. Same lots, two different collections.

Six landmarks 6

Buildings people can name without being told the address. Each opens its own card: zoning, land use, community board and landmark status.

Empire State Building
Built at record speed during the Depression, and for forty years the tallest building in the world.
Chrysler Building
An Art Deco tower that briefly held the world height record, and still the tallest brick building with a steel frame.
Williamsburgh Savings Bank Tower
Brooklyn’s tallest building for eighty years, and its most visible landmark from almost anywhere in the borough.
Radio City Music Hall
The largest indoor theatre in the world when it opened in 1932, and the anchor of Rockefeller Center’s public face.
Grand Central Terminal
A Beaux-Arts civic monument whose threatened demolition produced the Supreme Court case that secured American landmark law.
Brooklyn Academy of Music
The oldest continuously operating performing arts centre in the country, and the anchor of its own historic district.
